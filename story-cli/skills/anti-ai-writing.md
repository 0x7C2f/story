# Anti-AI Writing

Adapted from zenstory-ai's story-deslop anti-ai-writing method (MIT),
compressed to the operating principles. For the enforcement side, run
`story slop`; for the gate workflow, see the deslop-gates card.

## Principles

1. **Show through consequence.** Emotion earns its place by changing a
   choice, a line, an object, or an outcome. A label ("he felt angry") is
   the fallback, not the method.
2. **Concrete over abstract.** The archivist's hands, the hum of the server
   bank, the checksum on the screen — sensory fact before interpretation.
3. **Delete-first.** Padding polished is still padding. Cut, then see what
   the passage needed.
4. **One idea, one breath.** A sentence carries one center of gravity; a
   paragraph carries one dramatic unit. Sentences that only stack
   qualifications are thinking, not writing.
5. **Dialogue is action.** People speak to get something: information,
   dominance, comfort, a delay. If a line could be cut without changing the
   scene's pressure, it is a lecture.
6. **Voice is choice.** Close third on Elias stays close: what he notices is
   procedural, paranoid, technical. Kaelen clips. Jarek grunts in the
   vocabulary of survival. Vane speaks only in administrative certainty.
7. **End on momentum.** Close on action, sharp dialogue, or an open
   complication — never on the moral of the scene.
8. **Rhythm has shape.** Comma-length narration carries the scene; short
   sentences are accents at the turns. Telegraphic runs are the other
   failure: prose chopped into confetti reads as machine, too.

## The rhythm check (from the source's black-box method)

When a passage feels off, compute before judging: sentence lengths (avg and
spread), short-sentence ratio, dialogue share, and where the em-dashes land.
Numbers point at the failing paragraph; they never decide the fix. `story
review` prints these signals per chapter.

## The five-minute self-check before a chapter is "done"

- Every flagged line from `story slop .` fixed or exempted with a reason.
- The close is momentum, not summary.
- Each named character sounds like themselves (read their voice notes).
- No fact contradicts `continuity/` — run `story continuity .`.
- The chapter *changed* something: plot, relation, information, or emotion.
