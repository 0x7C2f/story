# Elias at New Providence Outskirts - Phase 43
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek flee through the Outskirts canyon, evading Committee thermal sweeps.
2. Jarek guides them into a hidden network of subterranean smuggler tunnels to escape the aerial patrols.
3. Deep in the tunnels, they encounter an unforeseen hazard—a collapse or an old automated security drone—that traps them in the dark, cutting off their escape route.

---

## Chapter Text

The heat from the burning transport still clawed at Elias Thorne’s back as he scrambled up the scree slope. Overhead, the shrill whine of the Committee interceptors tore through the canyon, their engines echoing like distant thunder. He did not look back. He kept his eyes on Jarek’s broad shoulders, moving with a singular, desperate focus.

"Keep low!" Jarek yelled over the noise, his voice tight. He ducked under an overhanging shelf of rusted steel, gesturing for Elias and Kaelen to follow. "Thermal sweeps will paint us in the open. We need cover."

Kaelen slid in beside them, her carbine tracking the ridgeline. "They are doubling back. The mesh is active, which means Vane is going to scorch the earth trying to contain it."

"Then we make sure she has no earth to scorch," Elias said, his chest heaving. The throbbing in his skull had settled into a dull, persistent ache, a stark reminder of the concussive blast. He checked his wrist terminal. The screen was cracked, but the network diagnostics showed the mesh was propagating across the lower sectors. The Committee’s tight control over the narrative was unraveling, node by node.

"This way," Jarek instructed, pushing away from the overhang. He moved with a surprising grace for his size, making his way through the treacherous terrain of the deep Outskirts with practiced ease. "There is an old maintenance access shaft near the dry wash. Smuggler route. It is off the grid, shielded from thermal scans."

They moved in silence, the only sound the crunch of gravel and the distant roar of the interceptors. The area was a graveyard of discarded tech and crumbling concrete, the skeletal remains of an earlier era. Elias focused on the rhythm of his breathing, forcing down the rising tide of exhaustion.

Jarek stopped before a sheer rock face, his hands tracing a faint seam in the stone. He shoved his fingers into the crack and heaved, exposing a heavy, rusted hatch set into the rock. "Inside. Move."

Kaelen dropped through first, her carbine leading the way. Elias followed, the air in the shaft instantly cooler, thick with the smell of damp earth and stale ozone. Jarek slid in last, pulling the heavy hatch shut with a final, echoing clang that sealed them in darkness.

Kaelen clicked on a narrow beam light, the pale light cutting through the gloom. They were in a narrow, cylindrical tunnel, the walls slick with condensation. Thick cables, long dead, snaked along the ceiling.

"This connects to the old transit sub-levels," Jarek explained, taking point. "It is a maze down here, but it will keep us off Vane's radar. We follow this for three klicks, then surface near Sector Five."

They moved deeper into the tunnel network, the temperature dropping with every step. Elias tried to mentally map their progress, his archivist training kicking in. But the subterranean labyrinth was chaotic, a chaotic jumble of intersecting paths and collapsed sections. The oppressive silence pressed in on them, a sharp contrast to the chaos above.

"Hold," Jarek said suddenly, raising a hand.

Kaelen stopped, her weapon raised. "What is it?"

Jarek crouched, shining his light on the tunnel floor. A thick layer of dust coated the ground, undisturbed. "Nothing. That is the problem. This route is supposed to be clear, but the air is dead. No circulation."

A low, grating rumble echoed through the darkness ahead, shaking dust from the ceiling. It sounded like grinding metal, a heavy, mechanical groan that rattled in Elias’s chest.

"That doesn't sound like a cave-in," Kaelen noted, her tone clipped.

Jarek swore under his breath. "Automated security drone. Old world tech. The Committee must have reactivated the subterranean grid."

The rumble grew louder, accompanied by the heavy thud of mechanical treads. A single, blindingly bright sensor light pierced the darkness ahead, sweeping the tunnel walls. The exit route was blocked, and behind them, the heavy hatch was sealed tight against the Committee's thermal sweeps. They were trapped.
