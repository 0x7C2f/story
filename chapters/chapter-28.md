# Chapter 28

The migraine hit Elias like a physical blow. It wasn't the dull, throbbing ache he had grown accustomed to since leaving the Nexus Archives; this was a sharp, jagged spike that drove straight through his skull and into the optic nerve. The Scrapyard Settlement's central spire whined with the strain of channeling the uncorrupted memories, its ferrocrete base trembling under the electrical load.

He slumped against the jury-rigged console, his breath coming in ragged gasps. Every time he forced a cycle of raw data outward, his neural interface punished him with a spike of feedback. The air above them continued to shimmer, a localized aurora of static and conflicting truths.

"The mobile repeaters are shutting down," Kaelen reported. She stood near the heavy mesh barricade by the main gate, her pulse pistol still raised, though there were no targets in sight. She was watching a small diagnostic feed tethered to her wrist monitor. "The conflicting signals hit their logic gates and triggered a decentralized failsafe. Vane's system requires absolute synchronization. When it encountered our broadcast, it initiated a full network sever to prevent the corruption from spreading."

Jarek stepped back from the settlement's power core. The massive generator was venting steam into the chilled night air, the heavy cables hot to the touch. "The grid can't sustain this," the Scavenger leader said, his voice a low rumble. "We're pushing the threshold. Another two minutes, and we'll slag every relay in the Outskirts."

"We don't need two minutes," Elias said, his voice strained. He pushed himself off the console, his vision blurring slightly. "The repeaters have severed their connection to the staging ground. The rewrite is stopped."

He reached out with a trembling hand and severed the primary tether.

The silence that followed was absolute, heavy and sudden. The shimmering aurora above the settlement faded, replaced by the suffocating gloom of the Outskirts. The central spire groaned as the massive electrical load dissipated, the settlement's ambient lighting flickering back to life.

Elias sank to his knees on the ferrocrete, his hands pressing against his temples. The immediate danger had passed, but the physical toll of the counter-broadcast was severe. He had forced raw, uncompressed memory through a network designed for carefully curated fabrication, and his own neural architecture had acted as the conduit.

"We stopped the rewrite," Kaelen said, moving away from the gate. She holstered her weapon, though her posture remained tense. "But you used the root key to do it. The broadcast was localized, but the signature was distinct."

"Vane knows exactly where we are," Jarek finished for her, looking down at Elias. "She'll send the heavy sweep teams. The loyalists."

Elias looked up, his face pale, sweat beading on his forehead. "She will," he agreed softly. "But she just lost her primary staging ground, and her mobile rewrite protocol completely collapsed. The command structure in Sector One is already fracturing from our first broadcast. When the loyalist forces realize that Vane's failsafe just failed... she won't just be fighting us. She'll be fighting her own enforcers."

He slowly pushed himself back to his feet, ignoring the lingering pain. He looked at the root key—the sleek, obsidian drive that held the unaltered history of New Providence—still slotted into the console.

"She has to commit her final reserves to take this settlement," Elias said, his voice gaining a trace of its old, procedural calm. "Which means the Nexus Spire is exposed."

Jarek crossed his massive arms. "You want to go back to the Spire? We just barely escaped the staging ground with our lives."

"The staging ground was a symptom," Kaelen interjected, her eyes narrowing as she caught the thread of Elias's logic. "Director Vane is the disease. As long as she controls the central repository in the Spire, she will just build another rewrite protocol."

Elias pulled the root key from the console. The metal was warm to the touch. "We don't run from the sweep teams," he said. "We use them. We draw them into the Outskirts, lock them in a protracted engagement with the Scavengers, and while they're fighting here..."

"We go back to where it started," Kaelen finished.

Jarek looked around the settlement, at the exhausted scavengers and the cooling generator. "You're asking my people to hold off the Committee's heavy armor."

"I'm asking your people to win the war," Elias said.
