## Chapter Text

Elias did not look back at the terminal. The hum of the massive data transfer still resonated in the floor plates, a physical echo of the truth they had just unleashed upon the city. He checked the diagnostic readouts on his wrist display. The local sub-network was already fracturing as the Spire's automated systems desperately tried to isolate the central broadcast tower.

"They are routing power to the primary blast doors," Elias said. His voice was steady, the procedural calm anchoring him against the adrenaline. "Vane is sealing the lower vaults to trap us inside."

Kaelen fired a short, controlled burst from her weapon, forcing the advancing reserve enforcer to seek cover behind a shattered pillar. The sharp crack of the energy discharge echoed through the vast, sterile chamber. "How long until the doors lock?" she asked, her tone flat and purely tactical.

"Ninety seconds," Elias replied. He crouched behind the secondary console, his fingers dancing across the haptic interface. "I can introduce a recursive loop into the door's hydraulic control system. It will stall the closure, but only temporarily."

Jarek stepped past Kaelen, his massive frame absorbing the brunt of the returning fire on his armored plating. He leveled his heavy slug-thrower and unleashed a deafening volley. The synthetic armor of the nearest enforcer sparked and gave way under the sheer kinetic impact. "Less talking, more hacking, Archivist," Jarek growled. The scavenger did not retreat, holding the choke point with grim determination.

Elias bypassed the standard security protocols, exploiting a vulnerability he had documented months ago during a routine audit. The system was designed to fail-secure, but he forced a diagnostic override. A screech of grinding metal echoed from the far end of the corridor as the massive blast doors stuttered, halting their descent just meters above the floor.

"The hydraulic system is locked in a feedback loop," Elias said, disconnecting his physical tether from the console. "We have a gap. It will not hold forever."

"Move," Kaelen ordered. She covered Jarek as the large man retreated toward the compromised doorway.

Elias grabbed his portable rig and sprinted after them. The reserve enforcers were advancing cautiously now, their tactical programming struggling to adapt to the sudden failure of the facility's automated defenses.

As they slid under the stalled blast doors, Elias glanced at his wrist terminal one last time. The uncompressed histories were still flooding the public grid. The city was finally remembering, and the Committee's grip on the truth was shattered.
