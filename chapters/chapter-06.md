# Chapter 6: The Manifest Analysis

## Outline

1. Regroup: Elias and Kaelen return to a safehouse in the Undercity, breathless and adrenaline-fueled.
2. Analysis: They project the partial manifest. Elias decodes the batch protocol methodology, realizing the sheer scope of the Committee's edits.
3. The Flaw: The file is incomplete. They possess the records of the changes, but lack the root encryption key required to verify the original, unaltered memories to the public.
4. Decision: They must choose between leaking the unverified partial data or risking another run to find the root key.

---

## Chapter Text

The safehouse was little more than a hollowed-out maintenance junction deep within the Undercity’s labyrinthine ductwork. It smelled of ozone and stale protein rations, but it was heavily shielded against external biometric sweeps. Elias collapsed onto a crate, his chest heaving as the adrenaline of their escape began to curdle into exhaustion.

Kaelen paced the narrow space, her expression dark. "We cut it too close. The drones were seconds behind us. If they managed to log your neural signature during the disconnect..."

"They didn't," Elias interrupted, rubbing his temples where the interface ache throbbed fiercely. "I scrubbed the handshake protocol manually as I pulled the tether. We're clear for now."

"Show me what we died for," Kaelen demanded, stopping his pacing.

Elias unspooled his tether and slotted it into the safehouse’s standalone projector array. The air in the center of the room shimmered as the partial batch manifest materialized—a dizzying cascade of interconnected nodes, timestamps, and redaction orders.

As Elias began to parse the raw data, the sheer scale of the operation brought a cold dread to his stomach. It wasn’t just a few high-profile incidents being smoothed over. It was systemic.

"Look at this," Elias pointed to a cluster of nodes glowing an angry red. "The Sector Two uprising. They didn't just alter the memories of the leaders. They ran a semantic sweep across the entire regional network. They changed the sensory data of the tear gas to look like smoke from a localized fire. They dampened the emotional resonance of the crowd's anger. It's a complete narrative replacement."

Kaelen leaned closer, tracing a line of data in the projection. "And here's the authorization path. It all funnels up to one digital signature."

"Director Vane," Elias murmured. "She didn't just sign off on it. She authored the specific parameters of the batch protocol. We have her."

"Not quite," Kaelen said, her voice tightening. She swiped her hand through the projection, expanding a section of the data tree that abruptly cut off into static. "You said you got seventy percent of the file."

"I did."

"The thirty percent we left behind contains the cryptographic root key," Kaelen explained, frustration bleeding into her tone. "We have their list of changes. Not their proof. No key, no math. Broadcast it now and the Committee calls it an insurgent deepfake. We're discredited by sunrise."

Silence settled heavily in the cramped junction. The glowing data continued to slowly rotate between them, a monumental truth rendered inert by a missing string of code.

"So, what do we do?" Elias asked, the weight of the incomplete manifest pressing down on him. "Do we leak what we have and hope the public connects the dots?"

"They won't," Kaelen replied flatly. "The Committee controls the narrative infrastructure. They'll spin it. We need the key."

Elias looked at the fragmented data, his resolve hardening. "Then we find out where Vane keeps the master keys. We're going back in."
