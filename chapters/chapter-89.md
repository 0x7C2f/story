# Elias at The Undercity - Phase 89
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek crawl through the blistering thermal exhaust port.
2. They drop into the dark, damp maintenance levels of the Undercity.
3. Above, Vane orders enforcers to seal the exhaust vents, and Elias realizes the hunt has shifted.

---

## Chapter Text

The heat inside the thermal exhaust port was absolute, a heavy physical weight pressing against Elias's chest with every ragged breath. The metal grate above them had shut with a terrifying finality, sealing them in the claustrophobic darkness. He gripped the physical drive tightly, the edges digging into his sweaty palms. It was the only thing anchoring him to reality as the temperature continued to climb.

"Keep moving," Kaelen hissed from the shadows ahead, her voice strained. "The thermal bloom will mask our signatures, but only if we don't cook first. Don't stop."

Jarek grunted behind him, the scrape of his heavy gear echoing down the narrow shaft. The descent was agonizingly slow, each handhold a battle against the slick, superheated metal. Elias pushed forward, fueled by pure adrenaline and the desperate knowledge that the uncorrupted history of New Providence resided safely in his pocket.

Suddenly, the shaft widened, the blistering heat giving way to the sickly, damp chill of the Undercity. Elias slipped, tumbling the last few meters and hitting the rusted grating of a maintenance catwalk with a heavy thud. Kaelen was beside him in an instant, pulling him into the shadows of a massive, silent condensation pipe.

"We're out," she breathed, checking the charge on her plasma pistol. "But they're not far."

High above, a muffled boom vibrated through the metal lattice of the city. Then another.

"Breaching charges," Jarek said, dropping onto the catwalk beside them. He looked up into the darkness. "Vane. She's sealing the exhaust vents behind us."

Elias caught his breath, leaning against the damp bulkhead. The Archives were secure, but they were trapped. Director Vane wasn't just searching for them anymore; she was systematically closing every door. The sprawling maze of the Undercity was no longer just a refuge. It had become a cage. He tightened his grip on the drive, his mind racing. They had the memory, but now they had to survive it.
