# Chapter 21

The screen of Elias’s portable neural rig cast a harsh blue glow across his face. Beside him, the battered military-grade repeater vibrated, its low hum the only sound cutting through the howl of the Outskirts wind. Kaelen and Jarek stood shoulder-to-shoulder behind him, their gazes fixed on the holographic map projecting from the rig’s primary node.

"The handshake is complete," Elias muttered, his fingers flying across the haptic keyboard. "All three nodes are active. We have a stable mesh."

The map flickered, then stabilized. It displayed the jagged topography of the Outskirts, overlaid with the clean, grid-like geometry of the city's perimeter. Three pulsing green dots marked the locations of the salvaged repeaters.

"Now," Jarek said, his voice a low rumble, "show me what we're looking at."

Elias adjusted the parameters. "I'm running a passive scan on Committee frequencies. If Director Vane sent sweep teams, they'll be coordinating over secure channels. Our nodes are close enough to intercept the encrypted bursts."

He tapped a final sequence. For a long second, the map remained unchanged. Then, a cluster of red markers bloomed along the eastern ridge, moving in a tight, disciplined formation.

"There," Kaelen said, pointing at the projection. "Four units. They're sweeping the perimeter grid, moving sector by sector. They aren't just looking for the counter-broadcast source—they're looking for us."

Elias stared at the red markers. His instinct was to run, to pull the plug and hide. But the archivist in him saw a system, a pattern waiting to be decoded. He zoomed in on the cluster. The red dots weren't just moving; they were exchanging data packets.

"Computer," Elias whispered, "isolate the tactical comms stream. Run a localized decryption based on the root key architecture."

The rig hummed louder. Lines of code cascaded down the side of the projection, a chaotic blur that slowly organized into coherent text.

*Team Alpha, sweep Sector 4. No anomalies detected. Proceeding to Sector 5.*

Jarek let out a low whistle. "You cracked their comms."

"Not completely," Elias said, his temple throbbing with the familiar ache of the interface. "It's a localized breach. But we can see their patrol routes. We know exactly where they are, and exactly where they're going."

Kaelen leaned closer, her eyes tracing the projected path of the sweep teams. "They're bottlenecking at the old transit bridge," she noted. "It's the only stable crossing point over the eastern ravine."

Jarek’s expression hardened. "They think they're hunting us," he said, drawing a heavy slug-thrower from his belt. "But we have the high ground, and we can see every move they make."

Elias looked up from the screen. The fear that had driven him since he fled the Nexus Archives was gone, replaced by a cold, procedural clarity. "We aren't going to run," he said, confirming the decision. "We're going to set an ambush. If we can capture one of their transports, we won't just survive the sweep—we'll have a way to strike back."

Jarek grinned, a flash of white teeth in the gloom. "Now you're talking like a Scavenger, archivist. Let's move."
