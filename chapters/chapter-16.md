# Chapter 16: Proving Their Worth

The morning arrived with a biting chill that seeped through the thin, damp walls of their assigned corner in the lower hab block. Elias awoke shivering, his muscles protesting with a dull ache as he forced himself off the hard metallic grating serving as a floor. Beside him, Kaelen was already awake, checking her sidearm with practiced precision. They exchanged a silent look, both aware that today would determine whether they survived the Outskirts or became another forgotten casualty in the dust.

A heavy fist pounded against the rusted door. It swung open to reveal a pair of armed guards, their faces obscured by grimy filtration masks.

"Jarek wants you at the main generator," one of them growled, gesturing sharply with the barrel of a scavenged rifle. "Move."

Elias and Kaelen followed the guards through the labyrinthine alleys of the Scrapyard Settlement. The settlement was already awake and bustling, a chaotic symphony of grinding metal, shouted orders, and the ever-present howling wind. They arrived at a massive, subterranean cavern where the settlement's main generator thrummed unevenly, coughing thick, acrid smoke from multiple exhaust vents. The air was thick with the scent of burning ozone and machine oil.

Jarek stood on a raised catwalk, overlooking the struggling machinery with a deep scowl. He spotted them and gestured them over.

"This generator is the only thing keeping the perimeter defenses active and the water recyclers running," Jarek said, his voice carrying over the mechanical din. "It's been losing output for three weeks. My best mechanics say the control matrix is fried beyond repair, and we don't have the parts to rebuild it. You claimed you have technical skills. Prove it. Fix it, or you walk."

Elias stepped to the edge of the catwalk, looking down at the massive, sparking array. He recognized the design—a repurposed industrial-grade power core, similar to the auxiliary systems used in the Undercity archives.

"The matrix isn't fried," Elias said, his voice steady despite his exhaustion. "It's out of sync. The power core is generating energy faster than the aging distribution grid can handle, causing a continuous feedback loop that's burning out the regulators."

Jarek raised an eyebrow. "And your solution?"

"I need access to the primary control terminal," Elias replied. "I can rewrite the regulatory protocols to throttle the core's output, matching it to the grid's capacity. It will lower the overall power slightly, but it will stabilize the system and stop the burnout."

Jarek nodded slowly, a glint of genuine interest replacing his usual suspicion. "Do it."

Elias descended to the control deck, his fingers flying across the cracked and smeared interface. It took him two grueling hours, working through layers of corrupted code and bypassing degraded safety protocols, before he initiated the new synchronization sequence. The generator groaned, a deep, shuddering vibration echoing through the cavern, before settling into a smooth, steady hum. The acrid smoke began to clear.

Kaelen hadn't been idle. While Elias worked, she had analyzed the perimeter defense schematics provided by Jarek's lieutenants. She outlined a complete overhaul of their sensor network, optimizing overlapping coverage zones and identifying critical blind spots that the city's enforcers could exploit.

Jarek watched them both, his expression unreadable, before he gave a curt nod.

"You did what you said you could do," Jarek said, his voice gruff but lacking its earlier edge. "The generator is stable, and the defense plan is sound. You've bought yourselves a place here."

Elias exhaled a long, shaky breath, the tension leaving his shoulders. It was a small victory, but out here in the dust, it was a vital step forward. They were no longer just fugitives running from the city; they had a base, and they had allies.
