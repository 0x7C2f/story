# Elias at The Forgotten Depths - Phase 93
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek pry open the old access hatch and descend into the Forgotten Depths.
2. They explore the ancient, flooded tunnels, realizing the area predates the Oversight Committee entirely.
3. The heavy sentries above lose their track, but the deep dark presents new environmental hazards.

---

## Chapter Text

The rusted hatch groaned under the combined weight of Kaelen's pry bar and Elias's weight. Flakes of ancient oxidized metal rained down on their boots. With a final, agonizing screech, the seal broke, and the heavy circular door tilted inward, revealing a pitch-black shaft plunging straight down.

A rush of cold, stagnant air blasted up from the aperture, smelling of deep water and undisturbed dust. It was the scent of a time before the Oversight Committee, before the memory edits and the gleaming spires of the upper tiers.

"I'll go first," Kaelen said, slipping her pry bar back into her pack. She didn't wait for agreement, swinging her legs into the void and finding the rusted rungs of an old ladder. "It's stable enough. Just don't put all your weight on the center."

Elias followed, the ache in his temples a dull throb that he forced to the back of his mind. Jarek squeezed in last, his massive frame fitting through the narrow opening. As Jarek pulled the heavy hatch shut above them, the mechanical roar of Director Vane's sentries was cut off, replaced by the suffocating silence of the deep earth.

They descended for what felt like an hour, the beam from Elias's wrist-comp their only illumination in the oppressive gloom. The shaft opened out into a large, vaulted tunnel. The floor was submerged beneath a foot of dark, freezing water. Massive, corroded pipes lined the curved walls, dripping with condensation.

"The Forgotten Depths," Jarek rumbled, his voice echoing in the space. "The scavengers tell stories of these tunnels. They say the city was built on top of an older settlement, one that flooded decades ago."

Elias panned his wrist-comp across the water. The light caught strange, jagged shapes rising from the surface—the skeletal remains of ancient machinery. "The signal interference down here is absolute," Elias whispered, checking the static on his screen. "Vane's tracker won't find us. Her sentries can't map this."

"We're off the grid," Kaelen agreed, splashing forward. "But we are also blind. And freezing. We need to find high ground before hypothermia sets in."

Elias tightened his grip on his coat. The uncorrupted history drive was heavy in his pocket. They had escaped the immediate threat, but the dark labyrinth of the Forgotten Depths offered no guarantees, only a colder, quieter kind of danger.
