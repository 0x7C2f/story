# Chapter 47

## Outline

1. The first wave of enforcer drones breaches Checkpoint Charlie. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek use the barricades to funnel the machines into a kill zone.
2. The workers fight back with repurposed industrial tools and overwhelming numbers, destroying the initial vanguard.
3. Kaelen intercepts a Committee tactical transmission, revealing that the drones were merely a probing force, and heavy armor is already moving into position to level the sector.

---

## Chapter Text

The heavy blast doors at Checkpoint Charlie buckled under the kinetic force of a breaching charge. The explosion sent a shockwave of dust and pulverized concrete rolling through the narrow corridor. Elias Thorne crouched behind a barrier of overturned cargo crates, the ringing in his ears nearly drowning out the metallic shriek of tearing steel.

Through the settling dust, the first enforcer drone emerged. It was a quadrupedal chassis, sleek and heavily armored, designed for riot suppression and urban pacification. Its twin rotary cannons hummed as they spun up to speed. Red targeting lasers swept across the transit hub, cutting through the haze.

"Hold your fire!" Kaelen barked. She knelt beside Elias, her carbine pressed tight against her shoulder. "Let them enter the funnel."

Two more drones clattered over the rubble of the blast doors. They moved with unsettling synchronization, their optical sensors sweeping the barricades. Elias gripped his sidearm, his knuckles white. The procedural calm that had anchored him in the archives was gone, replaced by raw, cold adrenaline.

Behind them, the factory workers held their breath. They were armed with whatever they could salvage: heavy rivet guns, modified plasma torches, and lengths of reinforced rebar. Their rage had hardened them into something solid, far more dangerous than simple workers.

"Now!" Jarek roared.

The resistance opened fire. A volley of improvised projectiles and concentrated plasma beams slammed into the lead drone. The armor held for a second, but the concentrated heat from a heavy foundry torch melted through its front chassis. The drone sparked, staggered, and collapsed in a shower of blue sparks.

Elias fired, his shots pinging off the armor of the second drone. It rotated its cannons, tearing a line of craters into the cargo crates inches from Elias's head.

Kaelen leaned out, her carbine barking in short, controlled bursts. She aimed for the articulated joints, the only weak points on the heavily armored machines. A spray of hydraulic fluid erupted from the drone's front leg. It pitched forward, its targeting array struggling to compensate.

"Rush them!" Jarek commanded.

A group of workers surged over the barricades. They threw heavy slag bombs—improvised explosives made from the foundry floors—directly at the crippled machines. The detonation was deafening. The resulting shockwave knocked Elias back against the crates, but when he looked up, the remaining drones were nothing but burning wreckage.

The immediate silence was heavy, punctuated only by the crackle of burning wiring and the heavy breathing of the workers. A cheer rippled through the transit hub, a fragile, desperate sound of victory.

Elias lowered his weapon. They had held the line.

But Kaelen was not celebrating. She was focused on her wrist terminal, her face pale in the glow of the screen.

"What is it?" Elias asked, moving to her side.

"I intercepted a mesh transmission from the drone network," Kaelen said. Her voice was flat, devoid of its usual cynical edge. "These were not suppression units. They were recon."

Jarek joined them, his eyes narrowed. "Recon for what?"

Kaelen looked up. "They were painting targets. Vane is not sending infantry. She is sending the heavy artillery." Kaelen paused. "They are going to level Sector Five."
