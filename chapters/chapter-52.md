## Chapter Text

The command center in the Nexus Spire was unnervingly quiet. The holographic map of New Providence that usually dominated the space with its authoritative blue hum was now a patchwork of fractured static and erratic red alerts. Sector Five was dark. The Goliaths' telemetry streams, before a steady pulse of localized compliance, had vanished.

Director Vane stood before the primary console. She did not pace. She did not clench her hands. She observed the data void where her heavy ordnance was supposed to be leveling a rebellion.

"Telemetry on the Sector Five assault frames," Vane said, her voice carrying the flat, administrative calm that had defined her career.

The systems tech at the lower station did not answer right away. The silence stretched for a full two seconds.

"Director," the tech said at last, the word trembling. "The frames are offline. We lost the handshake."

Vane did not blink. "A mechanical failure in two simultaneous, hardened units is a statistical impossibility. Give me the last transmitted packet."

"It... it was an override command, Director. It came through the encrypted mesh network. The signature matched our localized failsafe protocols, but the transmission origin was spoofed. The ordnance bays locked themselves."

Vane absorbed the information without a shift in expression. Elias Thorne had not just survived the Goliaths; he had rewritten their operating parameters in the middle of a kinetic engagement. He was turning her own architecture into a weapon against her.

She tapped a sequence into her console, bringing up the last known location of the spoofed transmission. It was deep within Sector Five, hidden beneath the heavy interference of the foundry districts.

"They think a localized stand-down is a victory," Vane murmured to the empty air above her console. "They think stopping the machines stops the intent."

She turned away from the localized map and pulled up the wider city grid. The resistance was holding Sector Five, the Outskirts were lost, and the central broadcast tower was still a contested zone. The batch protocol had failed. The mobile rewrite had failed.

"Reroute all remaining loyalist sweep teams from the perimeter of Sector Four," Vane ordered, her voice echoing faint in the vast room. "Pull the inner guard from the Archives. I want every available asset concentrated on the Sector Five boundary."

The tech hesitated. "Director, pulling the inner guard will leave the Archives completely undefended. If they double back—"

"If they double back, they will find empty vaults and encrypted drives they can no longer read," Vane said, cutting him off. "They are not trying to steal memories anymore. They are trying to build an army. We will not wait for them to finish."

She watched the blue icons on her screen begin to shift, a steady migration of force toward the foundry district.

"Elias Thorne believes he is an archivist," she said, resting one hand on the edge of the terminal. "He is about to learn what happens when the record is no longer up for debate."
