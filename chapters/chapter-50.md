# Chapter 50

## Outline

1. Elias comprehends the scale of Vane's plan to drop Sector Five into the undercity.
2. While Jarek's fighters distract the trapped Goliath, Kaelen analyzes the firing mechanisms of the deep-core penetrators.
3. Elias proposes using the mesh network they built to spoof the command telemetry and disable the charges before they fire, ending the chapter on an unresolved complication as he attempts the hack under fire.

## Chapter Text

Elias stared at the monstrous silhouette of the Goliath struggling in the flooded trench. The steam rising from the cracked chassis was thick and choking, but it could not obscure the reality of Kaelen's readouts. Seismic charges. Deep-core penetrators. Vane did not just want to pacify Sector Five; she intended to erase it, to drop the entire foundry district into the lightless expanse of the undercity.

"She is cutting her losses," Kaelen said, her voice dropping to a harsh whisper over the din of the workers' makeshift artillery. "The counter-broadcast compromised the sector. If she drops it into the lower levels, she buries the evidence and the resistance at the same time."

Elias rubbed his temples. The familiar ache of a neural dive was already forming, sharp and insistent, even though he had not yet connected to the network. "We need to stop those charges from firing."

"Thorne, those are military-grade penetrators," Kaelen retorted, dropping to one knee as a volley of kinetic fire from the second Goliath shattered a nearby gantry. "They are hardwired into the assault frames. You cannot just pull a plug."

Below them, Jarek was a blur of motion. He directed a squad of workers, carrying heavy slag-cutters and plasma torches, to flank the immobilized Goliath. Sparks cascaded as they began to bite into the heavy composite plating. The tank's secondary turrets whined, tracking the new threat.

"We do not need to pull a plug," Elias said, his mind racing through the protocols. "We just need to speak their language. Kaelen, can you isolate the firing mechanism's command frequency?"

She looked at him, her eyes narrowing in the dim light. "I can get you the frequency, but it is encrypted. You cannot crack a military-grade handshake from a wrist terminal."

"I do not need to crack it," Elias said, tapping his primary node. "I need to spoof it. The mobile repeaters we set up in the Outskirts—they are still transmitting, right? The mesh network is active."

"Barely," Kaelen said, her fingers flying over her console. "The Committee forces are jamming the primary bands, but the decentralized mesh is holding on the fringe frequencies."

"Then we use the mesh," Elias decided. "If we route a localized counter-command through the mesh network, we can simulate an abort protocol from the Nexus Spire. We trick the Goliaths into thinking Command is ordering a stand-down on the seismic charges."

It was a theoretical maneuver, the kind of off-the-books splicing he would have never attempted in the Archives. But the Archives were gone, and the floor beneath them was about to be reduced to rubble.

"You need a direct line of sight to their command receivers," Kaelen warned, pointing toward the second Goliath, which was now grinding its way through the debris of the outer wall, its main cannon swiveling toward the catwalks. "And you need to hold the connection open long enough for the handshake to complete."

"I will get close," Elias said, adjusting the tether cable on his portable neural rig.

"You are an archivist, Thorne, not an assault operative," Kaelen said, grabbing his arm.

"I am the only one who knows the Committee's abort protocols," Elias countered, pulling his arm free. "Cover me."

Before she could argue, Elias scrambled down the rusted iron ladder, descending into the suffocating heat of the foundry floor. The air tasted of ozone and vaporized metal. The second Goliath loomed ahead, a fortress of dark armor, its sensory arrays sweeping the area.

Elias darted behind a massive industrial forge, the heat radiating through his heavy coat. He flipped open his rig, his fingers trembling slightly as he keyed in the initiation sequence. The console lit up, a fragile blue square against the inferno.

"Computer, route connection through the secondary mesh nodes," he whispered, his breath catching in his throat.

The screen flickered. *Establishing connection. Warning: Hostile environment detected.*

A deafening roar shook the floor as the Goliath's main cannon fired, vaporizing a section of the upper catwalks. Concrete rained down around Elias.

"I need that frequency, Kaelen," he hissed into his comms.

"Sending it now," her voice crackled back, tight with strain. "You have a ninety-second window before their internal logic overrides external commands."

Elias caught the data packet and slammed the frequency into the spoofing program. He initiated the handshake, his heart hammering against his ribs. The progress bar crawled forward. Ten percent. Twenty.

Then, the Goliath's secondary turret snapped toward his position. The targeting laser painted a stark red line across the front of the forge.

Elias stared at the screen, the progress bar frozen at forty-five percent. He had the connection, but he did not have the time.
