# Chapter 24

## Outline

1. Opening beat: The captured crawler breaches the Sector Six staging ground perimeter under the guise of a returning patrol.
2. Escalation: Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek infiltrate the central hub, encountering heavy loyalist resistance and discovering the primary signal repeater is already spooling up.
3. Turn or decision: The trio destroy the primary signal repeater using repurposed crawler munitions, crippling Vane's mobile rewrite protocol before it can deploy.

## Chapter Text

The Sector Six staging ground was a skeletal sprawl of rusted ferrocrete and skeletal gantries, an old distribution hub repurposed by the Oversight Committee's desperation. Floodlights cut through the dust, carving stark white wedges into the gloom, illuminating the heavy transports marshaled in the yard.

Inside the captured crawler, Elias watched the perimeter approach through the narrow viewing slit. The crawler’s engine vibrated through his boots, a low, constant thrum.

"We are coming up on the outer checkpoint," Jarek said from the helm, his hands steady on the controls. He wore a scavenged loyalist uniform jacket over his armor, a thin disguise that wouldn't hold up to close scrutiny. "Get ready to broadcast the spoofed clearance."

Elias adjusted his neural rig, feeling the familiar ache behind his eyes as he interfaced with the tapped operational network. "Spoofing now," he muttered. "The handshake is authentic. We are transmitting as Patrol Unit Delta-Seven, returning from a sweep."

Kaelen checked the action on her slug-thrower, her expression hard. "If they stop us for visual confirmation, we hit them hard and fast. We can't afford to be pinned at the gate."

The crawler rumbled forward, the heavy blast doors of the staging ground parting sluggishly. A pair of loyalist guards watched them pass, their faces hidden behind matte-black visors. Elias held his breath as the crawler rolled through the gap, the doors grinding shut behind them.

"We are in," Jarek said, his voice a low rumble. He steered the crawler toward a cluster of supply crates near the central hub. "Now the hard part."

They disembarked swiftly, moving like shadows among the heavy machinery. The staging ground was a hive of activity. Technicians in sterile white coats moved between the transports, calibrating the secondary signal repeaters, guarded by heavily armed loyalists.

Elias checked his wrist terminal. "The primary command node is in that central structure," he whispered, pointing to a reinforced blockhouse bristling with antenna arrays. "If we take out the primary repeater inside, the secondary units won't have a synchronization signal."

They moved closer, using the noise of the idling transports as cover. As they approached the blockhouse, Kaelen held up a fist.

"Patrol," she murmured.

A squad of loyalists rounded the corner of the blockhouse, their weapons lowered but their posture alert.

"Too many to bypass quietly," Jarek said, his grip tightening on his weapon. "We have to go through them."

"Give me ten seconds," Elias said. He tapped a sequence into his wrist terminal, accessing the staging ground's local grid through the spoofed connection. He targeted the floodlights surrounding the blockhouse.

He hit execute. The lights shattered in a shower of sparks, plunging the area into darkness.

"Now," Kaelen hissed.

Jarek moved first, a blunt instrument of violence. He slammed into the lead loyalist, driving him into the ferrocrete wall with a sickening crunch. Kaelen followed, a blur of motion, her slug-thrower barking twice, dropping two more guards before they could raise their weapons.

Elias stayed back, his hands shaking as the adrenaline spiked. He wasn't a soldier; he was an archivist. But he had chosen this fight. He watched as Jarek and Kaelen cleared the remaining guards in seconds, leaving only the quiet hum of the blockhouse.

"Clear," Jarek grunted, checking the perimeter.

They breached the heavy doors of the blockhouse. Inside, the air was cold and sterile, smelling of ozone and ozone. At the center of the room sat the primary signal repeater, a massive column of brushed steel and glowing blue conduits, identical to the architecture in Director Vane's vault.

It was already spooling up, emitting a high-pitched whine that resonated in Elias's teeth.

"It's active," Elias said, his eyes wide. "They are running a localized diagnostic. If we don't destroy it now, the rewrite will deploy."

"How?" Kaelen asked, looking at the massive machine. "Slugs won't breach that casing."

Jarek unslung a heavy satchel from his shoulder. "We didn't come empty-handed." He pulled out a bundle of repurposed munitions they had scavenged from the captured crawler. "Set these on the primary power couplings. When it blows, it'll take the repeater and the roof with it."

"Do it," Elias said, pulling a data drive from his coat. "I need to extract the deployment logs before it goes. We need to know if Vane has other staging grounds."

Jarek moved to plant the charges while Kaelen watched the door. Elias plugged his rig into the repeater's terminal, his fingers flying across the haptic keyboard. The encryption was heavy, but he had the root key. He pushed through the firewall, downloading the logs as the whine of the repeater grew louder.

"Charges set," Jarek called out. "Two minutes."

"I have the logs," Elias said, yanking the drive free. "Let's move."

They sprinted from the blockhouse, retracing their steps through the shadows of the staging ground. They reached the captured crawler just as the alarms began to blare, a shrill, piercing klaxon that cut through the night.

"Get us out of here," Kaelen yelled, slamming the crawler's door shut.

Jarek gunned the engine, tearing through the staging ground toward the outer gate. Behind them, the blockhouse erupted in a blinding flash of white light, followed by a concussive roar that shook the crawler. The primary signal repeater was gone, and with it, Vane's mobile rewrite protocol.

Elias leaned back in his seat, his heart hammering against his ribs. They had won this battle, but he knew the war was far from over. He looked at the drive in his hand, the stolen logs a heavy weight in his palm. They had bought the Outskirts time, but Vane would not stop until the record was rewritten.

"Good work," Jarek said, his eyes fixed on the road ahead.

Elias nodded, the interface ache returning as a dull throb. "It's a start," he said quietly. "It's a start."
