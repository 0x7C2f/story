# Chapter 24

The heavy transport juddered as its treads bit into the pulverized ferrocrete of the old highway. Inside the dimly lit cab, the stench of ozone and scorched wire from the EMP blast still lingered, an acrid reminder of the ambush that had bought them the vehicle. Elias Thorne sat rigid in the co-pilot's seat, his neural rig directly jacked into the crawler's navigation interface.

"We are approaching the outer perimeter of Sector Six," Elias said, his voice clipped, adopting the procedural tone that helped mask his fraying nerves. He tapped a sequence onto his haptic keyboard. "I'm broadcasting the captured command cipher. If their rolling encryption cycles before we clear the gate, the automated turrets will vaporize us."

Jarek grunted from the pilot's seat, his large hands easily managing the heavy steering yoke. "Then make sure it doesn't cycle, archivist. This armor is thick, but it won't hold against a sustained barrage from fixed emplacements. Just get us inside."

Through the reinforced viewing slit, the Sector Six distribution hub loomed like an industrial fortress against the smog-choked sky. Once a logistical nerve center for the lower tiers, it had been fortified into a staging ground for the Oversight Committee. The perimeter was ringed with high-tension mesh and concrete barriers. Beyond it, Elias could see the clustered silhouettes of other heavy transports, identical to the one they were driving, marshaling to form the mobile rewrite network.

"Gate approaching," Kaelen said from the shadows behind them, her posture coiled. She was checking the action on her slug-thrower, a rhythmic, metallic snick-clack. "Stay calm. We are a returning sweep team. They have no reason to suspect otherwise until we give them one."

The crawler decelerated as they neared the massive steel barricades. A localized scanning beam swept over the vehicle, painting the cab in a stark, blinding red. Elias's wrist terminal vibrated as the staging ground's security mainframe pinged the crawler's transponder. He closed his eyes, visualizing the handshake protocol, forcing the root key through the gaps in the local network's defense.

*Access Granted.*

The heavy steel gates groaned open, retracting into the reinforced concrete walls. Jarek guided the crawler through the threshold and into the chaotic expanse of the staging ground.

The yard was a hive of controlled, military precision. Dozens of transports were parked in precise rows, their engines idling with a low, collective thrum that vibrated through the floor plates of their own vehicle. Loyalist troops moved between the vehicles, coordinating the deployment. In the center of the yard stood a bunker-like structure constructed of heavy ferrocrete and composite plating. Rising from its roof was a thick, metallic spire bristling with antenna arrays—the primary signal repeater.

"There it is," Elias said, pointing toward the central structure. "That's the master node. If we take that down, the mobile network collapses before they can deploy it to the Outskirts."

Jarek parked the crawler near the edge of a logistics line, blending in with a row of similar vehicles. He killed the main drive, leaving only the auxiliary power running. "We can't just walk up to it," he rumbled, eyeing the patrols circling the bunker. "It's heavily guarded."

Kaelen slung her weapon over her shoulder and moved toward the cargo bay hatch. "We don't walk up to it. We use the maintenance tunnels. This hub used to move supplies underground. The old schematics show a sub-level access point near the southern loading docks."

"I can slice the sub-level door," Elias confirmed, disconnecting his neural rig from the crawler's nav-computer and coiling the tether. "But once we are inside the bunker, I'll need to physically interface with the master node to overload its power core. It won't be quiet."

"It doesn't need to be quiet once it's done," Jarek said, grabbing a heavy breaching charge from the supply crate. "Let's move before they realize this transport is dead weight."

They slipped out of the crawler's side hatch, using the shadows cast by the massive vehicles as cover. The air in the staging ground was thick with the smell of promethium fuel and exhaust. Elias followed close behind Kaelen, his heart hammering against his ribs. This was no longer just preserving the record; this was active demolition.

They reached the southern loading docks undetected, slipping behind a stack of rusted cargo containers. The sub-level access door was heavily oxidized, but a modern mag-lock had been slapped onto its frame. Elias stepped forward, pulling a bypass shunt from his coat pocket. He jammed it into the lock's interface port, whispering a string of commands under his breath as he rapidly typed on his wrist terminal.

The lock sparked, sputtered, and disengaged with a heavy thud.

Jarek pulled the door open, revealing a dark, downward-sloping concrete corridor. "In," he ordered.

Elias stepped into the gloom, the chill of the subterranean passage a sharp contrast to the heat of the staging ground above. They were inside. Now, they just had to bring the network down before Director Vane's loyalists realized the ghosts of the Nexus Spire were already in their house.
