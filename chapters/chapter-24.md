## Chapter Text

The heavy transport juddered over the uneven, fractured ferrocrete of the approach road. Inside the cramped cabin, the air was stale, reeking of ozone and the metallic tang of old sweat. Elias gripped the edge of the secondary console, the vibration rattling his teeth. He focused on the tactical display projected from his neural rig onto the scratched screen.

"Approaching the outer perimeter," Elias murmured, his voice tight. "The automated sentries are sweeping the avenue."

Jarek sat in the pilot's seat, his large hands wrapped around the heavy steering yoke. He wore a scavenged Committee enforcer jacket that was tight across his broad shoulders, his features obscured by the transport's reinforced shadow.

"Keep the spoofed transponder steady," Jarek said, his tone a low rumble. "If they flag us, we won't even clear the first gate."

"The handshake protocol is active," Elias replied, tapping a sequence into his haptic keyboard. "The network reads us as returning Patrol Unit Seven. We should register as cleared."

Kaelen leaned between the two front seats, her gaze fixed on the viewport. The Sector Six staging ground loomed ahead. It was an old distribution hub, a massive skeletal structure of rusted girders and ferrocrete walls now bristling with Committee ordnance. Blinding arc lights cut through the perpetual smog of the Dead Zone's edge, casting long, stark shadows across the vehicle yard.

"It's a fortress," Kaelen said, her voice clinically clipped. "Three layers of mesh-fencing, elevated sentry turrets, and heavy barricades."

"And a dozen heavy transports marshaling in the yard," Elias added, referencing the heat blooms on his display. "Vane isn't wasting time. They are already syncing the mobile repeaters."

The transport rolled toward the primary checkpoint. A heavy barricade gate blocked the road, flanked by two automated sentry turrets that tracked their approach. Elias felt a cold sweat prickle along his hairline. He checked his wrist terminal out of habit, then forced his hands back to the keyboard.

"Hold your speed," Elias whispered. "Don't break the profile."

Jarek maintained the transport's steady, grinding pace. The turrets locked onto them, their sensor arrays flashing a sharp, interrogative red. Elias held his breath, watching the code cascade across his screen as the checkpoint interrogated their spoofed transponder.

For three agonizing seconds, the interface hesitated. Then, the sensors flashed green.

The heavy barricade retracted with a deep, grinding whine. Jarek drove the transport through the gate, merging into the organized chaos of the vehicle yard.

"We are in," Jarek said. He steered the transport toward a row of parked crawlers near the western wall, killing the engine as they slipped into a gap in the formation.

Elias exhaled, the tension releasing from his shoulders in a sudden rush. He uncoupled his primary tether, letting the interface fade from his vision. "We are inside the perimeter, but the central node is still active. The primary signal repeater is housed in the main distribution structure."

Kaelen checked the charge on her heavy slug-thrower. "Then we don't stay in the truck. We move on foot, breach the central structure, and plant the charges on the repeater's power core."

"The yard is swarming with personnel," Elias pointed out, watching the movement through the narrow viewing slit. "If we are spotted out of uniform, the entire base will converge on us."

"Then we don't get spotted," Jarek said, stepping out of the pilot's seat and reaching for the munitions crate they had salvaged from the ambush. He tossed a heavy satchel of EMP charges to Kaelen, and another to Elias. "We move through the shadows, use the transports for cover. We hit the node, cripple the repeater, and we leave the same way we came in."

Elias caught the satchel, its weight solid and reassuring against his chest. He looked at Kaelen, who gave him a brief, sharp nod, and then at Jarek, whose expression was grim and determined.

The archivist tightened the strap of the satchel. He was no longer just preserving memory. He was dismantling the machine that sought to erase it.

"Let's go," Elias said.
