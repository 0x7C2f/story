# Chapter 24

The Sector Six staging ground emerged from the haze of the Dead Zone as a sprawl of rust and harsh utilitarian geometry. It had once been a distribution hub, a graveyard of forgotten logistics now repurposed by the Oversight Committee. A perimeter of heavy concrete barricades and floodlights cut a sharp line against the dust, enclosing dozens of heavy transports parked in neat, orderly rows.

Elias sat in the navigator’s seat of their captured crawler, the engine idling with a low, throbbing hum. The cracked ferrocrete of the old highway stretched toward the fortified checkpoint ahead.

"They are running a rolling authentication cycle," Elias whispered, his hands moving over the haptic keyboard. He kept his eyes on the terminal, watching the data stream he had spliced from the transport’s internal network. "The checkpoint scanners will ping the crawler for a valid clearance code every thirty seconds."

"Do we have it?" Kaelen asked. She stood right behind his seat, her posture tense, her eyes fixed on the approaching barricades.

"I am simulating it," Elias said, adjusting the feed. "The encrypted handshake we pulled at the bridge will hold, provided they don't manually inspect the cargo hold."

Jarek sat in the driver’s seat, his hands resting loosely on the heavy steering yoke. "If they stop us, we don't wait. We punch through."

"We need the central node," Elias reminded him, his voice clipped. "If we hit the barricades loud, they will lock down the primary signal repeater before we can get close to it."

The crawler lurched forward as Jarek eased the throttle. They approached the checkpoint, the harsh glare of the halogen floodlights sweeping over the battered hull of the transport. Two Committee enforcers stood by the reinforced boom gate, their kinetic rifles slung across their chests, watching the crawler’s approach with bored indifference.

Elias rubbed his temple. The interface ache was a dull, constant pressure, a reminder of the raw data flowing through his neural rig. "Authentication ping incoming," he said.

The terminal flashed. The rolling cipher demanded a response.

Elias forced the ghost handshake through the local network, matching the precise cryptographic signature he had isolated back at the bridge. He held his breath.

The terminal flashed green.

Outside, the boom gate slowly lifted. The enforcers stepped back, waving the crawler through without a second glance.

"We are in," Kaelen breathed.

Jarek drove the crawler into the staging ground, navigating the narrow lanes between the parked transports. The scale of the operation was staggering. Dozens of crawlers were being prepped, their cargo bays loaded with heavy, box-like structures that Elias recognized from the technical schematics. The secondary repeaters.

"There," Elias said, pointing toward the center of the yard.

A large, heavily fortified structure dominated the staging ground. It was an old administrative bunker, its concrete walls reinforced with fresh durasteel plating. A massive, dish-like antenna sat on its roof, humming with suppressed power. Thick cables snaked from the bunker to the surrounding transports, linking them into a single, cohesive network.

"The primary signal repeater," Kaelen said. "The central node."

"They are already syncing the broadcast protocols," Elias said, checking the terminal. "The network is live. If we destroy the central node, the secondary repeaters won't be able to establish the overwrite matrix."

Jarek parked the crawler in the shadow of a massive fuel silo, a hundred yards from the bunker. He killed the engine. The silence in the cabin was immediate and heavy.

"The bunker is sealed," Kaelen observed, peering through the viewing slit. "Mag-locked doors. At least four enforcers on the perimeter, and likely more inside."

"I can bypass the mag-locks from here," Elias said, his fingers flying across the keyboard. "But the security network is partitioned. I can't disable the perimeter alarms without triggering a lockdown."

Jarek picked up his slug-thrower and racked the slide. The heavy, metallic clack echoed in the cramped cabin. "Then we don't disable the alarms. We use them."

Elias turned to look at the Scavenger leader. "A diversion?"

"You said you can bypass the mag-locks," Jarek said. "You and Kaelen get inside the bunker. Find the central node and destroy it. I will take the crawler and hit the fuel depot on the far side of the yard. It will draw the perimeter guards away."

"That is a suicide run, Jarek," Kaelen said, her voice sharp. "The entire staging ground will converge on you."

"I know the dust," Jarek said, his tone flat, unyielding. "I'll lose them in the wreckage. But you two have to be fast. Once that bunker goes on alert, you won't have much time."

Elias looked at the terminal, then at Jarek. The statistical probability of survival was plummeting, but the necessity was absolute. "I can open the door on your signal," Elias said.

Jarek nodded. "Get ready."

He opened the driver's side door and slipped out into the shadows. Elias and Kaelen followed, moving silently toward the rear of the crawler. The air outside was cold, smelling of diesel and ozone.

They waited in the dark, watching the bunker. The enforcers patrolled the perimeter with methodical precision, completely unaware of the threat lurking in the shadows.

A minute passed. Then two.

A deafening explosion shattered the quiet.

A massive plume of orange fire erupted on the far side of the staging ground, illuminating the rust and concrete in a chaotic, flickering light. The ground shook with the force of the blast. Sirens began to wail, a shrill, piercing sound that cut through the night.

The enforcers at the bunker turned toward the explosion, their weapons raised. Shouting echoed across the yard as patrols abandoned their posts, rushing toward the burning fuel depot.

"Now," Kaelen said.

Elias sent the bypass command. The heavy mag-locked doors of the bunker slid open with a deep, grinding thud.

They ran across the open ground, slipping through the doorway just as the doors began to close behind them. They were inside.
