# Chapter 24

## Chapter Text

The captured heavy transport ground forward, its treads churning the desolate soil at the edge of the Dead Zone. The engine's deep vibration rattled Elias's bones, a constant reminder of the tons of armor that separated them from the loyalist forces marshaling ahead. He sat in the cramped auxiliary seat behind the main console, his neural rig tethered to the crawler's internal network, feeding false telemetry back to the Oversight Committee's grid.

"Approaching the outer perimeter," Jarek called out from the pilot's station. His voice was steady, betraying none of the tension that pulled at his shoulders. He adjusted the drive yoke, keeping the transport perfectly aligned with the rutted track leading into Sector Six.

Through the narrow viewport, the staging ground slowly materialized from the gloom. It was an old distribution hub, a sprawling complex of reinforced ferrocrete and rusted steel gantries that had been repurposed by Director Vane's forces. Floodlights carved harsh, overlapping circles into the perpetual twilight, illuminating rows of identical heavy transports lined up like dormant beetles.

"Maintain speed," Kaelen said, standing close behind Jarek's seat. She had a heavy slug-thrower slung over her shoulder, her eyes fixed on the approaching checkpoints. "If we slow down, we give them a reason to verify our clearance."

Elias focused on the data stream scrolling across his interface. He had spoofed the transport's transponder, masking it as a retreating unit from the transit bridge ambush. The deception was fragile, reliant on the Committee's current logistical chaos following the failed synchronization and the recent engagement.

"They are pinging us," Elias warned, his fingers flying across the haptic board. He intercepted the automated query from the hub's defense perimeter, injecting the fabricated authorization codes before the system could register an anomaly. "Handshake complete. They are logging us as Beta-Seven. We are clear for the outer gate."

The transport rumbled past the first set of heavy barricades. Two loyalist guards in full riot gear watched them pass, their expressions hidden behind mirrored visors. Jarek didn't look at them, his gaze locked on the central compound ahead.

"They're not just staging," Kaelen observed, peering through the side slit. "Look at the loading bays."

Elias brought up an external feed on his monitor. Several transports were being fitted with massive, cylindrical arrays—the mobile signal repeaters he had seen in the decrypted schematics. The localized rewrite protocol was already in the final stages of deployment. If those crawlers fanned out, the Scrapyard Settlement would be wiped clean.

"We have to hit the primary command node," Elias said, pointing to the fortified structure at the heart of the hub. It was a brutalist block of concrete, bristling with communication antennae. "If we take out the central synchronization server, the individual repeaters won't be able to establish a coherent rewrite matrix. They'll just broadcast static."

"We can't just drive up and knock," Jarek said grimly. "The inner perimeter is crawling with infantry."

"We don't need to," Kaelen replied. She pointed to a service trench running parallel to the main loading avenue. "Jarek, veer right at the next junction. We can park the crawler in the shadow of the old cooling towers. From there, it's a short sprint to the maintenance entrance."

Jarek nodded, easing the transport into a slow turn that brought them off the main thoroughfare. The shadows of the cooling towers swallowed them, providing a momentary respite from the glaring floodlights.

"Shutting down the primary drive," Jarek said as the crawler lurched to a halt. The sudden silence was jarring, broken only by the hum of the auxiliary systems.

Elias uncoupled his rig from the console, rolling his shoulders to ease the stiffness. He drew the compact sidearm he had taken from the transit bridge, checking the power cell. It felt heavy and unfamiliar in his hand.

"Alright," Kaelen said, turning to face them. Her expression was hard, determined. "We go in fast and quiet. Jarek, you cover the rear. Elias, get us through the security doors and onto the server floor. We plant the charges on the central node and we leave."

"And if we run into a patrol?" Elias asked.

"Then we stop being quiet," Jarek answered simply, racking the slide of his slug-thrower.

They slipped out of the crawler's side hatch, the chill air of the Dead Zone biting at their exposed skin. They moved quickly, using the rusted hulks of old machinery as cover. The staging ground was a hive of activity, the sound of heavy boots and shouted orders drifting across the open spaces.

Elias approached the rusted steel door of the maintenance entrance, pulling a decrypter from his belt. He attached it to the electronic lock, holding his breath as the device whined, searching for the bypass frequency. After a tortuous five seconds, the light flashed green, and the heavy door hissed open.

They stepped into the dimly lit corridor, the stale smell of ozone and old dust filling their lungs. The assault on the central node had begun.
