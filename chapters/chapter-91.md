# Elias at The Undercity - Phase 91
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek discover that Vane's seeker drones are actively mapping the Undercity and forming a net.
2. They realize they must find a defensible position or a way out before the drones close the perimeter.
3. The team encounters a partially collapsed sector of the Undercity and decides to set up a hasty ambush to destroy a node drone and break the net, knowing the resulting noise will draw enforcers.

---

## Chapter Text

Elias Thorne pressed his back against the weeping condensation of the bulkhead, counting his own heartbeats. The crimson sweep of the seeker drone had vanished down the corridor, but the high-pitched whine of its repulsor lifts lingered in the damp air like a physical vibration. They were deep in the Undercity, yet the walls were closing in.

"They're moving in a grid," Kaelen whispered, her eyes tracking an unseen path on her wrist-comp. The soft glow painted her sharp features in erratic shadows. "Three drones passed through the last intersection in five minutes. Vane is weaving a net, and we're standing in the center."

Jarek shifted his weight, his heavy boots grinding against the metal grating. "We can't just run blind. If they establish a complete perimeter, they'll just tighten it until we're squeezed into an enforcer kill box."

"He's right," Elias said, his voice raspy from exhaustion. He pulled the physical drive from his pocket, the uncorrupted history of New Providence cold and heavy against his palm. "We didn't steal this just to die in a sewer. We need to break the net."

Kaelen dragged her thumb across the partial schematics they had extracted earlier. "There's a collapsed transit hub two clicks south," she said, tracing a path. "The structural integrity is zero, which means the drones will have to fly low and slow to thread the debris. It's a choke point."

"An ambush," Jarek rumbled, a dark satisfaction creeping into his tone. "We hit a node drone, punch a hole in their grid, and slip through before the system recalculates."

Elias nodded, pushing himself off the damp wall. "But it will be loud. The moment we drop a drone, Vane will know exactly where we are. She'll send the heavy sentries."

"Then we make sure we're not there when they arrive," Kaelen said, already moving down the corridor. "Let's go. We're on Vane's clock now."

They pushed through the labyrinthine corridors in near-total darkness, relying on Kaelen's fractured map and Jarek's instincts to avoid the primary routes. The air grew thicker, smelling of rust, ozone, and centuries of forgotten decay. The distant hum of the city above was swallowed whole by the oppressive silence of the deep Undercity.

When they reached the collapsed transit hub, Elias understood Kaelen's assessment. A massive section of the ceiling had caved in, creating a chaotic ruin of twisted metal beams, shattered concrete, and hanging cables. It was a perfect trap.

"Jarek," Kaelen pointed to a precarious outcropping of rebar. "Get up there. When it passes underneath, drop the heaviest thing you can lift straight onto its sensor cluster."

Jarek didn't argue. He began climbing the debris with surprising agility for a man of his size, disappearing into the shadows above.

Elias knelt beside Kaelen behind a large slab of concrete. "What's our part?"

"We're the bait," she said, her voice hard, drawing a compact kinetic pistol from her hip. "When it enters the hub, I'll clip its repulsor coil. It will drop to stabilize, and Jarek crushes it. Then we run."

They waited in silence, the only sound the slow drip of condensation. Then, the whine returned. It started as a low vibration in the floor panels and grew into a piercing hum. A seeker drone drifted into the ruined hub, its red optical beam slicing through the darkness, scanning the debris field with mechanical precision.

Elias held his breath, watching the red light sweep closer. It paused, the sensor cluster rotating rapidly, analyzing a thermal anomaly near Kaelen's position.

"Now," Elias hissed.

Kaelen fired. The sharp crack of the kinetic round echoed loud and sharp in the cavernous space. The drone shuddered violently as sparks exploded from its lower repulsor lift. It dropped sharply, struggling to stabilize its flight path.

From the shadows above, a massive chunk of reinforced concrete plummeted downward. It struck the drone directly on the main chassis with a sickening crunch of metal and glass. The machine hit the floor, its red light flickering wildly before dying with a final shower of sparks.

The silence that followed was heavy and tense.

"Grid is recalculating," Kaelen announced, checking her wrist-comp. "We have a three-minute window before the adjacent drones cover this sector."

"Enforcers will be here in two," Jarek grunted, landing with a heavy thud on the ground beside them. "Let's move."

Elias didn't hesitate. They sprinted out of the ruined transit hub, plunging deeper into the unmapped dark of the Undercity, the uncorrupted truth burning a hole in his pocket. Vane's net was broken, but the hunt had only just begun.
