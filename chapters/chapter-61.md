# Chapter 61

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek make their way deeper into the Undercity, seeking a secure staging ground to analyze the raw data stolen from the Spire.
2. They find an abandoned sublevel, but the local ambient interference prevents a clean connection. Elias realizes they need to tap into a forgotten, deep-infrastructure line to proceed without detection.

---

## Chapter Text

The dense fog of the Undercity clung to Elias’s skin, a heavy mix of industrial exhaust and decaying synthetics. Every step down the rusted gantry felt like wading through deep water. Above them, the distant, muffled thumps of mechanized patrols continued to echo from the primary arteries. Director Vane’s dragnet was tightening, but they had managed to slip beneath its first layer.

Kaelen took the lead, her movements sharp and practiced in the gloom. Her pulse pistol remained drawn, the faint blue hum of its capacitor casting long, restless shadows against the corrugated walls. Behind them, Jarek moved with a quiet, lumbering grace, his heavy boots finding the solid points on the treacherous walkways.

"We need a blind spot," Kaelen whispered, not turning around. "The local network is saturated with passive surveillance. If we stop in the open, the loyalist sweeps will flag our heat signatures within minutes."

"I know a place," Jarek rumbled, his voice barely carrying over the ambient roar of the sector. "Old structural sub-level. Sub-level forty-two. They condemned it twenty years ago when the primary coolant lines ruptured. It is toxic, but the interference from the residual radiation scrambles most standard optics."

Elias checked the readout on his wrist terminal. The device struggled to maintain a signal, the screen glitching with bursts of static. "Radiation? My equipment isn't shielded for sustained exposure."

"It is marginal," Jarek replied. "Enough to hide us. Not enough to kill us. Not today, anyway."

"Lead the way," Kaelen ordered.

They descended further, leaving the relative safety of the maintenance gantries for a maze of forgotten tunnels. The air grew stagnant, the temperature dropping sharply as they entered the older, unpowered sections of the Undercity. Emergency chemical lights, long since expired, hung like dead insects from the ceiling.

Jarek navigated the darkness with an eerie familiarity, bypassing collapsed corridors and flooded chambers. After an arduous hour, he stopped before a heavy blast door, its surface scarred by deep impact craters and coated in a thick layer of synthetic mold. He gripped the manual override wheel and wrenched it sideways. With a grinding scream of protesting gears, the door yielded, sliding back to reveal a cavernous, pitch-black space.

Kaelen swept the room with her pistol's light. It was a vast staging area, filled with the rusting hulks of decommissioned transport vehicles and mountains of discarded tech-scrap. The air tasted of ozone and metallic dust.

"It will do," Kaelen said, holstering her weapon. "Secure the perimeter, Jarek. Elias, get to work. We need to know exactly what we stole."

Elias moved to a relatively flat surface—the hood of an overturned hauler—and unpacked his gear. He connected the physical jack from the stolen data drive to his terminal. He initiated the boot sequence, bypassing his own safety protocols to force the raw feed. The screen flickered violently, struggling to parse the sheer volume of encrypted data.

"I am hitting a wall," Elias muttered, his fingers flying across the holographic interface. "The encryption is dynamic. It is shifting keys every microsecond. I need a stable connection to the Undercity’s dark mesh to run a localized decryption algorithm, but the ambient interference here is too high."

Kaelen frowned, stepping closer. "Can you boost the signal?"

"Not without broadcasting our location," Elias said, his frustration mounting. "If I push the gain, Vane’s surveillance net will triangulate us instantly."

He stared at the garbled mess on his screen. The data was there, buried beneath layers of cryptographic armor, but he could not reach it. He rubbed his temples, the familiar ache of a connection attempt beginning to throb behind his eyes. He thought of the auxiliary exhaust shaft, the massive, archaic infrastructure that formed the spine of the Nexus Spire.

"Wait," Elias said, a spark of realization cutting through the exhaustion. "The primary network is saturated, but the Undercity runs on a redundant, hardwired system. The old infrastructure lines."

He looked up at Jarek, who was returning from his patrol of the perimeter. "Jarek, when they condemned this level, did they sever the physical data trunks, or just kill the power?"

Jarek paused, considering the question. "They cut the power. The physical lines are likely still intact, just buried."

"If I can tap directly into a deep-infrastructure trunk, I can bypass the local wireless interference," Elias explained, his voice quickening. "It is a closed loop. Vane’s sweepers are monitoring the airwaves, not the dead cables beneath our feet."

"Where is the nearest trunk?" Kaelen asked, her eyes narrowing.

"Usually running parallel to the primary drainage culverts," Elias said, pulling up a crude, wireframe map of the sector on his terminal. "There is a junction box approximately two hundred meters below us. But it will not be a clean tap. The lines are archaic, probably corroded."

"It is our only option," Kaelen said. "Pack up. We are going deeper."

Elias quickly disconnected his terminal, the encrypted drive still secured in his grip. The momentary sense of safety the staging area provided evaporated. They were moving back into the unknown, but the promise of unraveling the data—of understanding exactly what Director Vane was hiding—drove him forward. The descent was not over.
