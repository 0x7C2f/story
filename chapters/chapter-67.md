# Elias at Sector Four - Phase 67
## Outline

1. Elias realizes the bridge connection attempt by Vane has left a digital anchor on his deck, essentially acting as a tracker.
2. He frantically tries to wipe the hardware while they keep moving, but Kaelen notes the sweep teams are adjusting their flanking maneuvers perfectly in sync with their movements.

## Chapter Text

Elias stumbled through the shadowed arches of the maintenance concourse, his portable interface deck still clutched tightly against his ribs. The burn of the feedback loop from the security gate override throbbed steadily behind his eyes. The resistance vanguard pushed ahead, the heavy footfalls of Jarek and Kaelen echoing against the ferrocrete, but Elias could not share their momentum. Something was wrong.

His deck was vibrating. It was a subtle, rhythmic hum, out of sync with the standard cooling cycle. He dragged his thumb across the cracked display, pulling up the raw diagnostic logs. The screen flickered, casting a pale, sickly glow over his sweating face.

There it was. Buried beneath the localized telemetry and the spoofed sensor data he had used to mask their advance. A micro-routine, elegant and terrifyingly quiet. *Director Vane.* She hadn't just tried to fry his neural link when he bridged the connection; she had dropped a digital anchor.

"Kaelen," Elias gasped, his voice cracking as he forced his legs to keep moving. "The deck. She marked it."

Kaelen halted at the intersection ahead, her submachine gun sweeping the gloom. She didn't look back, her focus locked on the branching corridors. "Define 'marked,' Archivist."

"A tracker," Elias said, his fingers flying across the interface, initiating a localized purge sequence. "A dormant signal. It's pinging the mesh network."

Jarek materialized from the shadows of a rusted stanchion, his heavy slug-thrower resting against his shoulder. "Can you scrub it?" he rumbled, his eyes scanning the darkness above them.

"I'm trying," Elias muttered, watching the purge sequence stall at thirty percent. The anchor was embedded, wrapped around the core kernel of the deck's operating system. "It's hardcoded into the power management loop. If I wipe it, I brick the deck."

"We need that deck," Kaelen stated. She turned her head slightly, listening. "And we are out of time. The sweep teams. They aren't searching anymore."

Elias looked up from the screen. "What do you mean?"

"They're flanking," Kaelen said, her voice dropping into the clipped, efficient register of a cornered fighter. "Two squads, parallel corridors. They are pacing us."

The reality of the situation crashed over Elias, colder than the damp air of the undercity. Vane wasn't guessing anymore. She was watching a bright, undeniable dot moving across her tactical map.

Miles above them, in the sterile silence of the Nexus Spire, Director Vane watched the telemetry feed with cold satisfaction. The resistance was localized. She touched a single command prompt, ignoring the chatter of the standard pursuit units.

"Redirect Heavy Enforcer Squad Seven," she ordered, her voice an administrative absolute. "Cut the main transit line. Crush them."

Down in the dark, the rhythmic thud of heavy armor began to echo, ahead of them.
