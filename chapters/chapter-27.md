# Chapter 27

The captured transport slammed through the Scrapyard Settlement's reinforced perimeter gates, trailing a plume of dead-zone dust and burning ozone. Jarek brought the heavy vehicle to a shuddering halt in the center of the trading plaza. The transport's engine hissed as it dumped its remaining coolant, a white cloud billowing against the rusted facades of the scavenger shelters.

Elias uncoupled his neural interface before the transport fully stopped, throwing open the passenger door. "We need the primary repeater array," he shouted over the din of alarmed scavengers emerging from their dwellings. "Jarek, tell them to spool up the main generator. Now!"

Jarek vaulted from the driver's seat, his heavy slug-thrower slung over his shoulder. He bellowed orders into the crowd, his voice carrying the unquestioned authority of survival. Scavengers scattered, sprinting toward the settlement's power core.

Kaelen dropped from the transport's cargo bay, her boots crunching on the ferrocrete. She immediately began unloading the salvaged mesh network equipment they had assembled before the staging ground assault. "The transport's sensors are redlining," she reported, dragging a heavy transmission coil toward the settlement's central spire. "The mobile rewrite units are ten miles out and closing. We have less than twenty minutes before their broadcast fields overlap the perimeter."

Elias grabbed the synchronization interface—a jury-rigged console constructed from salvaged Committee tech and scavenged fiber optics. The blinding migraine that had plagued him since the staging ground now settled into a cold, persistent thrum. It was the physical cost of forcing uncorrupted memory into a system designed for fabrication.

He hauled the interface to the base of the central spire, his hands moving with frantic precision. He spliced the fiber optic feeds into the settlement's communication array, bypassing the standard regulatory protocols. The system protested, flashing streams of error codes across his retinal display.

"Override the safety margins," Elias muttered, his fingers blurring across the haptic keyboard. "This is not a clean upload. It's a brute-force overwrite."

Jarek returned, a heavy power cable trailing behind him. "The generator is at maximum output. If you push the array past its threshold, it will slag the entire grid."

"If we don't push it, the grid won't matter," Elias replied, taking the cable from Jarek and slamming it into the interface's primary port. "The mobile repeaters are designed to carpet-bomb the area with a localized synchronization. We have to broadcast the unaltered memories—the raw files we stole from the Spire—with enough amplitude to disrupt their signal."

"You're using the truth as a jamming signal," Kaelen observed, securing the transmission coil and racking the slide on her weapon.

"I'm using it as a counter-virus," Elias corrected. "The Oversight Committee's system requires absolute synchronization. If we flood the local network with uncorrupted data, the mobile transports will read the conflicting signals as a fatal error. They'll initiate their internal failsafes and shut down to prevent the corruption from spreading."

Elias pulled the root key from his pocket—a sleek, obsidian drive containing the unaltered history of New Providence. He hesitated for a fraction of a second, feeling the weight of the memories inside. He drove the key into the interface.

"Kaelen," Elias said, his voice dropping the procedural tone. "Once the broadcast starts, the Committee will pinpoint our exact location. They won't just try to rewrite us. They will try to erase us."

"Let them try," Kaelen said, moving to cover the main gate. Jarek joined her, the two of them standing as the only line of defense between the archivist and the encroaching silence.

Elias engaged the primary tether, feeling the massive surge of data slam into his neural rig. The uncorrupted memories flooded his consciousness—the sounds of the Sector Two uprising, the unedited faces of the lost, the true history that Director Vane had tried to bury. He pushed the data outward, channeling the raw truth through the settlement's central spire and into the heavy atmosphere.

The counter-broadcast began. The air above the Scrapyard Settlement shimmered, the visual manifestation of competing truths colliding in the digital spectrum. Elias braced himself against the terminal, holding the line.
