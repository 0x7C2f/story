# Chapter 2: The Descent

## Outline

1. Elias evades the Nexus Archives' security perimeters and descends into the labyrinthine depths of the Undercity.
2. He seeks refuge in an abandoned maintenance sector, out of reach from the corporate monitoring grid.
3. Hooking up the physical drive to a jury-rigged terminal, he begins to untangle the encrypted raw data of memory M-774-B.

---

## Chapter Text

The transport tube shrieked as it hurtled downward, the sheer velocity pressing Elias Thorne against the scratched plexiglass. Above him, the gleaming spires of New Providence receded, replaced by the suffocating, humid gloom of the Undercity. He checked his wrist terminal. The Nexus Archives sentinels were already pinging his last known coordinates, their search algorithms tearing through the upper district’s network. He had minutes before they isolated his descent trajectory.

When the doors wrenched open, Elias plunged into the narrow, neon-lit alleys of Sector Four. The air here tasted of ozone and synthesized rot, a stark contrast to the sterile, climate-controlled halls of the Archives. He kept his head down, weaving through crowds of scavengers and rogue data-brokers. The heavy coat he wore felt like a target on his back, but the physical drive tucked in his inner pocket felt like a ticking explosive.

He didn't stop until he reached the rusted bulkhead of an abandoned hydro-maintenance hub. It was a dead zone—a pocket of structural decay where the corporate monitoring grid could not penetrate. Elias sealed the heavy door behind him, the metallic clang echoing in the damp cavern. His breath came in ragged gasps, his heart hammering against his ribs. He was completely off the grid.

Elias set up his makeshift workstation on a crate slick with condensation. He retrieved a jury-rigged terminal he had hidden here months ago, built as a fail-safe for his more sensitive, off-the-books archivist work. He slotted the untraceable physical drive into the console. The screen sputtered to life, casting a pale green glow over his face.

"Alright," he whispered to himself, his voice trembling. "Let's see what you really are."

He bypassed the initial visual overlay of the coastal seawall. The static returned, violent and erratic, threatening to overload the terminal’s rudimentary processor. Elias fed the raw code through a secondary decryption matrix, peeling back the layers of the fabricated memory.

Soon, the static coalesced. The holding facility snapped into view, far clearer than before. It wasn't just a corporate installation; it was a black-site modification lab. Rows of containment chairs filled a sterile white room, each occupied by motionless figures wired to central cognitive servers. And standing at the forefront, observing the procedure, was a face Elias recognized—a high-ranking director from the Oversight Committee.

The implication was terrifying. They weren't just storing memories. They were rewriting them at the source.
