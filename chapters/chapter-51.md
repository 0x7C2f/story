# Elias at Sector Five - Phase 51
## Outline

1. Elias manages to complete the spoofing handshake just as the Goliath fires, dodging the blast while sending the abort signal.
2. The Goliaths' command protocols accept the spoofed signal from the mesh network and initiate a localized stand-down, locking their heavy ordnance.
3. With the immediate threat neutralized, Jarek's fighters secure the disabled Goliaths, giving the resistance a major victory and solidifying their hold on Sector Five.

---

## Chapter Text

The targeting laser burned a brilliant, merciless red across the rusted iron of the forge, inches from Elias's face. The secondary turret of the Goliath whined, the kinetic autoloader slamming a heavy round into the chamber. The sound cut through the ambient roar of the foundry, a mechanical death sentence ticking down to zero.

On the blue screen of his wrist terminal, the progress bar ticked to fifty percent. It was not fast enough.

"Elias, move!" Kaelen's voice crackled over the comms, panicked and sharp.

"I cannot break the connection!" Elias shouted back, shielding the fragile screen with his body. The handshake protocol required an uninterrupted line of sight to the tank's short-range receiver. If he moved out of cover, he would lose the link. If he stayed, he would be vaporized.

The turret mechanism locked into place with a definitive clack.

Elias threw himself backward, dragging the portable neural rig with him just as the kinetic round fired. The impact deafened him, a concussive shockwave that shattered the industrial forge into jagged shrapnel and white-hot slag. Searing heat washed over him as he tumbled across the ferrocrete floor, his ears ringing with a high-pitched whine.

He scrambled to his knees, blinking away the dust and afterimages. The rig had survived the fall. The tether cable was intact. He jammed his thumb onto the terminal screen.

Eighty percent. Ninety percent.

The Goliath pivoted its massive chassis, seeking the target it had failed to destroy. The primary cannon lowered, aligning perfectly with Elias's exposed position. The thermal coils in the barrel began to glow, a deep, menacing orange.

"Complete the handshake," Elias muttered, his voice trembling. "Come on, complete the handshake."

*Connection Established.*

The prompt flashed in green across his vision. Elias did not hesitate. He executed the localized abort protocol, routing the encrypted command through the decentralized mesh network Kaelen had tapped into. The packet transmitted, a digital ghost wearing the authoritative signature of the Nexus Spire.

The Goliath's primary cannon flared brightly, then immediately dimmed. The massive assault frame shuddered, its engine pitch dropping from an aggressive roar to a sullen idle. A series of heavy, metallic thunks echoed from within its armored shell—the sound of the internal logic locking the ordnance bays.

*Command Override Accepted. Weapons Lock Engaged.*

Elias collapsed onto the soot-stained floor, his chest heaving as the adrenaline crashed.

From the upper gantries, the resistance fighters fell silent, watching as the second Goliath mirrored the first. The massive war machines sat motionless in the center of the ruined foundry, their threat neutralized by a fabricated command.

"I do not believe it," Kaelen breathed over the comms. "Their telemetry just zeroed out. The deep-core penetrators are safed."

Jarek emerged from the smoke, flanked by a dozen workers armed with heavy slag-cutters. He stared at the dormant Goliaths, then looked up at the catwalks where Elias was slowly pulling himself to his feet. A rare, fierce grin split the resistance leader's soot-streaked face.

"Secure those frames," Jarek ordered, his voice booming across the foundry. "Rip the comm arrays out. I want them blind."

As the workers swarmed the disabled tanks, Elias unhooked the neural rig from his wrist. His hands were shaking uncontrollably. They had stopped Vane from dropping the sector, but the victory felt fragile.

Kaelen descended the ladder, picking her way through the debris. She stopped beside Elias, eyeing the smoldering remains of the forge that had nearly been his grave.

"That was reckless," she said, her tone clipped, though her eyes betrayed a profound relief. "Even for you."

"It worked," Elias said, his voice raspy.

"It worked this time," Kaelen corrected him, pulling up a new data stream on her terminal. "But Vane will know the signal was spoofed. Once she realizes her Goliaths are offline, she will isolate the mesh nodes we used to transmit."

Elias looked at the massive, silent machines. They were a testament to the power of the archives, a weapon forged from digitized memories and protocols.

"Let her try," Elias said softly. "Sector Five is ours now."
