# The Broadcast Tower Plan

The hum of the Undercity felt different now—less like a sanctuary and more like a countdown. Elias Thorne sat on a repurposed crate in the safehouse, the small obsidian drive containing the cryptographic root key resting on the metal table before him. The neural ache behind his eyes had faded to a dull throb, a lingering reminder of their escape from the Nexus Spire.

Kaelen paced the narrow length of the room, her boots clicking rhythmically against the grating. "The Oversight Committee isn't going to wait," she said, her voice tight. "Vane knows the key is gone. They'll accelerate Synchronization."

"They need the city-wide grid to execute a mass overwrite," Elias said, his gaze fixed on the drive. "If we tap into the central broadcast tower before they initiate the sequence, we can use the root key to push the unaltered memories back to the citizens."

Kaelen stopped pacing and leaned against the wall, crossing her arms. "The tower's in the heart of Sector One. Heavily fortified. Vane will have tripled security by now."

"We don't have a choice," Elias replied. He picked up the drive, feeling its smooth, cold surface. "A secondary relay won't have the bandwidth to override the synchronization protocol. It has to be the primary node. We use their own architecture against them."

"It's a suicide run, Elias." Kaelen's expression was grim. "But it's the only play we have left."

Elias nodded slowly. "We need a diversion. Something loud enough to draw the Committee's attention away from the tower long enough for me to interface with the broadcast terminal."

"I can rig some localized EMP charges in Sector Three," Kaelen suggested. "Knocks out their surveillance. Pulls their response teams to the wrong quadrant."

"It'll be tight," Elias said, running a hand through his hair. "Once I'm in the system, I'll have to manually bypass the physical air-gaps on the transmission controls while uploading the true files. If they catch on before I finish..."

"They won't," Kaelen said, her tone resolute. "We didn't come this far to let them rewrite history."

Elias looked at the drive again. The weight of the city's truth rested in his palm. The past was slipping away, but for the first time since he found the fragment, he felt a surge of clarity. They were going to the surface, and they were going to broadcast the truth.
