# Chapter 18

The air in the Dead Zone tasted of metal and dry earth. It wasn't the sharp, processed tang of the city's synthetic atmosphere, nor the localized grit of the Scrapyard Settlement. This was something older, a pervasive radiation that seemed to hum against the skin. Elias checked the dosimeter strapped to his wrist. The needle hovered near the yellow zone, a steady reminder of why the Scavengers usually avoided this stretch of the Outskirts.

"Keep your breathing shallow," Kaelen advised, his voice crackling over the short-range comms. He was a few paces ahead, scanning the ridge line with a scavenged optical rig. "The dust carries the worst of it. The longer we're out here, the deeper it settles."

"Noted," Elias muttered, adjusting the seal on his filtration mask.

They had been walking for two hours since leaving the settlement, a slow, deliberate trek eastward through a landscape that looked as though it had been scrubbed clean by fire. The ground was baked hard, split by deep fissures and dotted with the skeletal remains of pre-collapse infrastructure. There was no life here, not even the hardy desert scrub that clung to the edges of the Scrapyard.

Jarek walked beside Elias, his pace steady and practiced. He carried a heavy-duty plasma cutter slung over his shoulder, a piece of gear that had cost the settlement a month's worth of trade. "It's not far now," Jarek said, his voice muffled by his own rebreather. "Just over the next rise. The old relay station. Built before the Committee walled the city off. It was supposed to be part of a regional communications grid that never got finished."

Elias nodded, his mind working through the technical parameters of what they were looking for. "If the repeaters were shielded to pre-collapse military specs, they might have survived the ambient radiation. We need at least three functional units to create a stable, decentralized mesh network back to the city."

"If they're not melted to slag," Kaelen interjected grimly.

They crested the ridge, and the relay station came into view. It was a brutalist block of poured concrete and reinforced steel, half-buried in the side of a sloping hill. The massive dish on its roof was crumpled, folded inward like a crushed flower, but the main structure appeared intact. A heavy blast door sealed the entrance, its surface scored by decades of wind and irradiated sand.

"Looks untouched," Jarek observed, lowering his plasma cutter. "No tracks. No signs of recent salvage."

Kaelen moved up to the blast door, running a gloved hand over the locking mechanism. "Mechanical seal. Rusted solid. We're not picking this."

"That's why I brought the cutter," Jarek said, stepping forward. "Elias, check the external power couplings. If there's any residual charge in the station's capacitors, cutting through the main frame might trigger a feedback loop."

Elias moved to the side of the entrance, locating a panel that was mostly fused shut. He pried it open with a prybar, exposing a tangle of thick cables. He attached his diagnostic meter. "Dead," he confirmed after a moment. "No juice. Cut away."

Jarek engaged the plasma cutter. A blinding blue-white flame erupted from the nozzle, biting into the thick steel of the blast door. The smell of ozone and burning metal filled the air, cutting through the dry taste of the Dead Zone. It took ten minutes of grueling, precise work to carve a rough square through the door.

Kaelen kicked the weakened section inward. It fell with a heavy, echoing clang, kicking up a cloud of stale dust from the dark interior.

They waited a moment for the dust to settle before stepping through the breach. The inside of the station was cavernous, illuminated only by the thin shafts of sunlight penetrating the cracks in the ceiling. The air was cool and dead. Rows of massive server racks lined the walls, coated in decades of undisturbed dust.

Elias walked down the central aisle, his eyes scanning the equipment. "This is older than I expected," he murmured. "Analog interfaces mixed with early digital architecture."

"Can you use it?" Jarek asked, his tone urgent. "The radiation in here is climbing. The shielding on this place is compromised."

Elias stopped in front of a reinforced cabinet near the back of the room. It was marked with faded yellow warning stripes. He wiped away the grime to reveal a heavy latch. "Help me open this," he said.

Kaelen and Jarek lent their strength, forcing the latch up. The cabinet door groaned open, revealing a stack of sleek, matte-black components. They were pristine, untouched by the decay that gripped the rest of the station.

Elias carefully pulled one out, turning it over in his hands. It was surprisingly heavy, packed with high-density circuitry. "Signal repeaters," he breathed. "Military grade. Fully shielded." He quickly checked the diagnostic ports. "The internal seals are intact. They're viable."

"How many?" Jarek asked.

"Six," Elias replied, a rare smile breaking across his face. "More than enough. With these, we can bounce a signal from the settlement straight through the city's perimeter jamming, directly into the Undercity."

"Then let's pack them up," Kaelen said, slinging his pack off his shoulder. "Before my dosimeter starts singing."

They worked quickly, carefully loading the heavy repeaters into their reinforced packs. The silence of the station was broken only by the scrape of metal and the ragged sound of their own breathing.

As they stepped back out into the harsh light of the Dead Zone, the weight of the packs was a tangible reminder of their success. They had the hardware. Now they just needed to build the network. The Oversight Committee had spent years trying to erase history, but with these repeaters, Elias and Kaelen were about to give the city its memory back.
