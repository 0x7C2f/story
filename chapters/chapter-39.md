# Chapter 39

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek flee the destroyed Sector Six staging ground in the captured heavy transport.
2. They race against Director Vane's advancing loyalist sweep teams toward the safety of the Outskirts.
3. Elias suffers from a blinding migraine due to the recent system overloads. They manage to evade the immediate sweep teams and plan their next move to solidify the resistance.

---

## Chapter Text

The heavy transport shuddered as Jarek pushed the aging engines past their established redline. Elias Thorne clung to the rusted grab-handle above the co-pilot's seat, his teeth set against the bone-deep vibration. Behind them, the subterranean access tunnel was a receding throat of darkness, momentarily lit by the fading, violent orange glow of the staging ground's collapse.

Elias squeezed his eyes shut. The blinding migraine that had started at the Nexus Spire was now a physical presence in his skull, pulsing in time with the transport's uneven roar. The neural load from overriding the central node, followed by the concussive force of the signal repeaters detonating, had left him hollowed out.

"We are tracking three pursuit signatures," Kaelen reported from the rear compartment, her voice tight over the internal comms. She was braced against the armored bulkhead, her eyes locked on the stolen tactical display. "Committee interceptors. They are dropping into the tunnel network from the access hatches above Sector Six."

"Let them drop," Jarek growled. His hands were clamped on the heavy yoke, his knuckles white under the grime. "This rig is built for hauling ore, not outrunning corporate flyers. But we have armor, and we know the deep tracks."

"They don't need to catch us," Elias said, his voice louder than a whisper, yet carrying the sharp edge of procedural certainty. "They just need to paint our transponder for a targeted strike. Vane won't let us reach the surface."

"Then we blind them," Kaelen said. "Elias, can you spoof the drive signature?"

Elias forced his eyes open, blinking away the static that swam in his peripheral vision. He pulled himself toward the auxiliary console, his fingers trembling as he engaged the crude haptic interface. The console was a mess of salvaged parts and exposed wiring, a far cry from the pristine, climate-controlled archives he had once commanded.

"The tactical network is still chaotic from the counter-broadcast," Elias muttered, his mind slipping into the familiar, cold logic of data manipulation. "I can introduce a recursive feedback loop into their tracking telemetry. It will read as a massive hardware failure on their end."

"Do it," Jarek said, throwing the transport into a violently banked turn that sent a shower of sparks cascading along the left hull as it scraped the tunnel wall.

Elias initiated the sequence, his hands moving with practiced, if uncoordinated, speed. The code was crude, a blunt instrument compared to the elegant cryptographic seals he used to build. He watched the progress bar on the display, a thin sliver of green against the glaring red warnings of the pursuit ships.

"Handshake confirmed," Elias said, leaning back as the sequence completed. "I'm feeding them ghost data."

"Signatures are breaking off," Kaelen confirmed a moment later, relief bleeding into her tone. "They are holding position at intersection four."

The transport burst from the tunnel mouth, tearing through a rusted, chain-link perimeter fence and out into the desolate expanse of the Outskirts. The sky above was a bruised, unnatural violet, thick with the perpetual haze of the industrial zones.

Jarek finally eased the throttle back, allowing the engine's roar to subside into an exhausted, metallic whine. He guided the heavy vehicle into the shadow of a decaying overpass, killing the primary running lights.

"We are clear for now," Jarek said, his broad shoulders slumping slightly.

Elias rested his head against the cold plasteel of the console, the adrenaline receding to leave only the throbbing ache. They had survived Sector Six, but the resistance was fractured, their primary infrastructure reduced to ash.

"Vane knows exactly what we are capable of now," Kaelen said, stepping forward from the rear compartment. "This isn't a sweep anymore. It's a war."

Elias looked out at the jagged ruins of the Outskirts. They had traded the illusion of safety for the harsh reality of the dust. "Then we better start planning," he whispered.
