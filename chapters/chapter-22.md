# Chapter 22

The eastern ravine was a jagged scar cut into the bedrock of the Outskirts, and the old transit bridge that spanned it was little more than a skeletal arch of rusted ferrocrete. The freezing night air whistled through the structural supports, carrying the acrid scent of ozone and dust. Elias crouched behind a collapsed pylon, the cold metal biting through his jacket. His neural rig hummed against his temple, feeding him a localized tactical overlay derived from the intercepted Committee frequencies.

"They are three minutes out," Elias whispered into his comm bead. The words felt strange in his mouth. A week ago, he was a memory archivist cataloging municipal data. Now, he was calculating interception vectors.

"Three minutes," Kaelen’s voice crackled back, clipped and steady. She was positioned fifty meters away on the opposite side of the bridgehead, a heavy plasma rifle resting on the lip of a concrete barricade. "Jarek, hold the charge until the transport hits the middle span. Don't blow it early."

"I know how to set a trap, city-blood," Jarek grunted from the shadows below the bridge structure. "Just make sure you hit the driver."

Elias checked the rig's projection. The four red markers—Team Alpha—were advancing exactly as predicted. Three infantry units flanking a light armored transport. Director Vane had sent a show of force, confident that a sweep of the perimeter would flush out unorganized scavengers. She hadn't expected a coordinated defense using her own encrypted channels.

The low hum of a repulsor engine vibrated through the ground. A pair of intense, cold white headlights swept across the rusted struts of the bridge. The loyalist transport rolled onto the ferrocrete, its armored plates gleaming under the sparse moonlight. The three flankers moved in tight formation, their rifles raised, scanning the shadows.

"Steady," Kaelen murmured over the channel.

Elias watched the tactical overlay. The red dots moved past the first pylon. Then the second. They reached the center span.

"Now," Jarek said.

A blinding flash erupted beneath the bridge. The concussive wave hit Elias a fraction of a second later, rattling his teeth and showering the area with pulverized concrete. The shaped charge didn't destroy the bridge; it localized the blast against the transport’s rear repulsor lift. The vehicle lurched violently, its back end dropping to the deck with a screech of shearing metal.

Before the loyalist troops could recover from the shockwave, Kaelen fired. Three brilliant streaks of plasma cut through the darkness. The first shot punched through the driver's side window of the disabled transport. The second and third dropped the two lead flankers before they could even raise their weapons.

The surviving loyalist soldier scrambled for cover behind the crippled transport, firing wildly into the gloom. Sparks rained down as his shots ricocheted off the rusted arches.

"Suppression fire," Elias yelled, rising from behind his pylon. He raised his borrowed sidearm, his hands shaking, and fired three rapid shots at the transport's hull. It was inaccurate, but the noise forced the soldier to keep his head down.

Jarek surged up from the underside maintenance ladder. He moved with terrifying speed for a man his size. He didn't fire his slug-thrower. He vaulted the low guardrail, landed behind the remaining loyalist, and struck him across the back of the helmet with the heavy stock of his weapon. The soldier collapsed without a sound.

Silence returned to the ravine, save for the hiss of venting coolant from the disabled transport.

Elias lowered his gun, his chest heaving. The tactical overlay on his rig showed four gray, stationary markers.

Kaelen emerged from her vantage point, her rifle sweeping the perimeter as she approached the vehicle. She checked the downed soldiers, kicking their weapons out of reach, before inspecting the transport.

"Driver's neutralized," she reported. She moved to the rear doors and keyed the manual release. "Repulsor array is damaged, but the main drive is intact. With a few hours and some scrap, we can patch the lift."

Jarek knelt beside one of the unconscious soldiers, stripping him of his ammunition belts and comms unit. "Vane's going to wonder why Team Alpha missed their check-in," he rumbled.

"Let her wonder," Elias said, stepping into the open. The cold air felt sharper now, cleaner. "We have their tactical frequencies. We have a transport. The sweep is broken."

Kaelen looked at Elias, a rare, grim smile touching the corner of her mouth. "We aren't just hiding anymore."
