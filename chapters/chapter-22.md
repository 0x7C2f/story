# Chapter 22

The undercarriage of the old transit bridge smelled of rust and damp earth. Elias crouched on a narrow maintenance gantry, his portable neural rig balanced precariously on a crossbeam. Below him, the eastern ravine plunged into darkness; above him, the cracked ferrocrete of the main span groaned under the constant pressure of the Outskirts wind.

"They are two minutes out," Elias whispered, the words loud in the enclosed space. He did not look away from the holographic projection. The red markers of the sweep team were moving in a disciplined line, funneling toward the bridge exactly as predicted.

Beside him, Jarek checked the priming mechanism on a bulky, cylindrical device. It was an improvised electromagnetic pulse charge, assembled from the military-grade salvage they had pulled from the Dead Zone. "Let them get halfway across," the Scavenger leader rumbled. "The debris field will slow them down. When the transport hits the apex, I blow the charge."

Kaelen knelt on the opposite side of the gantry, her slug-thrower resting against the rusted railing. "If the EMP doesn't take out their escort drones, we are going to have a very short firefight."

"The pulse will fry their localized network," Elias said, his voice clipped, procedural. "The drones rely on a continuous handshake with the transport's command node. No node, no drones."

He rubbed his temple. The interface ache was building, a dull throb behind his eyes. He focused on the screen. The red markers reached the edge of the bridge. The heavy transport was a massive, armored crawler, flanked by four enforcers on foot and a pair of hovering surveillance drones.

The crawler's massive treads ground against the twisted rebar of the roadway, a sound that vibrated down through the stanchions and into Elias's boots. It moved slowly, navigating the chokepoint with cautious precision.

"Wait for it," Jarek muttered.

Elias watched the telemetry. The crawler reached the center of the span. "Now," he said.

Jarek slammed his fist down on the detonator.

A silent shockwave ripped through the air, carrying the sharp scent of ozone. Above them, the crawler's heavy engine died with a mechanical groan. The high-pitched whine of the escort drones sputtered and cut out, followed immediately by the harsh crash of metal hitting ferrocrete as they dropped from the sky.

"Move," Jarek ordered.

They scrambled up the rusted access ladder, emerging onto the roadway. The scene was chaotic. The transport sat dead in the center of the bridge, its external floodlights extinguished. The enforcers were shouting, their helmet comms rendered useless by the pulse.

Kaelen and Jarek did not hesitate. They moved with a brutal, practiced efficiency. Jarek leveled his slug-thrower, laying down a barrage of suppressive fire that forced the enforcers into cover behind the transport's reinforced treads. Kaelen flanked them, using the debris as a shield, and neutralized the remaining two with calculated precision.

Elias ignored the firefight. He sprinted toward the rear of the crawler, pulling the tether cable from his rig. The transport's security lockout was engaged, heavy mag-locks securing the armored doors.

"Computer," Elias exhaled, jamming the tether into the exterior access port. "Initiate a brute-force handshake. Bypass the physical lockout."

*Access Denied. Local network offline.*

"I know it's offline," Elias muttered, his fingers dancing across the haptic keyboard. "Reroute power from the auxiliary cell. Overload the mag-lock relays."

The rig hummed. A line of code spiraled across the projection, a chaotic blur that Elias forced into alignment. Sparks showered from the access port. With a heavy clunk, the mag-locks disengaged.

Elias hauled the door open. The interior of the crawler was dark, smelling of ozone and polished metal. The cargo bay was stacked with supply crates, field rations, and heavy ordnance. But Elias's eyes immediately locked onto the primary communications terminal bolted to the bulkhead.

Jarek stepped into the bay behind him, his breathing heavy. He looked at the crates, then at Elias. "You did it, archivist."

Elias plugged his rig into the terminal. The screen flickered to life, displaying a cascade of Committee tactical data. They hadn't just captured a transport and its supplies. They had secured a direct, physical tap into Director Vane's operational network.

"We didn't just survive the sweep," Elias said, the cool glow of the screen reflecting in his eyes. "We just took the offensive."
