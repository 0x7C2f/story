# Chapter 7: The Archive Heist Plan

## Outline

1. Researching the Target: Elias and Kaelen sift through the partial manifest's metadata to trace the origin of the root key, narrowing it down to Director Vane's personal, air-gapped archive in the Nexus Spire.
2. Formulating the Approach: Kaelen reveals she has old underworld contacts who can provide a localized EMP to bypass the Spire's biometric sensors, while Elias realizes he will need to interface directly with Vane's node.
3. Preparation and Tension: They gather their modified gear. Elias confronts the psychological toll of diving into an unfamiliar, heavily fortified mental architecture, fully committing to the risky infiltration.

---

## Chapter Text

The holographic projection of the partial manifest cast jagged, neon shadows across the walls of the maintenance junction. Elias had spent the last three hours isolating the cryptographic handshake that terminated at the missing root key. The code was elegant, layered with quantum encryption that bore the distinct architectural style of the Nexus Spire's core engineers.

"It doesn't make sense to keep the master key on the same network as the batch protocol," Elias muttered, his fingers tracing the digital threads in the air. "If the system was compromised, the key would be vulnerable. Vane is too paranoid for that."

Kaelen leaned against the rusted bulkhead, arms crossed. "So she keeps it offline. An air-gapped vault."

"Exactly," Elias said, expanding a secondary window showing the Spire's public schematics. "And if I were the Director of the Oversight Committee, the only place I'd trust an air-gapped vault is right under my feet. Her personal archive."

Kaelen let out a low whistle. "The Nexus Spire. The most heavily surveilled citadel in New Providence. The biometric sweeps alone would flag us before the plaza."

"We can't go in through the network, and we can't walk through the front door," Elias agreed, feeling the familiar, cold knot of anxiety tightening in his chest. "But you know people down here. Smugglers. Tech scavengers."

A slow, dangerous smile crept onto Kaelen's face. "I might know a crew in the lower sectors. EMP charges. Old tech, messy, but it works. One burst blinds the biometric sensors on the executive line. Long enough to reach her private lift."

"And once we're inside?"

"That's where you come in, archivist," Kaelen said, tapping the obsidian node still connected to Elias's rig. "I get us to the door. You pick the lock. Vane's node isn't a standard interface. It's laced with neural feedback loops. Slip up and it doesn't just deny you. It fries your cortex."

Elias swallowed hard. He knew the risks of an aggressive neural dive. Archiving memories was a delicate, cooperative process. Slicing into a fortified, hostile architecture was something else entirely. It required a violent synchronization of mind and machine that could leave permanent psychological scars.

"I can do it," Elias said, the words feeling heavy on his tongue. "I have the base structural parameters from the M-774-B file. If I can mimic the Committee's own access protocols, I might be able to trick the vault into opening long enough to pull the key."

"Might," Kaelen repeated, raising an eyebrow. "That's a thin wire to walk, Thorne."

"It's the only wire we have," Elias countered, beginning to pack up the portable projector and his rig. "We have the partial manifest. We know they rewrote the Sector Two uprising. If we don't finish this, nobody will. We leave in four hours. Get the EMP."
