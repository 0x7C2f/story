# Vane at The Nexus Spire - Phase 37
## Outline

1. Director Vane stands in her command center, watching the city's grid fracture as true memories broadcast. She suppresses a moment of panic and forces herself into cold analysis.
2. She reviews the telemetry from the failed mobile rewrite deployment at Sector Six, identifying the localized pulse that triggered the early failsafe. The signature matches Thorne's methods.

## Chapter Text

The holographic map of New Providence in Director Vane’s command center flickered in a chaotic rhythm, the ambient blue light replaced by jagged bursts of crimson. Each red anomaly represented a node in the grid broadcasting unauthorized data—unaltered memories flooding the public consciousness.

Vane stood motionless before the console, her hands resting on the cool metal edge. The silence in the room was absolute, contrasting the silent roar of the city tearing its own history apart. She did not clench her jaw. She did not permit her hands to tremble. Panic was a luxury for those who did not understand the architecture of control.

"They think this is victory," Vane said, her voice an unhurried, administrative hum in the empty room. "They think a momentary lapse in the maintenance schedule implies the collapse of the facility."

She tapped a sequence into the console. The chaotic city-wide map minimized, replaced by raw telemetry data streaming from the Outskirts. The synchronization broadcast had failed, severed at the tower, but the secondary mobile network—her failsafe—had also collapsed.

Vane’s eyes tracked the code, searching for the point of structural failure. The mobile transports had deployed off schedule, acting on alternative programming. They had been forced into a localized failsafe by a massive energy surge at the Sector Six staging ground.

She isolated the signature of the surge. It was crude, utilizing a chained array of military-grade signal repeaters pushing an impossible load through an aging mesh network. It was reckless. It was the kind of desperate architecture Elias Thorne would design.

He was not running anymore. He was marshaling resources. The staging ground at Sector Six was not just a point of failure; it was a stronghold.

Vane closed the telemetry window. The time for sweeping, elegant protocols had passed. A mass overwrite was impossible as long as the resistance held the physical infrastructure to contest it. The problem required a focused, decisive solution.

She activated the secure channel to the loyalist sweep teams holding the perimeter in the Outskirts.

"This is Director Vane," she said, her tone devoid of emotion. "Cease the perimeter lockdown operations. Reroute all available tactical assets to the transit sublevels beneath Sector Six."

The comms crackled as a squad leader responded. "Director, we are containing a panic in Sector One. Pulling teams will expose—"

"You are not containing anything," Vane interrupted, her voice maintaining its steady cadence. "You are managing symptoms. The disease is in Sector Six. Do not attempt to take them alive. Do not attempt to secure the mesh network. I want the staging ground leveled. Acknowledge."

A brief pause hung on the line before the response came. "Acknowledged, Director. Rerouting teams now."

Vane cut the connection. She turned back to the flashing crimson map of the city. They had disrupted the record, but they had not erased her. The city would burn before she let Elias Thorne write its future.
