# Chapter 103

## Outline

1. Opening beat: The team in the Undercity monitors a sudden drop in network traffic as Vane initiates the system wipe.
2. Escalation: Kaelen decodes the incoming signal, realizing it is a localized memory purge designed to induce mass cognitive failure.
3. Turn or decision: Elias determines the only way to stop the protocol is to physically sever the Spire's connection to the lower sectors before the wipe completes.

## Chapter Text

The chaotic chatter of the Undercity's network feeds died, replaced by an eerie, uniform silence. Elias Thorne pushed himself up from the cot, ignoring the sharp pull in his ribs. On the monitors Kaelen had rigged along the damp concrete wall, the chaotic mosaic of civilian panic and enforcer retreats vanished. The screens displayed a synchronized loading bar, stark white against a crimson background.

"What is she doing?" Jarek asked, stepping closer to the displays, his heavy rifle lowered.

Kaelen's fingers blurred over her terminal. Lines of raw code reflected in her widened eyes. "Traffic is bottlenecking. It isn't a firewall. She's isolating the Spire's core network from the lower sectors." She paused, her cybernetic arm whirring as she forced an override. "She's initiating a batch protocol. A massive one."

Elias leaned closer to the monitor, his breath catching in his throat. Memories were supposed to be immutable, protected by layers of cryptographic seals. But Vane was the architect of the overwrite. He watched the data structures forming on the screen. "This is worse than an overwrite," Elias realized, cold dread settling in his stomach. "It's a purge. She's initiating the final contingency. A complete system wipe."

"A wipe?" Jarek's low gravel rumble carried a note of disbelief. "That will erase every localized memory core in the lower sectors. It will induce mass cognitive failure. The citizens won't just forget the rebellion; they'll forget how to breathe."

"That's the point," Elias said, his voice flat. "A city that cannot agree on what happened cannot tear itself apart. She's going to scorch the earth and rebuild from the ashes."

Kaelen slammed a fist against the console. "We can't counter-broadcast against a system wipe. The local security subroutines are locked behind her root clearance. The protocol is already compiling."

"Then we don't fight her code," Elias said, his mind racing through the schematics of the Nexus Spire he had memorized in the Archives. "We fight her infrastructure. The Spire's core network has to transmit the purge sequence through the physical data trunks running beneath the Sector Four black site."

Jarek looked at him, understanding dawning. "We sever the connection. We cut the city off from the Spire before the transmission completes."

"It's a suicide run," Kaelen said, but she was already disconnecting her terminal, packing her gear. "The black site will be locked down."

"It's our only move," Elias replied, grabbing his coat. The war of memories was over. Now, it was a war of survival.
