# Chapter 66

## Chapter Text

The silence lasted as long as it took the blinded turrets to reset their secondary logic loops. Above the cavernous transit hub, the automated emplacements swiveled blindly, their targeting sensors blown out by Elias's telemetry flood. But the Sector Four garrison did not rely solely on automated defenses.

From the far end of the concourse, the heavy, rhythmic thud of Committee Enforcers echoed over the grating. They advanced in a wedge formation, their matte-black tactical armor absorbing the flickering light of the failing lumen strips.

"Soft-skins," Jarek muttered, a low rumble of disdain. He didn't wait for them to close the distance. He charged the gap, his heavy slug-thrower leveled at the center mass of the lead Enforcer. The weapon bucked in his hands, sending a shower of sparks across the ferrocrete as the high-velocity rounds found their mark.

Kaelen moved laterally, slipping between a pair of rusted ticketing kiosks. Her movements were sharp of someone who had spent her life moving through the Undercity's blind spots. Her custom submachine gun spat a short, controlled burst, dropping an Enforcer who had broken formation to flank Jarek.

"Keep them pinned," Elias shouted over the sudden eruption of gunfire. He was crouched behind a shattered maintenance terminal, his fingers flying across his portable interface deck. The screen was a chaotic mess of red warnings and cascading code. He wasn't just managing the spoofed telemetry that kept the turrets offline; he was fighting a digital phantom.

*Director Vane.*

She was mapping his interference pattern, using the blind spots he created to triangulate his physical location. Every time he pushed garbage data into the localized grid, her counter-slicing algorithms tightened the net.

"Archivist," Kaelen's voice scraped over the comms channel. "The magnetic rail line to the right. Can you clear it?"

Elias glanced at his screen. The rail line was locked down by a physical security gate, a massive slab of reinforced steel that had slammed shut when the hub went into lockdown. "I can't open it remotely," he replied, his voice strained. "The command structure is isolated."

"Then break the isolation," Kaelen ordered.

"If I bridge the gap, I expose my root access to Vane's hunter algorithms," Elias warned. He rubbed his temples, feeling the familiar ache of a deep dive beginning to throb behind his eyes.

"We need that path, Thorne," Jarek bellowed, ducking as a plasma charge scored the pillar he was using for cover. "Break it."

Elias took a ragged breath and isolated the ambient roar of combat, dropping into the structured logic of the code. He found the physical relay for the security gate and slammed a brute-force override into the local node. The heavy steel slab groaned, grinding upward with a shower of sparks.

Instantly, his interface deck burned hot against his ribs. The screen flashed a brilliant, agonizing white. Vane's hunter algorithms hadn't just found him; they were surging through the bridged connection, attempting to fry his neural link with a massive feedback loop.

Elias gasped, his hands shaking as he frantically severed the hardline connection, dropping the interface deck onto the grating. The security gate remained open, but the smell of ozone and burning circuitry filled the air.

"Gate's clear," Elias choked out, his vision swimming. He looked up, the ache in his temples now a sharp, stabbing pain. "Move."

The resistance vanguard surged forward, pouring through the open gate and deeper into the transit arteries. They were gaining ground, but Elias knew it was a hollow victory. Vane wasn't just defending Sector Four. She was using it as a trap, and they had just walked willingly into the jaws.
