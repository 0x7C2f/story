# Chapter 15: Negotiating Trust

The heavy airlock gate groaned on rusted hinges, opening to reveal the chaotic heart of the Scrapyard Settlement. Elias blinked against the stinging, metallic dust as he and Kaelen were escorted inside by the armed guards. The sprawling settlement was a dense labyrinth of repurposed metal structures, bustling with people wrapped in thick, protective layers against the cold and grit. They were a tough, resilient people hardened by their harsh exile, their eyes tracking the newcomers with overt suspicion and wary curiosity.

They were led to a central clearing where a makeshift, reinforced command center sat atop a massive, gutted transit hauler. A man waited for them there, leaning against a rusted railing while observing their approach. He wore heavy, mismatched armor that was a patchwork of discarded city enforcer gear and scavenged metal plating. His face was weather-beaten from years in the Outskirts, his eyes sharp, calculating, and unforgiving.

"I'm Jarek," he said, his voice a low, gravel rumble that was almost lost in the howling wind. "They tell me you're the ones who killed the broadcast and plunged the city into chaos."

"We are," Elias replied, forcing himself to stand tall despite the bone-deep exhaustion weighing him down. "I'm Elias Thorne. This is Kaelen."

Jarek looked them up and down, unimpressed by their appearance. "You look like city soft-skins. What makes you think you're capable of bringing down the Oversight Committee's crown jewel?"

"Looks can be deceiving, as I'm sure you know," Kaelen said, her tone level but carrying a hard edge of firm resolve. "We disrupted the synchronization protocol. We stopped them from overwriting the entire city's collective memory."

Jarek chuckled, a dry, humorless sound that vanished into the wind. "Memory. That's a luxury for those who don't have to worry about where their next meal is coming from. Out here, we deal in scrap, water, and survival. Your high-minded memory wars mean nothing to the dust."

"It means they're vulnerable right now," Elias countered, stepping forward. "And it means they'll be looking for us soon. We need sanctuary."

"Sanctuary?" Jarek stepped closer, the sharp, pungent smell of machine grease and rust clinging to his armor. "This isn't a charity. Everyone here pulls their weight, or they get thrown out. What can you offer the Scrapyard?"

Elias considered their limited options. They had no weapons beyond Kaelen's sidearm, zero supplies, and no currency recognized anywhere in the Outskirts. But they had something else.

"Knowledge," Elias said. "I'm a trained memory archivist. I understand their complex systems, their advanced tech. I can see your main generators are struggling, and your vital filtration systems are patched with duct tape and hope. We can fix them. We can upgrade them."

Kaelen nodded in agreement. "I know their patrol routes. Their layered security. I can fortify this place. Harder for them to find us. Harder for them to bother you."

Jarek studied them in silence for a long, uncomfortable moment. He glanced over at the coughing exhaust vents of the nearby hab structures.

"Words alone don't keep the cold out," Jarek said, his expression remaining hard and unreadable. "But concrete actions might just buy you some time. You'll get a damp corner in the lower hab block for tonight. Tomorrow morning, you prove you're as useful as you claim to be. If you can't, you're going right back out into the dust."

It wasn't a warm welcome by any means, but it was a much-needed start. Elias nodded. "We'll prove it."
