# Chapter 29

## Chapter Text

The Scrapyard Settlement transformed into a fortress of rusted metal and salvaged energy shielding. Elias Thorne stood near the central spire, the lingering ache of the neural feedback a dull thrum behind his eyes. He watched as Jarek directed the scavengers, turning the trading plaza into a layered kill zone. Heavy debris barricades were dragged into position, while snipers took their places atop the precarious hab-units.

"The perimeter sensors are tracking the first wave," Kaelen reported, her fingers moving rapidly over the portable tactical console they had rigged near the generator. She did not look up from the screen. "Heavy sweep teams. They are advancing in a wedge formation from the eastern ridge. Vane is not sending standard enforcement. These are Committee loyalists."

"They want a decisive victory," Elias said, his voice steady despite the exhaustion gripping his muscles. "Director Vane lost her primary staging ground. If she fails to secure the root key and silence this broadcast, her authority in Sector One shatters. She has to commit everything to this strike."

Jarek approached, his heavy slug-thrower resting against his shoulder. His face was smeared with engine grease and dead-zone dust. "My people are ready, Archivist. We hold the choke points. They have the armor, but we know the terrain. We can bleed them for every yard."

Elias met the scavenger leader's gaze. "You do not need to defeat them, Jarek. You just need to hold them here. Draw their command structure into the engagement. Force them to escalate until they pull their reserves from the Spire."

"A diversion," Jarek agreed, his voice a low, rumbling growl. "We buy you the opening. You and Kaelen slip through the southern drainage network while they are focused on breaking our front gate."

"The drainage network is narrow," Kaelen cautioned, finally looking up from the telemetry feed. "It bypasses the primary jamming field, but we will have no comms once we are subterranean. If the settlement falls..."

"It will not fall," Jarek stated, a hard edge to his words. "The Committee thinks we are rats hiding in the scrap. Today, they learn what happens when you corner us."

Elias checked the modified synchronization interface one last time. The root key was secure in his jacket pocket, but the ghost signal—a looping, decentralized ping designed to mimic the root key's signature—was primed for activation.

"When I initiate the decoy signal, their tactical net will lock onto this exact coordinate," Elias explained, adjusting the frequency band. "They will concentrate their heavy ordnance on the central spire, thinking I am trying to re-establish the counter-broadcast."

He looked at Kaelen, then at Jarek. The sheer scale of the risk weighed heavily on him. He was trading the lives of these outcasts for a chance to strike at the heart of the Committee. But it was the only path forward. The truth had a price, and they were all paying it.

"Initiating the decoy," Elias said.

He slammed his hand onto the activation array. A piercing, high-frequency whine emanated from the spire, a beacon in the digital void. Almost immediately, the horizon beyond the settlement walls lit up with the brilliant, searing flash of incoming artillery. The ground shuddered as the first impact struck the outer barricades, throwing plumes of ferrocrete and ash into the smog-choked sky.

The siege of the Outskirts had begun. Elias grabbed his pack, his eyes locked on the southern exit.

"Move," Jarek bellowed over the deafening roar of the bombardment, raising his weapon. "Give them hell!"

Elias turned and sprinted toward the drainage network, Kaelen close behind him, leaving the settlement to hold the line against the encroaching darkness.
