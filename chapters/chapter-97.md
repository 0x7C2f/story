# Elias at Sector Six Staging Ground - Phase 97
## Outline

1. Opening beat: The mag-lev train approaches the maintenance sublevels of Sector Six, but the tracks end at a sealed barricade. Elias forces an emergency stop.
2. Escalation: They disembark and find an access shaft leading up to the Sector Six staging ground. They discover it's fortified by Vane's loyalists.
3. Turn or decision: Elias realizes the staging ground's central repeater is the only way to broadcast the uncorrupted history to the city.

## Chapter Text

The ancient mag-lev train screamed. It tore through the pitch-black transit tunnel, the rusted chassis vibrating so violently Elias thought it might shake apart. The air inside the cabin was stale. It hung heavy with the smell of ozone, thick and suffocating.

"We are running out of track," Kaelen shouted over the deafening clatter, leaning over the cracked navigation console. "The schematics show a maintenance hub ahead, but the line is dead."

Elias checked the haptic readout projected above his wrist. Fragmented data streamed from the ancient system—a mess of archaic code and warning alerts. "The hub is sealed," he yelled back. His fingers flew across the virtual keys. "There is a ferrocrete barricade blocking the line."

"Stop it then," Jarek grunted, bracing himself against the reinforced doorframe of the passenger car.

"I am trying," Elias said, sweat stinging his eyes. The rusted magnetic dampeners were fighting every command he fed them through his neural rig. He bypassed the primary throttle, dumping raw power into the emergency braking system.

The train shuddered. Metal screamed against metal as the rusted locks engaged, tearing a deafening shriek through the tight confines of the tunnel. The sudden deceleration threw Elias against the console. Hard. Sparks showered the cabin, momentarily illuminating the massive, unyielding ferrocrete barricade that loomed in the darkness ahead. The train ground to a halt with a final, violent lurch. It stopped barely ten meters from the wall.

"We are clear," Elias gasped, pushing himself off the console. The train hissed, a cloud of steam rising from the undercarriage.

Kaelen didn't wait. She forced the cabin door open, stepping out onto the narrow maintenance platform. "Let's move before it catches fire," she said, her rifle already tracking the darkness.

Elias uncoupled his rig. The sudden disconnect left a sharp ache behind his eyes, a familiar throb that pulsed in time with his racing heartbeat. He followed Kaelen onto the platform. His boots echoed loudly in the massive, cavernous space of the abandoned hub. Jarek dropped down behind them. His imposing figure dwarfed the rusted turnstiles.

"There's an access shaft at the far end of the platform," Elias said, pointing toward a heavy blast door half-hidden in the shadows. "It leads up to the Sector Six staging ground."

They moved quickly, the silence of the maintenance hub oppressive after the deafening roar of the train. Jarek forced the blast door open with a dull groan of protesting metal. A narrow spiral staircase wound upward, disappearing into the gloom.

"Quietly," Kaelen whispered, taking the lead.

They climbed for what felt like hours, the air growing warmer and thicker with the smell of engine grease and ozone. As they neared the top, a dull hum vibrated through the metal grating beneath their feet. Elias recognized the sound immediately: the idling engines of heavy transports.

Kaelen stopped, holding up a hand. She peered through a narrow grating near the top of the shaft, her eyes narrowing. "It's fortified," she whispered, stepping back so Elias could see.

Elias pressed his face against the cold metal. The Sector Six staging ground was a sprawling yard of heavy ferrocrete barricades and blinding arc lights. Dozens of Committee enforcers moved with clipped, precise purpose, marshaling heavy transports equipped with massive signal repeaters.

"Vane is locking down the perimeter," Jarek whispered, his voice low and dangerous.

Elias stared at the central distribution hub dominating the center of the yard. Atop its reinforced roof sat the primary signal repeater, a massive antenna designed to relay data to the entire sector.

"We don't need to bypass them." Elias said it with a flat, certain voice. He looked back at Kaelen and Jarek. "We have the uncorrupted history. We reach that central repeater, and we broadcast it. We push it to the entire city."

"It's a suicide run," Kaelen said, her grip tightening on her rifle.

"It's the only way," Elias replied. "If Vane deploys those mobile repeaters, she'll overwrite the city before we can mount a defense. We hit the hub."
