# Elias at New Providence Outskirts - Phase 40
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek flee the destroyed Sector Six staging ground in the captured heavy transport.
2. They race against Director Vane's advancing loyalist sweep teams toward the safety of the Outskirts.
3. Elias suffers from a blinding migraine due to the recent system overloads. They manage to evade the immediate sweep teams and plan their next move to solidify the resistance.

---

## Chapter Text

The heavy transport shuddered violently as it ground to a halt beneath the crumbling skeleton of an industrial crane. The engine ticked as it cooled, the metal radiating a harsh, metallic heat into the frozen air of the Outskirts. Jarek killed the primary drive, plunging the cabin into absolute darkness save for the weak, amber glow of the damaged tactical console.

Elias Thorne slumped against the bulkhead, his breath shallow and ragged. The migraine had reduced the world to a pulsing, localized pressure behind his eyes, a physical manifestation of the neural load he had carried since the Nexus Spire. He pressed the heels of his hands against his temples, trying to steady the frantic beating of his heart.

"We are clear," Jarek said, his voice a low, gravel rumble that vibrated through the metal floorboards. He unbuckled his restraints and stood, rolling his broad shoulders. "The spoofed telemetry held. They are searching the transit tunnels three sectors away."

Kaelen moved forward from the rear compartment, her boots hitting the deck plates with sharp, precise rhythm. She checked the seal on her carbine before sweeping a critical gaze over the ruined console. "The hardware is failing," she stated, her clipped tone betraying none of the exhaustion Elias felt. "The transport took plasma scoring along the port side, and the primary array is compromised."

Elias forced his eyes open, staring at the faint readouts. The mesh network they had fought so desperately to establish was a chaotic tangle of fragmented signals. "The staging ground is gone," he whispered, the procedural certainty of his past life failing him. "The repeaters were our anchor."

Jarek crossed his arms, his silhouette immense against the dim console light. "Vane burned our advantage. She isolated the grid and executed a clean strike."

"It was not a clean strike," Elias argued, pushing himself upright. His muscles ached, but the cold logic of the archives was slowly returning to him. "She destroyed her own infrastructure to stop us. That means she is desperate."

"Desperate or not, she has the resources," Kaelen countered. "We have a crippled transport and a fractured network."

Elias activated his wrist terminal, the pale blue light throwing sharp shadows across his face. He pulled up the fragmented data from the counter-broadcast, watching the corrupted files spool across the small screen. "The signal repeaters at Sector Six are gone, but the nodes we activated during the initial broadcast—the ones scattered across the lower sectors—they are still listening."

Jarek narrowed his eyes, tracking the logic. "They are listening, but we have no way to talk to them without the primary array."

"We don't need a primary array if we change the protocol," Elias said, his mind locking onto the problem. "We were treating the network like a central broadcast system. If we modify the transport's communication suite, we can use it as a mobile transmitter. We ping the dormant nodes individually and force them to relay the signal."

"A decentralized mesh," Kaelen murmured, leaning over the console. "It would take longer to establish a city-wide connection."

"But it would be impossible for Vane to track a single point of origin," Elias finished. "She would have to hunt down every node to stop the broadcast."

Jarek stared out the cracked viewport, the bruised, violet sky of the Outskirts a stark reminder of their isolation. He weighed the scrap, the survival odds, and the sheer audacity of the plan.

"It will require stripping the transport's armor to boost the signal strength," Jarek finally said. "And if they paint our transponder while we are transmitting, we won't have the speed to run."

"If we don't transmit," Elias said, his voice steadying, "we have already lost."

Kaelen slung her carbine over her shoulder, her expression resolute. "Then we start stripping the armor. I will reconfigure the communication suite."

Elias watched her move toward the rear compartment, the immediate crisis shifting into a concrete set of actionable tasks. He looked back at Jarek, the scavenger leader already pulling a heavy wrench from his utility belt.

"The Committee thinks they buried the truth in Sector Six," Elias said, the pain in his skull receding behind a cold, rising determination. "Let's show them what a ghost looks like."
