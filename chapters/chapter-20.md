# Chapter 20

The dust of the Outskirts tasted like copper and old rust. Elias knelt beside the battered chassis of a military-grade signal repeater, wiping a streak of grime from his forehead. The unit hummed, a low and steady vibration that traveled through his boots. Beside him, Kaelen handed over a spool of coaxial cable, her expression tight with focus.

"That's the third node online," Elias said, his voice clipped. "If the triangulation holds, we'll have a mesh network running from the Scrapyard Settlement all the way to the perimeter grid."

"It has to hold," Kaelen replied. She glanced over her shoulder at the jagged horizon of the Dead Zone, her eyes narrowing against the biting wind that whipped the dust into a stinging frenzy. "We didn't risk radiation exposure just to build a glorified radio."

Jarek approached, his heavy steps crunching on the loose gravel. The Scavenger leader carried a makeshift antenna, its surface scarred but functional. "The settlement generator is holding the load," Jarek said, setting the antenna down. "But we're bleeding power to keep the signal masked. How long until the network is fully operational?"

Elias checked his wrist terminal, tapping a sequence on the haptic keyboard. "Fifteen minutes. We need to sync the handshakes across all three repeaters before we can push a test broadcast. After that, we'll have a shadow network that the central tower can't jam."

The wind shifted, bringing the faint, acrid scent of ozone. Elias frowned, looking up from his screen. He knew that smell. It belonged to the heavy repulsor engines of Committee transports.

"Do you hear that?" Kaelen asked, her voice dropping to a whisper.

Jarek moved instantly, scrambling up a pile of rusted corrugated iron to get a better vantage point. He raised a pair of scuffed macro-binoculars to his eyes. For a long moment, the only sound was the howling wind and the steady hum of the repeater.

"Sweep teams," Jarek said, his voice flat. "Loyalists. They're moving in a line across the eastern ridge."

A cold knot tightened in Elias's stomach. Director Vane hadn't wasted any time. "How many?" he asked, already reaching for the portable neural rig tethered to his hip.

"Enough," Jarek replied, sliding down the rubble. "They've got armor, and they're sweeping the perimeter grid. They're looking for the source of the counter-broadcast."

"They'll find the mesh network if we turn it on," Kaelen said.

"If we don't turn it on, we're cut off," Elias countered. "We lose the only advantage we have."

He looked at the repeater, the culmination of their desperate salvage run. Protocol dictated that he should power it down, hide it, and wait for the threat to pass. The archivist in him—the man who had seen the truth overwritten—understood that waiting was just another form of erasure. It was unacceptable.

"We finish the sync," Elias said, his fingers flying across the haptic keys. "Jarek, get your people ready. We're going to use the network to track their movements before they track us."

## Chapter Notes

Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek set up the salvaged mesh network repeaters in the Outskirts to establish a secure comms line. During the sync, Jarek spots loyalist sweep teams deployed by Director Vane searching the perimeter. Instead of hiding the equipment, Elias decides to finish the sync to use the network to track the loyalists' movements, turning a desperate defense into a tactical advantage.
