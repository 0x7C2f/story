## Chapter Text

The ambient heat of the foundry settled over the staging ground, a dense, metallic fugue that carried the scent of ozone and burnt lubricant. Hundreds of workers stood shoulder to shoulder in the cavernous assembly hall, their silhouettes cut out against the amber glow of the active smelters. They held whatever makeshift implements of violence they could scrounge: modified rivet drivers, plasma cutters stripped of their safety limiters, and jagged lengths of hardened rebar.

Elias Thorne stood on a rusted gantry overlooking the assembly. He had spent his entire career cataloging the silent, immutable past. Now, looking at the grim, soot-stained faces of the resistance, he was watching the present fracture into something entirely unpredictable.

"This is not a precision strike," Jarek's low gravel rumble carried easily over the low murmur of the crowd. He stood beside Elias, the enforcer's massive frame armored in layered ballistic plating salvaged from the neutralized Goliath frames. "The inner guard relies on structure. Choke points. Calculated risk. We give them none of that."

Kaelen checked the power cell of her custom submachine gun, her movements clipped and efficient. She didn't look up from her weapon. "Vane thinks we're pinned down. She's staging her forces around the upper tiers, securing the central router. If we hit the transit hub in Sector Four, we force her to shift assets to protect the lower transit arteries. If she doesn't, we sever the Spire's physical supply lines."

"And if she does?" Elias asked, his eyes tracking a group of workers hauling a jury-rigged explosive charge toward the main loading bay doors.

"Then the Spire's perimeter weakens," Kaelen said, snapping the cell into place. "And we walk right through the front door."

Elias gripped the cold iron railing. The mathematics of the strategy were sound, yet the human cost was staggering. These were not soldiers. They were mechanics, fabricators, and haulers. They were walking into the teeth of the Committee's most disciplined security force.

"You're calculating the odds, archivist," Jarek noted, turning his heavy gaze toward Elias.

"I am calculating the acceptable margin of error," Elias replied, his procedural training grounding the spike of anxiety in his chest. "The primary mesh node is primed. Once the assault begins, the interference pattern will mask our movements, but it won't stop a kinetic round."

"Nothing stops a kinetic round except armor or flesh," Jarek stated, a grim fact offered without inflection. He raised his hand, signaling the block captains scattered throughout the crowd. "We go."

The heavy hydraulic lock of the assembly hall doors disengaged with a shuddering groan that rattled Elias's teeth. The massive metal sheets parted, revealing the long, shadowed stretch of the transit tunnel leading directly into Sector Four.

The workers moved forward. There was no battle cry, only the collective, heavy rhythm of boots on steel grating. It was the sound of a city turning against its architects.

Elias kept pace with Kaelen and Jarek, the trio moving just behind the vanguard. The tunnel walls were slick with condensation, the pale lumen strips overhead flickering erratically. Elias kept his hand on the portable interface deck slung across his chest. The mesh network was holding, the nodes broadcasting a clean, steady pulse of encrypted data.

"Approaching the outer perimeter," Kaelen reported, her voice tight. "Thermal sensors ahead. Three... no, four automated emplacements."

"Can you blind them?" Jarek asked, unholstering a heavy slug-thrower.

Elias tapped into the network, his fingers flying across the haptic keyboard. "The local grid is isolated. I can flood the telemetry, but the physical failsafes will trigger. They will default to proximity tracking."

"How close?" Kaelen asked.

"Ten meters," Elias said. "Inside the kill zone."

When the vanguard reached the end of the tunnel, the rusted archway gave way to the Sector Four transit hub—a massive cavern of interlocking platforms and magnetic rails. Absolute silence hung in the cavern until the high whine of targeting servos cut through the damp air.

From the high gantries overlooking the platforms, twin-barreled automated turrets swiveled, their optic sensors burning a harsh, synthetic red. The proximity tracking locked onto the vanguard.

The first turrets opened fire, the deafening roar of high-velocity rounds tearing the silence apart.
