# Chapter 19: Director Vane's Move

Director Vane stood in the command center of Sector Vane, an industrial space buried deep in the sub-basements of the Nexus Spire. The room was lit almost entirely by the cool blue of holographic displays, a harsh contrast to the heavy matte fabric of her long, dark coat. A stylized silver shield pin, the only insignia she permitted herself, caught the light as she rested her right hand on the edge of the central black console.

Her posture was relaxed, but the slight furrow between her brows betrayed the intensity of her focus. On the main display, the status of Project: Synchronization flashed a dull, error-ridden red. The city-wide overwrite was dead.

"History is not a record of what happened," she murmured to the empty air, her voice an unhurried, administrative hum that carried more authority than a shout. "It is a maintenance schedule. And someone has disrupted the schedule."

Behind her, Enforcers moved in silent, practiced sweeps across the lower terminals, feeding her continuous data readouts. The breach was clear: a massive signal disruption originating from the central broadcast tower, deploying the very root key she had air-gapped beneath her feet. She filed the irony away. Paranoia had long ago immunized her against surprise.

"Status on the broadcast tower," Vane demanded.

A technician at the nearest console didn't look up, fingers flying across the interface. "The counter-broadcast has ceased, Director. We have severed the feed, but the unauthorized packet has already saturated the grid. The command structure is... fracturing. We are seeing localized uprisings in Sectors Two and Four. The Committee's internal records are bleeding into the public channels."

Vane absorbed the information without a flinch. A city that could not agree on what happened could not tear itself apart; therefore, the record belonged to whoever was strong enough to keep it stable. Elias Thorne, a low-level archivist, and his accomplice Kaelen, had introduced instability. They believed a curated past was a debt, but they didn't understand the cost of the interest.

"The broadcast tower is a symptom. The infection is mobile," Vane said, her voice devoid of panic. "They are no longer in the city. The perimeter sensors picked up anomalies hours ago."

"The Outskirts, Director?" The technician sounded doubtful. "Survival rates there are negligible."

"Do not mistake desperation for incompetence," Vane replied. "They breached my vault. They hijacked the central tower. They will not die quietly in the dust." She turned fully toward the main console, her silver hair catching the blue light. "You are not being punished," she said, addressing the room, though her eyes remained fixed on the tactical map of the city perimeter. "You are being corrected. There is a difference, and it is entirely mine to define."

She brought up the localized grid of the Dead Zone and the Scrapyard Settlement. "Deploy the loyalist sweep teams. I want a full perimeter lockdown by midday. Deny him the Outskirts. Then deny him again. I want the log of every attempt. We will not wait for them to build a resistance; we will dismantle the foundation before it cures."

Vane stepped back, her silhouette a stark line against the glowing projections. The synchronization may have failed, but the Committee was not dead, and neither was she. The board had simply changed.
