## Chapter Text

Elias threw himself forward as the heavy blast door began to slide. The high-pitched whine of the heavy sentry's rotary cannon peaked, and a torrent of plasma fire chewed through the ferrocrete where he had been standing a fraction of a second before. He scrambled through the narrowing gap, but not fast enough. A searing bolt clipped his left shoulder, tearing through his heavy archivist coat and the flesh beneath.

He hit the floor inside the central distribution hub with a stifled cry, rolling to disperse the momentum. The heavy door slammed shut behind him with a final, echoing thud, cutting off the deafening roar of the battle outside.

The interior of the hub was a stark contrast to the chaos of the staging ground. It was a cavernous, perfectly climate-controlled space, filled with the low, steady hum of towering server racks and the pulsing blue light of data streams. It smelled of ozone and clean, cold silicon.

Elias struggled to his knees, his left arm hanging limp at his side, hot blood soaking into the sleeve of his coat. He gasped for air, the pain radiating from his shoulder in sharp, blinding waves. He couldn't afford to stop. If Kaelen and Jarek couldn't hold off the enforcers, he had only minutes before they breached the door.

He staggered to his feet and lurched toward the primary control console situated at the center of the room. It was a massive, sweeping crescent of polished black glass and holographic interfaces. He leaned heavily against it, his right hand shaking as he fumbled for his neural rig's tether cable.

*Focus,* he told himself, his voice a harsh rasp in the quiet room. *Execute the protocol.*

He jammed the interface jack into the raw data port on the console. Pain spiked behind his eyes—sharper, more violent than before—as he brute-forced the final layer of local security protocols, dumping raw feedback directly into the hub's mainframe.

"Computer, run a bypass on the external routing firewall," Elias muttered, his voice tight with pain.

*Access Granted.*

With trembling fingers, he retrieved the uncorrupted history drive from his pocket. The physical drive, a relic from a bygone era, felt heavy with consequence. He slammed it into the console's primary reader slot.

The console flared with urgent, amber warnings, but Elias ignored them. He triggered the upload sequence.

Across the city, in her command center deep within the Nexus Spire, Director Vane stood perfectly still. Her hands rested lightly on the edge of the tactical display table as the holographic map of Sector Six flared brilliant crimson. A massive, unauthorized data stream was surging through the central distribution hub, overriding the primary repeaters and pushing outward into the city's mesh network.

Her unhurried, administrative hum was gone. The furrow between her brows deepened into a rigid line of absolute fury.

The broadcast had begun. The truth was unspooling into the minds of New Providence.
