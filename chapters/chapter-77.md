# Elias at The Undercity - Phase 77
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek push through the dark, unmapped tunnel in the deep Undercity.
2. They discover an old pre-Committee transit station, untouched by the recent conflicts.
3. Elias finds a dormant terminal that might still have a connection to the surface, offering a glimmer of hope.

## Chapter Text

The silence of the sealed tunnel was absolute, broken only by the ragged sound of their own breathing and the steady drip of condensation. Elias clicked on his shoulder-mounted lumen, the weak beam cutting a narrow path through the dust-choked air. The collapsed service artery lay behind them, a tomb of twisted concrete and the crushed remains of the hunter drone.

"We are buried," Jarek said softly, his voice echoing flatly against the damp walls. "There is no way back."

Kaelen was already moving forward, her rifle held tightly against her chest. "Then we go deeper. The Undercity wasn't built in a straight line. There will be other shafts, other arteries."

Elias followed her lead, his injured knee protesting with every step. The tunnel widened gradually, the rough-hewn stone giving way to reinforced plasteel archways that had not seen maintenance in decades. The air grew colder, carrying the faint, metallic scent of ozone and ancient rust.

They walked in silence for what felt like hours, moving through a labyrinth of forgotten substructures. Finally, the tunnel opened into a massive cavernous space. The lumen beam swept across cracked ceramic tiles and rows of rusted turnstiles. The skeletal remains of a transit car sat idly on a mag-lev track that stretched into the darkness.

"A pre-Committee station," Kaelen observed, her tone holding a rare note of surprise. "Off the grid. Untouched."

Elias limped past the turnstiles, his eyes drawn to a bulky console situated near the center of the platform. It was covered in a thick layer of dust, but the underlying structure was surprisingly intact. He brushed his hand over the interface, feeling the familiar contours of a dormant memory node.

"Jarek," Elias said, his voice barely a whisper. "Bring the light over here. I think this terminal still has a hardline to the surface." He looked at Kaelen, a flicker of hope overriding his exhaustion. "We might not be as buried as we thought."
