# Chapter 17: Forging a Resistance

The rhythmic hum of the newly stabilized generator resonated through the floor plating, a constant reminder of their newly minted alliance. Elias sat in Jarek's makeshift command center, a repurposed shipping container reinforced with scavenged armor plating. Holographic schematics of the city limits flickered across a cracked display table, casting a harsh, blue light over the room.

Kaelen leaned against the bulkhead, her arms crossed. "The Oversight Committee won't just let us go. The counter-broadcast disrupted their immediate synchronization, but it didn't dismantle their infrastructure. They will regroup, and when they do, they'll sweep the Outskirts."

Jarek traced a scarred finger along a projected topographical map, highlighting the jagged canyons that separated the Scrapyard Settlement from Sector Four. "My scouts report increased drone activity along the ridge. They are already searching. But out here, their sensors are partially blinded by the magnetic interference from the scrap fields."

"That buys us time, not safety," Elias said, his gaze fixed on the glowing map. "The root key is still active. I can use it to build a localized shadow network, linking our settlement's communication arrays and creating secure channels that bypass the city grid entirely. But we need more than just secure comms. We need a way to fight back."

"A resistance," Jarek said, testing the word. He looked at Elias, his expression unreadable. "My people are survivors, archivist. We fight for scraps and clean water. Taking on the Committee means going to war against the most heavily armed faction in New Providence."

"They already declared war when they decided to rewrite our minds," Kaelen countered, her voice carrying a sharp edge. "The broadcast woke people up. The command structure is fracturing. If we strike now, while they are disorganized, we can break their grip on the central servers."

Elias nodded in agreement. "We have the technical advantage. I know their systems, and Kaelen knows their tactical protocols. You have the numbers and the knowledge of the terrain. If we combine our resources, we can establish a true resistance. But we need more equipment to build the shadow network—specifically, high-capacity signal repeaters."

Jarek rubbed his jaw thoughtfully. "There's an old, abandoned relay station about twenty klicks east, on the edge of the Dead Zone. Heavily irradiated. The hardware should be intact. We've never risked the salvage, because the payout wasn't worth the exposure. You know what that radiation does to a man out there?"

"It's worth it now," Elias said, leaning forward. "If we retrieve those repeaters, we can link the Scrapyard Settlement directly to the isolated resistance cells that are forming inside the city. We can coordinate a unified front."

Jarek stared at the flickering map for a long moment before nodding slowly. "Alright. I'll assemble a salvage team. Kaelen, you're leading the security detail. Elias, you're with me. We make sure the tech is still viable before we haul it back. Any of you know what a signal repeater looks like?"

As the meeting concluded and they began to finalize the logistics for the salvage run, Elias felt a subtle shift in the atmosphere. The desperation of their flight from the city had evolved into something sharper, something far more potent. They were no longer just running; they were organizing. They were building a resistance.
