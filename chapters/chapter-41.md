# Chapter 41

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek maneuver the damaged heavy transport into an isolated, rusted canyon in the Outskirts.
2. Jarek physically strips the transport's heavy armor to expose the transmitter array, while Kaelen rewires the communication suite for decentralized broadcast.
3. Elias synchronizes the raw transmission with the scattered dormant nodes, initiating the new, untraceable mesh network just as Committee interceptors begin sweeping the adjacent sectors.

---

## Chapter Text

The transport scraped against the jagged rock face of the dry wash, the screech of protesting metal loud in the desolate canyon. Jarek killed the engines, the sudden silence thick and heavy beneath the violet sky of the Outskirts. The secluded ravine offered temporary cover from the Committee's high-altitude sweeps, a rare blind spot in the grid.

Elias unbuckled his restraints, ignoring the persistent, dull ache in his temples. He unspooled a coil of heavy-gauge optical cable from the supply bin, his hands moving with sharp, purposeful efficiency. "We have a three-hour window before Vane's interceptors expand their search pattern into this quadrant," he stated, throwing the cable toward the rear compartment.

Kaelen caught the coil, already deep into the exposed access panels of the primary communication suite. The scent of ozone and burning flux filled the cramped cabin as she bypassed the proprietary limiters. "The hardware is fighting me. The Committee hardcoded these transmitters to require a central handshake."

"Then break the handshake," Elias replied, moving to the auxiliary terminal. "We aren't asking for permission to transmit. We are forcing a brute-force injection into the local sub-nodes."

Outside the transport, the sound of tearing metal echoed off the canyon walls. Jarek stood on the roof of the vehicle, wielding an industrial plasma torch and a heavy pry bar. Sparks cascaded over the windshield as he sliced through the reinforced plasteel armor plating that housed the transmission array. He kicked a massive slab of armor free, letting it crash into the dust below.

"Array is exposed," Jarek shouted down through the open hatch, his face streaked with grease and sweat. "You are stripped of all defensive plating. If a sweep team spots us, a single plasma round will vaporize the cabin."

"Understood," Elias called back. "Keep the heat sink clear. We are going to push this transmitter far past its thermal threshold."

Elias brought the auxiliary terminal online. The screen flickered, displaying the chaotic static of the Outskirts' fragmented frequencies. He began isolating the faint, encrypted pings of the dormant nodes they had activated during their initial assault. The raw data stream was a mess of corrupted protocols and ghost signals.

"I am patching the bypass," Kaelen said, her voice tight with concentration. She connected the optical cable to the bypass rig, her fingers a blur over the manual overrides. "Transmitter is slaved to your terminal."

Elias cracked his knuckles. He initiated the sequence, sending a localized, low-frequency ping into the void. The terminal hung for a second, the progress bar stalling in the red. Then, a single node in Sector Four responded, a solitary green blip in a sea of static.

"We have a handshake," Elias said, his pulse accelerating. "Sector Four is listening."

He adjusted the frequency, casting the net wider. A second node in the Undercity flared to life. Then a third in Sector Six. The decentralized mesh network began to form, a fragile, invisible web of interconnected points.

"Vane is hunting for a massive, centralized broadcast," Elias murmured, his eyes tracking the expanding web. "She won't see a thousand whispers until they form a scream."

"Warning track," Jarek's voice dropped into the cabin, harsh and urgent. "I have visual on two Committee interceptors entering the canyon mouth. They are running thermal sweeps."

Kaelen racked the bolt of her carbine, the metallic clatter stark against the hum of the terminal. "How much longer, Elias?"

"The protocol needs ninety seconds to lock the mesh," Elias replied, his hands hovering over the final execution command. The pale light of the screen illuminated his face.

The low, oscillating whine of the interceptors drifted down the canyon, growing steadily louder. The thermal sweeps would paint their exposed heat signature in moments.

"Jarek, get down here," Elias ordered, his finger resting on the execution key. "We broadcast now."
