# Chapter 5: Sector Four Infiltration

## Outline

1. Approach: Elias and Kaelen navigate the flooded ruins of the old Sector Four transit hub.
2. Infiltration: Bypassing the perimeter sensors using Kaelen's scavenged tech.
3. Discovery: Accessing a dormant terminal and pulling the batch protocol manifest.
4. Escape: An automated security sweep forces them to flee before downloading the full log.

---

## Chapter Text

The transit hub in Sector Four had been abandoned long before the digital sea levels rose. Now, it was a subterranean cathedral of rusting mag-lev tracks and pooling, oil-slicked water. Elias adjusted the respirator mask over his face, the air here thick with the smell of ozone and decay.

"Keep your footing light," Kaelen murmured, her voice a low scrape through the comms channel. She moved ahead, a shadow slipping between the cracked support pillars. "The Committee doesn't patrol this far down. They leave trip-wires in the network. One stray biometric ping and we're ghosts."

Elias swallowed the knot of tension in his throat. His neural implant felt heavy, an anchor weighing him down. If they were caught, the Oversight Committee wouldn't just kill him; they would erase him. They would rewrite his existence until 'Elias Thorne' was nothing more than a statistical anomaly in the archives.

"I see the access point," Elias whispered. He pointed to a recessed alcove where the damp concrete gave way to smooth, modern composite paneling. A single red status LED pulsed in the gloom like a slow heartbeat.

Kaelen knelt by the panel, pulling a frayed splicing rig from her satchel. "Standard Committee architecture. Brutalist. Efficient. Let's see if Director Vane left the back door unlocked." She connected the rig, her fingers dancing across a haptic projection. A moment later, the red LED flared green. The panel slid open with a pneumatic hiss, revealing a narrow service corridor lit by cold strip lighting.

They stepped inside, the oppressive damp of the ruins instantly replaced by the sterilized, climate-controlled air of a server farm. Rows of obsidian archive nodes lined the walls, identical to the ones in Elias's workspace, but these were unmarked, stripped of all public-facing metadata.

"A black site," Elias breathed. The sheer scale of it was staggering. Thousands of nodes, each capable of holding lifetimes of compressed memory.

"Get to work," Kaelen said, handing Elias a tether cable. "I'll watch the uplink. You have three minutes before the automated sweeps cycle back to this sector."

Elias plugged the tether into the nearest primary node, his own neural interface flaring as it bypassed the physical airgap. Lines of raw sensory data cascaded across his vision. It was a chaotic storm of disconnected lives—birthdays, arguments, quiet moments of grief—all being run through a massive, automated filter.

He found the directory he was looking for: the batch protocol logs.

"I've got it," Elias said, his heart hammering against his ribs. "The manifest. It's... it's industrial. They aren't just altering individual files. They're changing regional sentiment, overwriting historical events. Vane's signature is on the approval chain for the entire Sector Two uprising."

"Pull the log," Kaelen urged, checking her wrist monitor. "Two minutes."

Elias initiated the extraction, the tether glowing as the dense data packet began to transfer to his secure local drive. The progress bar crawled. It was too much data; the raw, unredacted memories were bloated with sensory feedback.

*Warning: Unauthorized Access Detected in Sector 4-B.*

The synthetic voice echoed from the ceiling speakers, calm and terrifyingly polite. The strip lighting shifted from white to a harsh, strobing amber.

"They found the splice," Kaelen snapped, yanking her rig from the wall panel. "We have to go. Now!"

"It's only at seventy percent!" Elias shouted over the rising wail of an alarm siren.

"Seventy percent is enough to get us killed, and more than enough to prove your point," Kaelen grabbed Elias by the shoulder. "Pull it!"

Elias severed the tether. The transfer terminated abruptly, leaving a jagged, incomplete file in his drive. He didn't look back as they sprinted down the service corridor, the heavy composite door sealing shut behind them just as the first automated security drones descended into the flooded ruins of the transit hub.
