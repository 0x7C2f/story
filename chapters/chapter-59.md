# Vane at The Nexus Spire - Phase 59
## Outline

1. Vane evaluates the extent of the memory leak in the Spire's primary command center.

3. She orders loyalist sweep teams to descend into the Undercity and establish a perimeter ahead of them.

## Chapter Text

Director Vane stood in the center of the primary command center, surrounded by the stark red glow of critical alerts. The catastrophic data hemorrhage from the Nexus Archives continued to ripple outward. The localized hard reset had sterilized the immediate neural pathways in the lower sectors, but the uncompressed histories were already finding secondary routes through the compromised mesh network.

"Containment efficiency is dropping," the central intelligence reported, its synthesized voice a sterile counterpoint to the cascading alarms. "Project: Synchronization infrastructure is severely degraded."

Vane did not react to the noise. Her attention remained fixed on the three-dimensional schematic of the Nexus Spire projected before her. The architecture she had spent years building was fracturing. Elias Thorne had not merely stolen the cryptographic root key; he had turned her own delivery mechanism into a weapon.

"Reroute all remaining processing power to the primary firewall," Vane ordered, her voice an unhurried, administrative hum that cut through the command center's chaos. "Seal the structural breaches in the lower vaults. Isolate the compromised sectors."

She tapped a rhythmic command into the haptic console. The schematic shifted, zooming in on the sub-levels beneath the Archives. The thermal sensors, temporarily blinded by the hard reset's static wash, were beginning to re-establish connectivity.

"Locate the fugitives," she said.

The system highlighted a narrow, vertical shaft dropping away from the primary data arteries. An old auxiliary exhaust vent, unconnected to the main grid, descending straight into the labyrinth of the Undercity. It was a blind spot, a forgotten artery from before the Spire's expansion.

Vane traced the trajectory with a gloved finger. Thorne and his Scavenger allies had slipped the snare. They were moving off the grid, beyond the reach of the automated containment protocols.

"They have accessed the old transit infrastructure," she noted aloud. It was a structural oversight, and Vane did not tolerate oversights. If the population remembered the unauthorized histories, the Committee's authority would dissolve into uncontrollable anarchy. The record belonged to whoever was strong enough to keep it stable.

She opened a secure channel to the loyalist sweep teams marshaling in the perimeter staging areas.

"Commander," Vane said, her tone devoid of panic but heavy with absolute authority. "The targets are descending through the sub-level exhaust vents. They are attempting to reach the Undercity."

"Understood, Director," the commander replied. "Shall we pursue through the shafts?"

"No," Vane said. "The shafts are a choke point. They know the terrain down there better than your units do. I want you to calculate their descent trajectory and establish a hard perimeter at the exit points. Deny them the Outskirts. Then deny them again."

She terminated the connection and looked back at the failing data streams. The archivist thought he had won by opening the vault. He was about to learn that exposing the truth was only the first step; surviving it was a different matter entirely. Vane would dismantle the foundation of his resistance before it could cure.
