# Chapter 4: The Black Site Protocol

## Outline

1. Elias and Kaelen discuss the implications of the batch protocol.
2. They identify a secondary black site from the data fragments.
3. They formulate a plan to infiltrate the site and gather hard evidence before the Committee erases them.

---

## Chapter Text

The chaotic hum of Kaelen's servers felt suffocating in the enclosed space. Elias stared at the decrypted manifest on the central monitor, his mind struggling to process the sheer volume of altered records. Thousands of citizen files, systematically overwritten in a single batch process.

"It's an assembly line," Elias murmured, his voice hollow. "They aren't targeting dissidents or threats. They're rewriting collective memory. Changing the baseline reality."

Kaelen didn't look up from the keyboard, her fingers continuing her frantic dance across the keys. "It's efficient. If you want to control the narrative, you don't argue with the past. You just delete it and install an update. The Oversight Committee has turned history into a software patch."

"We have to expose this," Elias said, the shock hardening into resolve. "If we dump this raw data to the public archives..."

"They'll isolate the breach and wipe the network before anyone can decrypt it," Kaelen interrupted, finally turning to face him. The pale light of the monitors cast sharp shadows across her face. "You saw the encryption matrix on that drive. It's proprietary Committee tech. Anyone who accesses this without clearance gets their neural link fried. We need physical proof. Something they can't just delete with a keystroke."

Elias rubbed his eyes, the chronic ache of the neural link flare returning. "The primary archives are heavily guarded. If we try to breach the Nexus, we'll be intercepted before we even reach the perimeter."

"I know," Kaelen said, a grim smile playing on her lips. She tapped a few keys, bringing up a fragmented schematic. "But this isn't the primary archive. The metadata on your drive referenced a routing node. A secondary facility where the batch protocol is tested before it goes live."

Elias leaned closer, studying the schematic. It depicted a subterranean structure, completely off the grid. "Where is this?"

"Sector Four. Below the old transit hub," Kaelen replied. "It's a black site. Minimal security footprint to avoid drawing attention, but heavily automated."

"We need to get inside," Elias said, tracing the layout of the facility on the screen. "If we can access the testing servers, we can download the unencrypted source code of the batch protocol. It would prove the modifications are artificial."

"It's a suicide run," Kaelen warned, though the gleam in her eyes suggested they were already calculating the odds. "We'll need to bypass the automated sentries, spoof the biometric scanners, and extract the data before the Committee's failsafes trigger a total purge."

"Then we better start planning," Elias said, his voice steady. He looked at the fragmented memory on the screen, the fabricated childhood of an unknown citizen. He couldn't save the past that had already been lost, but he could fight for the truth of tomorrow.
