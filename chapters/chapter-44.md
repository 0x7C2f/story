# Chapter 44

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek realize they are trapped by the automated security drone in the subterranean smuggler tunnels.
2. Jarek devises a plan to use the tunnel's failing architecture against the drone, while Kaelen provides a distraction.
3. They successfully evade the drone and plunge deeper into the tunnel network, moving closer to Sector Five while Director Vane's loyalists scramble above.

---

## Chapter Text

The sensor light swept across the damp, curved wall of the tunnel, a glaring yellow eye searching the darkness. The grinding mechanical roar of the automated security drone filled the narrow space, shaking the dust from the heavy, long-dead cables overhead.

Elias Thorne pressed his back against the slick concrete, his breathing shallow. The cracked screen of his wrist terminal pulsed faintly, a fragile tether to the sprawling mesh network they had just ignited above ground. They were trapped between a sealed, rusted hatch and an old-world killer that the Oversight Committee had reactivated.

"It is an old crawler model," Kaelen said, her voice barely a whisper. She kept her carbine leveled, though the weapon would be useless against the drone's heavy front armor. "Thermal sensors and sonic targeting. If we run, it will track our footsteps. If we shoot, it will lock onto the muzzle flash."

"Then we don't shoot," Jarek muttered, his eyes narrowing as he studied the tunnel's deteriorating architecture. The scavenger leader pointed up toward the ceiling, tracing the path of a particularly thick bundle of corroded conduit pipes running parallel to the wall. "This section of the sub-levels is structurally compromised. See those support stanchions?"

Elias squinted through the gloom. The steel stanchions bracing the tunnel roof were rusted through, buckling under the immense weight of the earth above. The drone’s heavy treads were already causing the floor to vibrate hard.

"We bring the roof down on it," Elias realized, the procedural logic forming.

"Not the whole roof, just enough to pin it," Jarek corrected. "But we need it to stop right under that weak point. It is moving too fast on patrol."

"I will give it a reason to stop," Kaelen said, shifting her stance. "When it locks onto me, you hit the stanchions. Make sure you don't miss, Jarek."

Before Elias could protest, Kaelen broke cover. She sprinted down the center of the tunnel, toward the advancing machine.

The drone’s sensor array snapped toward her, the glaring yellow light turning an angry, crimson red. A mechanized shriek echoed off the walls, the sonic targeting locking on. The crawler’s treads screeched as it halted its forward momentum, planting itself to deploy its automated turrets.

"Now!" Kaelen yelled, diving into a shallow alcove carved into the concrete.

Jarek stepped out, his pulse rifle raised. He didn't aim at the drone. He aimed at the corroded joint of the primary support stanchion above the machine. He fired a sustained burst, the heavy plasma rounds glowing a brilliant, blinding blue in the dark tunnel.

The joint shattered in a spray of molten slag. For a long, terrifying second, nothing happened. Then, the tunnel groaned.

A massive slab of concrete and earth tore loose from the ceiling, plummeting downward. It struck the top of the security drone with a deafening crash, crushing the crawler’s sensor array and turret mounts beneath tons of rock. A thick cloud of dust filled the corridor, choking the air.

Elias coughed, waving his arm to clear the particulate. The mechanical screech had died, replaced by the sputtering whine of a failing drive core beneath the rubble.

Kaelen emerged from her alcove, dusting concrete powder from her shoulders. "It is pinned," she said, eyeing the mound of debris. "But that noise will draw any loyalist patrols listening on the surface."

"Then we don't stay to chat," Jarek said, already moving past the wreckage. The path ahead was partially blocked by the cave-in, but there was enough clearance near the right wall to squeeze through. "The tunnel forks a klick ahead. It will take us closer to Sector Five, away from Vane's perimeter sweeps."

Elias followed them, stepping over the jagged chunks of rock. The throbbing in his head continued, though the immediate adrenaline surge sharpened his focus. They had survived the canyon, they had bypassed the loyalists, and they had beaten the tunnel's defenses.

He checked his wrist terminal once more. The mesh network was holding strong, the broadcast data still replicating across the lower sectors. The truth was out there, rippling through the city’s sanitized history.

"Let's move," Elias said, his voice steady. "Vane is losing control of the narrative. We need to make sure she loses everything else."
