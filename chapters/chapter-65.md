# Elias at Sector Four - Phase 65
## Outline

1. Elias triggers the physical failsafes of the automated turrets, blinding their sensors.
2. Jarek leads the charge, providing cover while Kaelen neutralizes the emplacements.
3. The vanguard breaches the inner checkpoint, but Elias realizes the security feed is still transmitting to the Spire.

## Chapter Text

As high-velocity rounds roared through the air, the Sector Four transit hub became a slaughterhouse of ricocheting shrapnel and shattered tile. Throwing himself flat against the rusted ironwork of a maintenance gantry, Elias felt sparks rain down on his heavy archivist coat while the first automated turret swept its fire across the vanguard.

"Cover!" Jarek bellowed over the mechanical shriek of the turrets. Instead of dropping, the massive enforcer lunged sideways to brace his armored shoulder against a thick ferrocrete pillar. Raising his heavy slug-thrower, he fired a three-round burst at the high gantry. The shots only sparked against the turret’s reinforced casing.

Kaelen slid into the shallow depression beside Elias, her chest heaving, the custom submachine gun clutched tightly in her hands. "Proximity tracking is active," she yelled over the din. "They aren't even aiming. They're just saturating the kill zone."

Staring at the portable interface deck slung across his chest, Elias watched the screen turn into a chaotic blur of red warnings and telemetry spikes. The turrets were isolated from the main mesh network because they operated on localized, mechanical logic.

"I can't slice them," Elias said, his voice clipped and precise, forcing down the panic threatening to close his throat. He keyed a new sequence, his fingers flying across the haptic board. "But they are still connected to the hub’s primary power relay. If I overload the telemetry loop, the physical failsafes should trigger. It will blind their optical sensors."

"Do it," Jarek shouted, returning fire. "We can't hold this position."

Elias closed his eyes for a split second, isolating the ambient roar of gunfire from the quiet, structured logic of the code. He found the feedback loop controlling the optical arrays. He didn't try to shut it down—he fed it garbage data, thousands of false targeting parameters generated from the mesh network.

The interface deck burned hot against his ribs. Above them, the high whine of the targeting servos pitched into a frantic, uneven squeal. The twin-barreled turrets jerked violently, their optics flashing a blinding, erratic strobe of white light as the failsafes slammed shut to protect the overloaded sensors.

The gunfire stopped. The sudden silence was absolute and terrifying.

"They're blind!" Elias shouted, scrambling out from his cover. "Move!"

Jarek charged out first, his heavy bounding strides eating up the distance to the central control booth beneath the gantries. Close on his heels came Kaelen, her movements as tight and precise as always. Bringing up the rear, Elias ran with his head low while wincing every time the portable rig slammed against his chest.

Around them, the vanguard surged forward in a chaotic wave of raised makeshift weapons. They rushed right past the blinded turrets, which could only swivel uselessly in the air above.

At the control booth, Kaelen completely ignored the interface terminal. She simply jammed the muzzle of her submachine gun against the magnetic lock and held the trigger until the door gave way with a metallic screech. After she slipped inside, the heavy thud of secondary charges echoed outward across the platform. High above on the gantries, the automated turrets slumped forward, their sensors going completely dark the moment she severed the hardlines.

Frowning, Elias traced the routing path on his console. "The hardlines are severed," he muttered to himself. "But there's a secondary ghost protocol running beneath the primary feed."

Kaelen stepped out of the booth and wiped soot from her cheek. "What are you seeing, archivist?"

Looking up, Elias let out a breath as dread settled heavy in his stomach. "The turrets were isolated, but the security telemetry wasn't," he explained. "Director Vane wasn't just watching this sector. She's using the combat data to map our interference pattern."

He met Jarek's eyes before turning his gaze to the massive, shadowed expanse of the inner transit arteries leading up toward the Spire. "She knows we're here. And she's mapping the blind spots we create."
