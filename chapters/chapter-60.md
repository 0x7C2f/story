# Elias at The Nexus Spire - Phase 60
## Outline

1. The descent through the auxiliary exhaust vent ends in the upper boundaries of the Undercity.
2. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek discover the initial deployments of Director Vane's loyalist perimeter.
3. They must cross a hazardous environmental bypass to slip through the dragnet.

---

## Chapter Text

The drop through the auxiliary exhaust shaft was a grueling descent through centuries of architectural decay. Elias clung to the rusted rungs, his muscles burning with the strain of holding his own weight. Below him, Jarek moved with steady, mechanical precision, a broad silhouette blocking the faint, chemical glow rising from the Undercity. Above, Kaelen's breathing was a harsh rasp, the only sound accompanying the constant drip of condensation.

The sterile chill of the Nexus Spire was gone. The air here was heavy, thick with the smell of stale ozone, synthetic rot, and unwashed metal.

"End of the line," Jarek grumbled. Reaching the bottom grate, he braced his boots against the shaft walls and shoved. With a screech of tortured metal, the heavy grating gave way, tumbling into the gloom below.

Elias dropped the last few feet, landing on a precarious catwalk that shuddered under his weight. He crouched, his hands gripping the slick railing. Below them lay the vast, chaotic expanse of the Undercity—a staggering vertical drop into a canyon of neon-lit alleys, precarious scaffolding, and the chaotic hum of unregulated generators.

Kaelen dropped beside him, her pulse pistol instantly snapping up to cover the immediate perimeter. "We made it."

"Not yet," Elias said, checking his wrist terminal. The screen flickered, struggling to parse the chaotic localized network, but the encrypted overlay Kaelen had installed painted a grim picture. A constellation of red markers was blooming along the primary transit junctions leading down into the lower sectors.

"They are already here," Elias whispered.

Jarek peered over the edge of the catwalk, his heavy brow furrowing. "Sweep teams. They bypassed the shafts and descended through the primary maintenance arteries. They are locking down the perimeter."

Elias looked down. Far below, the harsh, sweeping beams of tactical searchlights sliced through the perpetual fog of the Undercity. Mechanized units were cordoning off the main bridges, their heavy tread patterns audible even over the ambient roar of the sector. Director Vane was not trying to catch them; she was building a wall to keep them from disappearing into the deep.

"If we cannot go down the main arteries, we go through the structural gaps," Kaelen said, her eyes tracing the tangled mess of piping and exposed circuitry that clung to the Spire's underbelly. "The cooling lines. They run parallel to the old transit bridge."

"The cooling lines are a death trap," Jarek said, stating it as a fact, not a complaint. "They vent superheated plasma every cycle."

"It is better than walking into a loyalist firing line," Kaelen said. She looked at Elias. "Can you patch into the environmental controls? Give us a window?"

Elias stared at the labyrinth of pipes. His hands were still trembling from the exertion, but his mind engaged with the procedural challenge. He pulled a physical jack from his terminal and slotted it into a nearby, exposed junction box. The interface sparked, fighting the unauthorized connection.

"I cannot control the primary vents," Elias said, his voice tightening as he worked through the archaic code. "But I can desynchronize the secondary pressure valves. It will buy us maybe three minutes before the automated safeties override and purge the lines."

"Three minutes is an eternity," Jarek said. He adjusted his grip on his heavy rifle. "Do it."

Elias initiated the sequence. A deep, resonant groan shuddered through the metal beneath their feet as the pressure valves shifted. The intense heat radiating from the adjacent pipe network lessened marginally.

"Move," Elias said.

They scrambled off the catwalk, dropping onto the primary cooling line. The metal was still hot enough to scorch through their boots. Below them, a loyalist patrol marched across the main bridge, their searchlights sweeping the shadows just meters away. Elias held his breath, pressing himself flat against the curvature of the pipe as the beam passed overhead, missing them by inches.

They crawled forward. The countdown on Elias's terminal blinked rapidly. Two minutes. One minute. The pipe began to hum, a deep vibration signaling the building pressure.

"Almost there," Kaelen said, sliding off the pipe onto a rusted maintenance platform on the far side of the checkpoint.

Jarek followed, landing with a heavy thud. Elias scrambled the last few feet, diving onto the platform just as a deafening roar erupted behind him. The cooling line purged, a violent geyser of superheated steam obliterating the space they had just occupied.

The blast of heat washed over them, but they were through the perimeter. They had slipped the snare. Elias looked back at the wall of steam, knowing Vane would soon realize the gap in her net. They were in the Undercity, but the hunt had only just begun.
