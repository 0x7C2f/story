# Chapter 25: The Signal Repeater Strike

## Chapter Text

The interior of the primary distribution structure was a cavernous expanse of structural steel and humming machinery. It smelled of ozone and hot metal. Below them, on the main floor, the primary signal repeater pulsed with a dull, rhythmic light, its massive power core shielded by heavy ferrocrete housing. Enforcers patrolled the perimeter in tight, disciplined pairs, their armor gleaming under the arc lights.

Elias knelt on the rusted grating of the overhead catwalk, his breath catching in his throat. The interface ache throbbed at his temple, a sharp reminder of the encrypted handshake he had maintained since they breached the outer perimeter. "Four pairs of guards on the ground level," he whispered, his voice barely carrying over the drone of the machinery. "And two automated sentry turrets bracketing the core access port."

Kaelen crouched beside him, her pulse pistol drawn. She checked the angle of the turrets, her eyes narrowed. "I can blind the sensors on the turrets," she said, her tone clipped and analytical. "But it will only buy us thirty seconds before the secondary systems override."

"Thirty seconds is enough," Jarek said. The Scavenger leader rested the heavy satchel of EMP charges against his thigh. He looked down at the repeater core. "I drop down, plant the charges, and we pull back before the enforcers converge."

Elias mapped the timing, the variables cascading through his mind like corrupted code. It was a brutal, blunt-force equation, the kind of logic the Undercity ran on. "If we hit the core, the ensuing cascade will fry the local command network," Elias noted. "But Director Vane will immediately realize the staging ground has been compromised."

"She already knows we have the transport," Kaelen pointed out. "This isn't an infiltration anymore, Thorne. It's a strike. You want to stop the memory wipe, we have to tear out the transmission node."

Elias nodded slowly. He reached for his neural rig, prepping the sequence. "On your mark, Kaelen."

Kaelen raised her wrist monitor, tapping in the override commands she had pre-compiled. "Executing in three, two, one. Now."

The sentry turrets stuttered, their tracking lasers dimming as the spoofed commands flooded their logic gates.

Jarek moved with shocking speed for a man of his size. He swung over the catwalk railing, dropping the fifteen feet to the main floor. He hit the ferrocrete with a heavy, muted thud and immediately sprinted toward the housing of the repeater core.

"Intruder! Sector three!" one of the enforcers shouted, his voice amplified by his helmet comms.

Kaelen leaned over the railing, her pulse pistol barking sharply. Two enforcers went down in a shower of sparks as she laid down suppressing fire.

Elias kept his focus on the network, maintaining the spoofed handshake that prevented the staging ground's alarms from locking down the exits. He watched Jarek slap the EMP charges against the primary access port of the core housing, arming the detonators with sharp, precise movements.

"Charges set!" Jarek bellowed over the din of the firefight. He turned and sprinted toward a stack of munitions crates for cover.

Kaelen tapped the detonator override on her wrist.

The explosion was a concussive shockwave that rattled Elias's teeth and blew out the arc lights in the immediate vicinity. The primary signal repeater shrieked, a high-pitched mechanical wail, as the EMP cascaded through its systems. Blue arcs of rogue electricity danced across the ferrocrete housing, and then, the rhythmic pulsing light died.

"Core is offline," Kaelen said, dropping a fresh power cell into her pistol. "Let's move before they manually reset the security doors."

Elias scrambled back along the catwalk, following Kaelen toward the secondary access hatch they had used to enter. Jarek was already taking the stairs two at a time to meet them.

They burst out of the hatch and back into the chaotic, shadowed perimeter of the vehicle yard. Alarms were beginning to wail across the compound, a harsh, discordant blare that cut through the smog.

They sprinted toward their captured transport, weaving between the heavy crawlers. Jarek vaulted into the pilot's seat and gunned the engine before Elias and Kaelen had secured the side doors. The transport lurched forward, grinding gears as it tore out of the formation.

"Hold onto something," Jarek grunted, spinning the heavy steering yoke as they barreled toward the compromised western gate.

Elias fell back against the bulkhead, gasping for breath. The ache in his temple had flared into a blinding migraine. He pulled up the tactical feed from his rig, expecting to see the localized network collapse.

The feed initialized, but the red markers of the mobile transports did not fade.

"Kaelen," Elias said, his voice tight with panic. He stared at the projection, his blood running cold. "The core is destroyed, but the mobile repeaters are still broadcasting."

Kaelen turned to him, her expression hardening. "That's impossible. They needed the primary node to synchronize."

"They did," Elias said, quickly parsing the incoming data stream. "But Vane must have anticipated a strike. The moment the primary node went dark, the secondary transports initiated a localized failsafe. They are acting as a decentralized mesh network. They don't need the central hub anymore."

Jarek tore through the remaining barrier of the western gate, the transport shuddering violently as it cleared the perimeter and hit the uneven approach road. "What does that mean for us?" he shouted over the roar of the engine.

Elias looked at the map. The red markers were beginning to fan out, moving steadily toward the Outskirts.

"It means we didn't stop the wipe," Elias said quietly, the grim reality settling over him like ash. "We just forced them to deploy early."
