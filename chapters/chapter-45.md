# Chapter 45

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek emerge from the subterranean smuggler tunnels into the industrial sprawl of Sector Five.
2. They encounter a group of Sector Five factory workers who have seen the counter-broadcast and are in a state of confusion and rising anger.
3. Elias must decide whether to slip past unnoticed or recruit the workers to the resistance, ultimately choosing to reveal themselves and risk Vane's tracking to build their network.

---

## Chapter Text

The air in Sector Five tasted of sulfur and machine oil, a bitter contrast to the damp earth of the smuggler tunnels. Elias pushed his shoulders against the heavy grate, ignoring the burn in his exhausted muscles until the rusted iron gave way with a harsh screech.

He climbed out into the shadow of a colossal coolant tower. He hauled himself up, then turned to offer a hand to Kaelen and Jarek. The three of them crouched in the dim alleyway, the steady mechanical hum of the industrial sector vibrating through their boots.

Above them, the sky was a bruised twilight, choked by the smog of the heavy manufactories. But it was the noise that drew Elias's attention. The usual rhythmic thrum of the automated assembly lines was broken by shouting.

"We made it," Jarek muttered, wiping grime from his face. "Sector Five. Vane's perimeter sweeps won't expect us this deep in the industrial zone, not yet."

Kaelen unslung her carbine, peering around the curve of the coolant tower. "They might not need perimeter sweeps. Listen."

Elias moved up beside her, following her gaze down the narrow corridor between two factory blocs. At the end of the alley, where it opened into a wider transit plaza, a crowd was gathering. They were factory workers, dressed in the heavy canvas jumpsuits of the lower tiers. They weren't moving toward the assembly lines for the shift change. They were clustered around a flickering public terminal.

The screen was playing the counter-broadcast on loop.

"They saw it," Elias said, the realization settling over him. The truth he had pushed through the mesh network was taking root. "They're seeing the unaltered history."

The crowd's murmurs grew louder. The confusion hardened into a sharp, jagged anger. A man near the front pointed at the screen and shouted. Elias couldn't make out the words over the ambient noise, but the raw fury in the gesture was clear.

"We should keep moving," Jarek said, his hand resting near his sidearm. "Before that crowd turns into a riot, or before Committee enforcers show up to put it down. We need to find a secure relay point to boost the signal further."

"If we run now, we leave them blind," Elias countered. He stared at the angry faces lit by the harsh glare of the terminal. "They know they have been lied to, but they do not know what to do next. The Committee will crush them if they riot without direction."

"Elias," Kaelen warned, her tone sharp. "If you step out there, Vane's surveillance will flag you in seconds. The drone in the tunnels was just a scout. She will send hunter teams."

"If we don't build a network here, the broadcast dies with us," Elias said. The throbbing in his head, a lingering remnant of the Nexus Spire dive, pulsed in time with his racing heartbeat. He stepped out from the shadow of the coolant tower, out into the alleyway.

"You're a fool, archivist," Jarek grumbled, but he didn't raise his weapon to stop him.

Elias walked toward the plaza. The heavy canvas and steel of Sector Five pressed in around him. He needed them to fight, but more than that, he needed them to remember.
