# Chapter 26

The heavy transport surged away from the Sector Six staging ground, its tires gripping the fractured ferrocrete. Inside the cabin, the atmosphere was suffocating. Elias stared at the tactical projection hovering over his console, the red markers of the mobile transports fanning out in a coordinated, predatory formation.

"They're not just deploying early," Elias said, his voice tight. The interface ache throbbed relentlessly against his skull. "They're moving in a dispersed grid. The failsafe was designed to operate without the central node."

Jarek ground the gears, coaxing every ounce of speed from the engine. "How much time before they hit the perimeter of the Outskirts?" he asked, his deep rumble barely audible over the engine's roar.

"Thirty minutes, maybe less," Elias calculated, watching the telemetry update in real-time. "Once they overlap their localized broadcast fields, the wipe will be absolute. It will rewrite everyone in the settlement."

Kaelen braced herself against the bulkhead, her expression hardened. "We have the mesh network back at the settlement. The salvaged repeaters. Can they shield against the localized fields?"

Elias parsed the data, searching the technical specifications he had stripped from the Committee's network. "The mesh network isn't a shield. It's a transmission platform. If the mobile transports begin broadcasting, their signal strength will overwhelm our setup." He paused, his mind racing through procedural logic. "Unless we broadcast first."

"A counter-signal?" Kaelen asked, her eyes narrowing.

"A preemptive overwrite," Elias confirmed. "If we push the uncorrupted memory files—the actual, unaltered history—through our mesh network at maximum gain, we might create enough interference to disrupt their localized fields. It would fracture the synchronization."

Jarek glanced in the rearview monitor. The sky above the staging ground was thick with smog and the distant, harsh glare of arc lights. "And what happens to the transports when their synchronization fractures?"

"The protocol fails," Elias said. "The mobile units will isolate themselves to prevent corruption. They'll shut down."

"It's a gamble," Kaelen pointed out. "If the mesh network fails to match their output, the settlement is erased."

"It's the only option we have left," Elias said. He decoupled his primary tether, shutting down the projection. The sudden absence of the neural feed left him dizzy. "Jarek, we need to beat those transports to the settlement. We have to initialize the mesh before they overlap the perimeter."

Jarek didn't reply. He simply pressed the accelerator flat against the floorboards, the transport careening through the desolate, ash-choked expanse of the Dead Zone.

The archivist leaned back in his seat, his mind running the calculations again and again. The margins were razor-thin. They were racing against an automated protocol designed by the best engineers the Oversight Committee had. But Director Vane had made a miscalculation. She assumed the resistance would fight with ordnance and attrition. She hadn't anticipated an archivist turning her own tools against her.

Elias closed his eyes, preparing for the strain of the coming synchronization. The real war wasn't in the staging ground. It was in the network.
