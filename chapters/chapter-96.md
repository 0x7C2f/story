## Chapter Text

The neural feedback from the ancient terminal felt like chewing on tin foil, but Elias held the connection. The mag-lev train's rusted chassis hummed, a low vibration that shook loose decades of dust from the ceiling of the transit tunnel.

"We have power," Elias muttered, his voice barely carrying over the waking machinery. "It's a localized grid, isolated from the Committee's main network."

Kaelen braced himself against the cracked doorframe of the passenger car. "Can you get it moving?" he asked, his rifle tracking the darkness of the tunnel behind them.

"I am trying," Elias said. He tapped the haptic keyboard projected over his wrist, bypassing a rusted physical throttle. "The startup sequence requires a manual override of the magnetic dampeners." He pressed his fingers against his temples, ignoring the dull ache that had settled behind his eyes.

Jarek stood watch near the rear of the train, his imposing figure silhouetted against the dim emergency lights. "The water level is still rising in the lower tunnels," Jarek called out. "Vane's sweeps will push the flooding this way soon."

Elias focused on the code cascading across his vision. The ancient system was brittle, prone to cascading failures if pushed too hard. He fed a raw command directly into the drive manifold. A screech of metal on metal echoed through the tunnel as the train lurched forward, then slammed back, magnetic locks refusing to disengage.

"It's fighting me," Elias whispered. He ran a diagnostic, the data spooling out in fragmented streams. "The secondary locks are mechanically fused. I can't release them from the terminal."

"Show me," Kaelen said, moving closer.

"Undercarriage, rear axel," Elias replied, his focus fixed on keeping the primary systems from overloading. "There's a manual release lever. It's likely rusted shut."

Jarek didn't hesitate. He dropped down and slid under the train, disappearing into the dark gap between the tracks. "I see it," Jarek's voice echoed up, muffled and strained.

Elias watched the power levels spike on his rig. The longer the train strained against the locks, the more likely it was to short out the isolated grid to a halt. "Jarek, we have thirty seconds before the primary relay trips."

"I am hitting it," Jarek grunted. A dull clang clanged from the undercarriage, followed by a shower of sparks. "It's jammed tight."

Elias tapped his wrist terminal, initiating a forced reboot of the magnetic field. "I am going to pulse the dampeners," he called out. "It will be violent. Get clear, Jarek."

"Do it," Jarek yelled, sliding back out from under the train.

Elias executed the command. The train shuddered violently, throwing Elias against the terminal console. A deafening crack echoed as the rusted locks snapped under the sudden magnetic surge. The train immediately lurched forward, the momentum throwing Kaelen against the bulkhead.

"We are loose," Elias gasped, pushing himself upright and scrambling to stabilize the throttle control. The train began to glide along the ancient tracks, the acceleration pressing them all back.

"Good," Kaelen said, recovering his balance. "How far does this track go?"

"The schematics suggest it connects to a maintenance hub beneath Sector Six," Elias said, checking the navigation readout. "If the rails are intact, we should bypass Vane's perimeter completely."

The train plunged deeper into the unlit tunnel, leaving the sounds of the flooded depths behind them. Elias didn't relax. The ache in his temples throbbed in time with the rhythmic clatter of the tracks. They were moving, but the destination was just another unknown.
