# Elias at Sector Five - Phase 46
## Outline

1. Elias rallies the factory workers in Sector Five. He reveals the truth about the altered memories and gives them a shared purpose.
2. Jarek and Kaelen help organize the workers into a functional resistance cell. They use their knowledge of the industrial sprawl to set up defensive perimeters.
3. The workers mobilize. Committee enforcer drones breach the sector's edge, which signals Director Vane's swift and violent response to the counter-broadcast.

---

## Chapter Text

The transit plaza in Sector Five was a canyon of rusted steel and flickering neon. It was packed tight with bodies in heavy canvas jumpsuits. The public terminal at the far end continued its loop. It cast a stark, harsh white light over the faces of the factory workers. The counter-broadcast played again and again—the raw, unaltered footage of the Sector Two uprising that the Oversight Committee had tried to bury.

Elias Thorne stood near the base of the terminal, and the low hum of the industrial sprawl vibrated against his boots. The crowd was a powder keg. The jagged edges of their confusion sharpened into rage. They had lived their whole lives on the digital feeds, and now the fundamental architecture of their reality had fractured.

"They are going to tear this place apart," Jarek said, his gravelly voice barely carrying over the rising clamor. He stood close to Elias, one hand resting on the grip of his sidearm. "And when they do, the Committee will send the hammer down. We need to channel this."

Elias nodded, and he rubbed his temples to soothe the persistent ache left over from his extraction run in the Nexus Spire. He stepped up onto a raised concrete partition and turned to face the crowd. He didn't have a megaphone, but the sheer tension in the plaza commanded a sudden, fragile silence from the nearest ranks.

"You are seeing the truth!" Elias shouted. His voice cracked slightly before it found its procedural cadence. "The Oversight Committee told you Sector Two was a localized containment failure. They told you it was a disease. But you are seeing it now. It was a massacre."

The murmur rippled outward when those closer relayed his words to the back of the plaza, a wave of realization crashing over the crowd.

"They did not just take your families," Elias continued. He leaned forward, the exhaustion in his muscles momentarily forgotten. "They took the memory of them. They are rewriting our history to keep you compliant. To keep you working these lines while they build their perfect order."

A worker near the front, his face streaked with machine oil, stepped forward. "Who are you? How did you get this footage?"

"I am an archivist," Elias said, the title feeling strange on his tongue now. "I was inside the Spire. I saw the batch manifest. They are planning a city-wide synchronization. If they succeed, everything you remember, everything you love, will belong to them. They will overwrite you."

"Let them try," the worker spat. He turned back to the crowd. "We burn the factories. We burn the lines!"

A roar of agreement surged through the plaza. Elias saw the violence sparking, wild and untethered. It was exactly what Director Vane wanted. A chaotic riot would give her the justification to deploy heavy suppression teams and wipe Sector Five off the grid.

"No!" Kaelen’s voice cut through the noise, sharp and commanding. She vaulted onto the partition beside Elias, her carbine slung over her shoulder, her stance aggressive. "If you burn the factories, you burn your own cover. You make yourselves targets for the gunships. You don't fight a machine by breaking your own tools."

The crowd hesitated, the raw energy faltering under her cold, cynical authority.

"She is right," Jarek added. He stepped up beside them. He looked over the sea of faces, his eyes calculating the odds. "You know these sub-levels. You know the ventilation shafts, the coolant lines, the smuggler tunnels. The Committee forces do not. We don't fight them in the open. We make them bleed for every inch of Sector Five."

Elias watched the shift in the crowd. The wild fury was still there, but Kaelen and Jarek were giving it a shape. They were turning a mob into a resistance.

"We need barricades at the primary transit hubs," Kaelen ordered. She pointed at the main arteries leading into the sector. "Weld the blast doors shut. Cut the localized mesh feeds so they can't track your biometric tags."

"And the heavy lifters?" a voice called out from the middle of the pack. "Can we re-purpose the industrial bots?"

"If you can bypass their inhibitor chips," Jarek said, a grim smile touching his lips. "Do it. Use the slag from the foundries. Turn this place into a fortress."

The workers scattered to their tasks, and their chaotic energy sharpened into a frantic but coordinated defense. Small units rushed toward the assembly lines and transit corridors, desperate to hold the line. Elias stepped down from the partition, and the tension in his chest finally eased. They had built a physical network.

Then, the proximity alarm on Kaelen’s wrist terminal shrieked.

She snapped her wrist up. Her eyes narrowed as she read the flickering data feed. "We are out of time."

Elias moved toward her, the hope turning to ash in his mouth. "What is it?"

"Thermal signatures," Kaelen said. Her voice clipped. Efficiency overrode everything else. "Heavy armor. Enforcer drones are breaching the perimeter at Checkpoint Charlie. They aren't waiting for the riot to start."

"Vane is sending the hammer," Jarek muttered. He racked the slide of his sidearm.

Elias looked back down the narrow alley they had just emerged from. The fight had found them. There was no running back into the dark. He drew his own weapon, and the cold steel grounded him.

"Then we make them bleed," Elias said.
