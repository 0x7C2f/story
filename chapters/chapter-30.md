# Chapter 30: The Diversion

The captured heavy transport idled in the center of the Scrapyard Settlement's trading plaza, its massive engine blocks humming with a low, predatory vibration. Exhaust vented into the chilled Outskirts air, mingling with the persistent smog and the scent of ozone left over from the counter-broadcast.

Elias Thorne stood beside the open cargo ramp, adjusting the straps on his tactical vest. The lingering migraine still throbbed at his temples, a dull, rhythmic ache that flared every time he blinked too quickly. He tapped his wrist terminal, reviewing the spoofed transponder codes Kaelen had uploaded into the transport's primary system.

"The handshake protocols are holding," Elias reported, his voice tight but steady. "We are still broadcasting as Patrol Unit Seven. As long as Vane's command structure remains fractured, the automated checkpoints at the Spire shouldn't flag the discrepancy."

Kaelen emerged from the transport's dimly lit interior, carrying a satchel of high-yield EMP charges salvaged from the Sector Six assault. She checked the seals on the explosives with practiced efficiency before slinging the satchel over her shoulder.

"The Spire's internal security won't rely on the external network," she noted, her gaze sharp as she looked over the settlement. "Once we breach the perimeter, the spoofed transponder won't save us. We will have to fight our way to the data core."

"I know," Elias said. "But the goal isn't just to survive. It's to lock down the central repository. If we sever Vane's access to the primary archives, she loses the ability to fabricate history. The Committee becomes blind."

Jarek approached them through the swirling exhaust. The massive Scavenger leader wore a heavily modified enforcer jacket, his broad shoulders squared against the impending assault. Behind him, the settlement was a hive of frantic activity. Scavengers were reinforcing the mesh barricades, distributing salvaged munitions, and fortifying the choke points leading into the plaza.

"The perimeter sensors are picking up movement on the primary access roads," Jarek rumbled, his voice carrying easily over the roar of the transport's engine. "Heavy armor. Sweep teams. Vane isn't holding anything back. She wants us erased before the city realizes what happened."

Elias met Jarek's steady gaze. The archivist felt a pang of guilt, a heavy weight settling in his chest. He was leaving these people to fight a war he had brought to their doorstep.

"Jarek," Elias began, but the Scavenger raised a massive hand to stop him.

"Don't," Jarek said simply. "We aren't fighting for you, Thorne. We are fighting because we finally remember what they took from us. They tried to rewrite our existence. Today, we remind them that we are still here."

Kaelen stepped forward, offering Jarek a brief, respectful nod. "Keep them focused on the plaza. Draw the heavy armor into the narrow alleys where they can't maneuver. Buy us as much time as you can."

"We will hold the line," Jarek promised, his expression grim. He looked at the heavy transport, then back at Elias. "Just make sure you finish it. If we fall here, and you fail at the Spire... there won't be anyone left to remember the truth."

"I will finish it," Elias said. The words tasted like iron, but they were true. The archivist was gone. Only the saboteur remained.

Elias turned and walked up the cargo ramp, the metal grating echoing under his boots. Kaelen followed, hitting the retraction release. The heavy ramp groaned as it lifted, sealing them inside the transport's armored shell.

Through the narrow viewport, Elias watched Jarek turn back toward his people, raising his slug-thrower high. The Scavengers roared, a sound of defiance that cut through the engine noise.

"Take us out," Elias said, uncoupling his primary tether and letting the tactical display fade from his vision.

Kaelen engaged the drive system, and the heavy transport surged forward, leaving the settlement behind and plunging into the shadows of the Dead Zone. The Spire awaited.
