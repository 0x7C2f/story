# Elias at The Undercity - Phase 62

## Chapter Text

The descent into the lower levels of the Undercity was a suffocating crawl. The deeper they went, the heavier the air felt, thick with the smell of stagnant water and ozone. Elias moved with a deliberate rhythm, his hand tracing the damp, rusted walls as Kaelen's light guided them through the maze of abandoned maintenance tunnels.

"Watch your step," Jarek murmured, his voice a low gravel rumble in the enclosed space. "The grating here is compromised."

Elias stepped over a jagged hole in the walkway, the black abyss below whispering with the sound of rushing runoff. They were close. The structural schematics Elias had committed to memory indicated a primary junction box just ahead, a relic of the city's original, hardwired infrastructure.

"There," Elias said, pointing toward a hulking metal cabinet fused to the bulkhead by decades of synthetic mold and rust.

Kaelen swept her light over it. "You are certain this line is active?"

"It has to be," Elias replied, shrugging off his pack. "The Undercity's redundant systems never fully shut down. If I can splice into the trunk, I can bypass the wireless interference and establish a clean link to the dark mesh."

He pulled a heavy plasma torch from his kit. The intense blue flame sheared through the corroded locks of the junction box. Inside, thick bundles of fiber-optic cables lay dormant, coated in dust. Elias retrieved a splicing rig and went to work. His fingers moved with practiced precision despite the tremor of exhaustion in his hands. Isolating a primary data line, he stripped the protective sheathing and clamped the rig into place.

Connecting the stolen drive to his terminal, he plugged the rig's output into the auxiliary port. The screen lit up instantly, the erratic static replaced by a steady, pulsing stream of raw data.

"We have a connection," Elias said, his breath catching in his throat. He engaged the localized decryption algorithm. The cryptographic armor that had thwarted him began to fracture, the shifting keys locking into place under the brute-force processing power of the hardwired link.

Line by line, the encrypted files spilled open. The terminal projected a dense, holographic overlay of schematics, manifest logs, and internal communications from the Oversight Committee.

Kaelen leaned closer, her eyes scanning the scrolling text. "What are we looking at, Elias?"

"It is not just a purge," Elias whispered, the realization settling like ice in his chest. "I thought Director Vane was simply trying to erase the history of the dissidents. But this... this is a mass rewrite protocol."

He brought up a complex diagram of the Nexus Spire's broadcast architecture. The primary transmission arrays were highlighted in harsh crimson, their coverage overlapping to blanket the entire city.

"The Spire," Elias continued, his voice tight. "It is not just an archive. It is a transmitter. Vane intends to use the core infrastructure to broadcast a localized neural update to every citizen connected to the grid."

"A localized update?" Jarek asked.

"A forced memory replacement," Elias clarified. "She is not just erasing the rebellion. She is going to overwrite the city's collective memory of it. Anyone who remembers the uprising, anyone who harbored a doubt about the Committee—their minds will be scrubbed and replaced with a sterilized version of events."

Kaelen cursed softly. "She is going to lobotomize the city."

"Worse," Elias said, staring at the projected data. "She is going to make them believe they chose it."

The silence in the cramped tunnel was absolute, save for the distant rush of water. The sheer scale of Vane's ambition was staggering. They had stolen the data hoping for an advantage, for a weakness to exploit. Instead, they had uncovered a ticking clock.

"When does the protocol initiate?" Kaelen asked, her tone shifting from cynical to sharply pragmatic.

Elias checked the sequencing timelines. "The preliminary staging is already complete. The inner guard was not just pulled to Sector Five to hunt us—they were moved to secure the perimeter for the broadcast sequence. The system needs forty-eight hours to cycle the deep-archive drives for the rewrite."

"Then we have two days," Kaelen stated, stating the odds as a fact.

"Two days to stop a broadcast that originates from the most heavily fortified structure in the city," Jarek rumbled.

Elias closed the projection and disconnected his terminal. The drive was secure, but the knowledge it contained weighed heavier than the metal casing. The memory archivist had spent his life preserving the past. Now, he had forty-eight hours to save the future from being erased.

"We need a new plan," Elias said, his procedural focus hardening into cold resolve. "And we need it now."
