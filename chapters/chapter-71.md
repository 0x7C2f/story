# Elias at Sector Four - Phase 71
## Outline

1. Inside the abandoned substation, Elias successfully connects a bypass interface to the central router, accessing the mesh network.
2. Kaelen and Jarek secure the perimeter as Elias initiates the counter-signal designed to combat Vane's mass rewrite protocol.
3. An unexpected system defense protocol activates, forcing Elias into a perilous digital battle to keep the connection alive while Vane’s trackers start zeroing in.

---

## Chapter Text

The heavy blast door of the relay substation groaned as Jarek heaved it shut behind them, sealing out the frigid draft of the fracture zone. The interior was a cavernous square of rust and shadows, illuminated only by the faint, pulsing amber of standby diodes along the server racks. It smelled of ozone and decades of stale, recirculated air.

Elias did not pause to catch his breath. The exhaustion that had almost dragged him into the abyss outside was temporarily overridden by the procedural focus that seized him whenever a terminal was in sight. He knelt before the primary access panel, his fingers moving with practiced, if slightly trembling, precision.

"The grid draw is stable," Elias muttered, his voice echoing flat in the enclosed space. "It's an older foundational layer architecture. Pre-Committee. That means fewer active monitoring daemons, but the physical bypass is going to take a minute."

"You have three," Kaelen stated. She was already moving, her submachine gun sweeping the dark corners of the substation before she took up a position near the sealed door. "If Vane is accelerating the rewrite protocol, every second we spend offline is a second she gains."

Jarek moved to the opposite side of the room, his massive form silhouetted against the amber light. "The door will hold against small arms. Anything heavier, and we are in a cage."

Elias stripped off his heavy coat, the garment too cumbersome for the delicate splicing required. He pulled a length of copper wire from his kit and began bridging the corroded connections on the board. The lack of his primary deck was a phantom limb; he had to rely on a secondary wrist terminal and the substation's archaic interface.

"Bridging the auxiliary power," Elias narrated under his breath. "Bypassing the primary lock. And... handshake."

A sharp hum vibrated through the floorboards as the main console flared to life, casting a harsh blue glow across Elias's face. Lines of raw, uncompiled code began cascading down the primary monitor.

"I'm in," Elias announced. "I have access to the decentralized mesh network. The mobile repeaters are still active in Sector Six, but they're fractured. I need to route the counter-signal through this substation and force them back into synchronization."

He began typing fast on the haptic keyboard, his fingers flying over the keys as he initiated the sequence. The terminal screen flickered, the blue light reflecting in his eyes as he watched the progress bar inch forward.

"Counter-signal compiling. Preparing to inject into the central router."

"Do it," Kaelen urged, her eyes fixed on the door.

Elias hit the execute command. For a moment, the room was silent save for the hum of the servers. Then, the console flashed violently, the blue light shifting to a stark, warning crimson.

A low, mechanical whine built up in the room as the terminal speakers cracked to life with a burst of static.

"Access denied. Unauthorized intrusion detected. Initiating counter-measures."

Elias's hands froze over the keyboard. "That's not a standard daemon." He leaned closer to the screen, his eyes scanning the fast shifting code. "It's an active defense protocol. A hunter-killer algorithm."

"Vane," Jarek rumbled, stepping away from the wall.

"She predicted the counter-move," Elias said, his voice tightening. "The moment I injected the signal, it flagged our location. It's not just blocking the counter-broadcast; it's actively tracing the connection back to this substation."

"Can you cut it?" Kaelen demanded, her weapon raised.

"If I cut the connection, the counter-signal dies," Elias replied, his fingers flying across the keys as he attempted to establish a firewall. "I have to fight it off long enough for the broadcast to synchronize. But the longer I stay connected, the closer she gets to pinpointing us."

The crimson light pulsed faster, mirroring the frantic pounding of Elias's heart. He was no longer just a memory archivist preserving the past; he was locked in a digital war of attrition for the future of the city's mind, and the enemy was already at the gates.
