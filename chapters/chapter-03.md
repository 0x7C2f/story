# Chapter 3: The Undercity Meeting

## Outline

1. Elias meets his contact Kaelen in the Undercity.
2. Kaelen analyzes the drive, confirming that the Oversight Committee is not just deleting memories, but actively rewriting history on an industrial scale.
3. This forces Elias to confront the enormity of the conspiracy and commit to exposing it.

---

## Chapter Text

Elias waited in the shadows of a defunct reclamation plant, the damp air thick with the smell of ozone and decaying synthetics. The hum of the Undercity was a constant, low thrum, far removed from the pristine silence of the Nexus Archives. He checked his wrist terminal again. Two minutes late.

"You're broadcasting your anxiety, Thorne," a sharp voice echoed from the metal rafters above.

Elias flinched as Kaelen dropped down onto the grating with practiced ease, her boots making barely a sound. Kaelen was swathed in layers of dull, protective fabric, the slight metallic ridges of neural implants visible along her neck. She did not offer a greeting, instead eyeing Elias with a mix of suspicion and weary resignation.

"You look terrible," Kaelen observed, tapping her fingers against her thigh in rapid succession. "And you brought heat. The upper network is buzzing with priority alerts. What did you pull?"

Elias didn't waste time. He reached into his coat and produced the physical drive. Kaelen's eyes darted to it, her tapping fingers stopping.

"Untraceable hardware," Kaelen muttered, stepping closer to inspect the small, dark rectangle without touching it. "Old school. That's a severe breach of protocol for an archivist."

"It's a memory file," Elias said, his voice tight. "Designation M-774-B. Standard childhood recollection on the surface. But underneath, there's static. A splice."

Kaelen scoffed, crossing her arms. "A splice? You can't splice a memory without breaking the subject's mind. It's cryptographic suicide."

"I saw it, Kaelen. A black-site modification lab. And one of the Oversight Committee directors was there."

Kaelen went perfectly still. The ambient noise of the plant seemed to fade. Slowly, she extended a hand and took the drive, treating it as if it might detonate. "Follow me," Kaelen ordered, turning toward a hidden access panel in the rusted bulkhead. "We need to get this on a clean rig."

They navigated a labyrinth of narrow corridors, eventually arriving at Kaelen's hidden sanctuary. It was a chaotic mess of jury-rigged servers and scavenged displays, lit by the harsh glare of raw data streams. Kaelen slotted the drive into a reinforced console and cracked her knuckles.

"Alright," Kaelen said, her fingers flying across a mismatched keyboard. "Let's see what you stumbled into. Bypassing the primary visual layer... cracking the secondary encryption matrix..."

The monitors flared, projecting the same violent static Elias had seen earlier. But Kaelen didn't stop. They fed the signal through a series of brute-force decoders, isolating the anomalies.

"This code," Kaelen whispered, leaning close to the screen, the pale light reflecting in her wide eyes. "It's not just a clumsy overwrite. Look at the architecture. It's systematic. They're using the subject's own neural pathways to weave the fabrication into reality."

"They're rewriting history," Elias said, the truth settling heavy in his chest. "Not just deleting it. Replacing it."

Kaelen hit a final keystroke, bringing the hidden image into sharp focus. The holding facility, the containment chairs, the Oversight director.

"Thorne," Kaelen said, her voice devoid of its usual cynical edge. "This isn't an isolated incident. The timestamp metadata... it references a batch protocol. They're doing this on an industrial scale. We aren't just looking at a cover-up. We're looking at a factory."

Elias stared at the screen, the magnitude of the conspiracy washing over him. The history of New Providence was a lie, curated and controlled from the shadows. And now, he was the only archivist who knew the truth.
