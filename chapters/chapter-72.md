# Chapter 72

## Outline

1. Elias struggles to maintain the counter-signal against the hunter-killer protocol.
2. The hunter-killer protocol traces the source of the counter-signal to Sector Four.
3. Kaelen and Jarek prepare to defend the substation.

## Chapter Text

The crimson warning light on the console bathed Elias's face in a sickly glow, flashing in sync with the rising mechanical whine of the terminal. The hunter-killer algorithm was not just a blunt instrument; it was a sophisticated, adaptive defense protocol designed to isolate and sever unauthorized connections before they could embed into the central router.

"It's trying to cut the handshake," Elias murmured, his fingers dancing across the haptic keyboard. "If it severs the connection, the counter-signal dies before it can synchronize the mobile repeaters."

"How much time?" Kaelen asked, her voice sharp and steady as she moved to the blast door, peering through the small, reinforced viewing slit.

"I don't know," Elias replied, the exhaustion in his limbs momentarily overridden by pure, panicked adrenaline. He pulled up a secondary diagnostic overlay, watching the red lines of Vane's code slice through his makeshift firewall. "It's mapping our interference pattern. It's tracing the connection."

Jarek stepped away from the wall, his heavy boots thudding against the metal grate floor. He unslung his heavy rifle and checked the chamber. "If it traces us, they will send sweeps. We are trapped in a cage."

Elias gritted his teeth, shifting his focus to a string of hexadecimal commands. He needed to create a loop—a false digital trail that would force the hunter-killer protocol to chase its own tail long enough for the counter-broadcast to take hold in the mesh network. He began writing a recursive script, splicing it into the foundational layer's auxiliary power management subsystem.

"Come on," Elias whispered, treating the console as an adversary. "Take the bait."

The terminal shrieked a burst of static, the monitor flickering wildly. The progress bar for the counter-signal synchronization hovered at forty percent. It was moving too slowly. The antiquated hardware of the relay substation was bottlenecking the transmission.

"They're coming," Kaelen announced suddenly, stepping back from the door. "I see movement in the fracture zone. Tactical lights. Fast approach."

"Loyalists?" Jarek asked, moving to flank the entrance.

"Has to be," Kaelen said, raising her submachine gun. "They must have deployed as soon as the trace started."

Elias felt a cold knot form in his stomach. The trace hadn't just flagged their location; it had summoned the executioners. He hammered the execute key on the recursive script, praying it would buy them the seconds they needed.

"I need sixty seconds!" Elias yelled, his eyes glued to the synchronization bar. "Just hold them off for sixty seconds!"

"We will hold the door," Jarek rumbled, his massive form planting itself like a pillar of ferrocrete beside the entrance. "Do not stop until the signal is sent."

The heavy blast door reverberated with a sudden, deafening impact, dust and rust raining down from the ceiling. The siege had begun.
