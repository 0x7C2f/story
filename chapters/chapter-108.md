# Chapter 108

## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek move through the lightless Undercity and discover an old pre-Committee bunker.
2. Vane deploys analogue sweep teams into the Undercity, operating without network support.

---

## Chapter Text

The descent felt endless. Without the low, ever-present hum of the Spire's data network, the Undercity was a tomb. Elias Thorne gripped the rusted rungs of the maintenance ladder, his muscles trembling from exhaustion. Above them, the severed vault was a sealed box; below, there was only the oppressive, stagnant dark of forgotten sectors.

"Keep moving," Kaelen rasped from below. Even her voice sounded different without the subtle electronic resonance of the active city grid above them. "They will not stay blind forever. Vane is too paranoid for that."

Jarek brought up the rear, his heavy boots echoing dully in the shaft. "This is old infrastructure. Pre-Committee. The network does not reach down here, and neither does the light."

Elias reached the bottom, his boots splashing into shallow, freezing water. He activated the small, localized lumen on his wrist rig. The beam cut weakly through the gloom, revealing a sprawling cavern of concrete and twisted metal. It was a graveyard of the city that used to be.

"We need to find a defensible position," Elias whispered, the quiet of the space demanding it. "A place to rest before we figure out our next move."

He scanned the immediate area. His rig, disconnected from the central Spire, was now just a localized tool. But even without the network, he recognized the architectural footprint of an old civil defense bunker partially buried under a collapsed walkway.

"There," Elias said, pointing the lumen toward the reinforced steel door.

Far above them, in the sterile command center of Sector Vane, Director Vane stood perfectly still before a wall of dark monitors. The silence in the room was absolute, a stark contrast to the usual frantic energy of a crisis.

"The primary trunks are severed," a technician reported, his voice tight with fear. "We have lost all telemetry in the lower sectors. The purge protocol failed."

Vane did not blink. She did not raise her voice. "History is a maintenance schedule," she murmured, her tone cold and precise. "And someone has disrupted the schedule."

She turned away from the dead screens. "The automated sentries are blind. The tracking grid is dead. We revert to analogue protocols. Deploy the sweep teams manually. Arm them with kinetic weapons and localized comms. I want every inch of the Undercity searched by hand. Deny him the shadows."

Her orders were absolute. She would not lose control, not even in the dark.
