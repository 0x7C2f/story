# Chapter 98

## Outline

1. Opening beat: From their hidden vantage point in the access shaft, Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek finalize their plan to breach the central distribution hub amidst the heavy enforcer presence.
2. Escalation: Jarek triggers an explosion in one of the transport refueling lines, creating chaos. Kaelen provides cover fire while Elias sprints across the bright yard toward the hub.
3. Turn or decision: Elias reaches the reinforced door of the hub just as a heavy sentry spots him. He realizes he cannot pick the lock in time and must override it forcefully with his neural rig, risking detection by Vane.

## Chapter Text

Elias crouched in the shadows of the narrow access shaft, the cold metal grating biting into his knees. Through the slats, the Sector Six staging ground spread out before them like a fortified canyon of ferrocrete and blinding arc lights. Dozens of Committee enforcers moved with clipped, mechanical precision, marshaling heavy transports equipped with massive signal repeaters. The low thrum of idling engines vibrated through Elias's boots.

"We cannot just walk across," Kaelen whispered, her eyes tracking a squad of enforcers patrolling the perimeter. "They have intersecting firing lanes covering every approach to the hub."

Jarek shifted his massive frame, pulling a heavy, cylindrical charge from his belt. It was cobbled together from scrap and volatile plasma cells salvaged from the Undercity. "We do not walk. We make them look away." He pointed toward a cluster of heavy fuel lines running along the eastern barricade, feeding the transports. "I set this off there. It will draw their attention and their fire."

Elias looked at the central distribution hub, a squat, reinforced structure dominating the yard. "It will be tight. Once the charge blows, they will scramble. But Vane's forces are disciplined. The gap will only last a few seconds."

"A few seconds is all you get," Kaelen said, checking the power cell in her rifle. "I will provide cover from here. When Jarek blows the lines, you run for the hub door. Do not stop."

Elias nodded, his throat tight. He tapped the neural rig at his temple. "If I can get into the hub, I can override the primary repeater. But the door will be locked down."

"Then you break it," Jarek grunted. He slapped the charge against his palm. "Get ready."

Jarek slipped away, disappearing into the shadows of the maintenance level, moving toward the eastern flank. Elias took a slow breath, trying to steady his racing heart. The air smelled of ozone and hot metal. He watched the enforcers below, their armored forms stark against the floodlights. Every second stretched.

Then, the yard erupted.

A blinding flash of blue plasma tore through the eastern barricade, followed instantly by a concussive shockwave that rattled the teeth in Elias's skull. The heavy fuel lines ruptured, sending a geyser of superheated gas into the air. Warning sirens wailed at once, cutting through the sudden roar of the explosion.

The enforcers reacted exactly as Jarek had predicted. Shouts echoed across the yard, and the patrol squads pivoted toward the blast, their weapons raised.

"Now!" Kaelen barked, her rifle already tracking a straggler.

Elias launched himself from the shaft. He hit the ground running, his boots slamming against the ferrocrete. He kept his head down, weaving between stacked supply crates and the massive treads of the idling transports. The yard was a chaos of flashing lights and thick, acrid smoke from the ruptured lines.

He was halfway there when a shout cut through the din.

"Intruder! Central hub!"

Elias didn't look back. He poured every ounce of speed into his legs, his lungs burning. He reached the reinforced door of the hub and slammed his shoulder against the cold metal. It didn't budge. He punched a bypass sequence with hurried desperation into the keypad, but it flashed a harsh, angry red. *Access Denied. Lockdown Initiated.*

A heavy plasma bolt scorched the ferrocrete inches from his foot. Elias spun around. A massive heavy sentry, its armor plating thick and angular, was stomping toward him, its rotary cannon spinning up with a high-pitched whine.

Elias realized he could not pick the lock. There was no time. He slammed his hand against the console and jammed his rig's interface jack into the raw data port. Pain spiked behind his eyes as he brute-forced the local security protocols, dumping raw feedback directly into the hub's mainframe. It was a massive spike—a signal flare that Director Vane would undoubtedly detect.

He felt the heavy sentry lock onto him, the whine of its cannon reaching a fever pitch. The heavy blast door of the hub hissed and began to slide open.
