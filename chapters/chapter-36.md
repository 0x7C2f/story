# Chapter 36

## Outline

1. Elias and Kaelen flee down the transit tunnels away from the approaching loyalist sweep teams.
2. They use a service shaft to bypass a chokepoint but are nearly spotted.
3. They emerge in the maintenance sublevels near Sector Six and manage to link up with Jarek, who has secured a fallback position at the staging ground perimeter.

---

## Chapter Text

The sound of the loyalist boots was a rhythmic drumbeat against the concrete of the transit tunnels. Elias Thorne pushed himself faster, the damp air tearing at his lungs. Kaelen ran just ahead of him, her boots splashing through shallow pools of stagnant water.

"They're closing the gap," Kaelen said, not breaking stride. She glanced over her shoulder. "Sweep teams move fast. We can't stay on the main line."

Elias activated his wrist terminal. The map of the undercity flickered, struggling against the local interference. The Committee was flooding the local bands with signal noise, trying to blind the resistance.

"There's a service shaft up ahead," Elias said, pointing toward a faint structural anomaly on his display. "It runs parallel to the main line for a mile before dropping into the maintenance sublevels below Sector Six."

"Will it hold us?" Kaelen asked.

"It has to," Elias replied.

They reached the heavy grate covering the shaft entrance. Kaelen wedged the muzzle of her carbine into the gap and levered it upward. The metal shrieked in protest, a terrible sound that echoed down the tunnel. Elias added his weight, and together they forced the grate aside.

"In," Kaelen ordered.

Elias scrambled into the narrow, vertical shaft. The air inside was stale, thick with the smell of old rust and forgotten machinery. He found the access ladder in the dark and began to climb down. Kaelen followed a moment later, pulling the heavy grate back into place just as the sharp beams of tactical flashlights swept across the tunnel ceiling above them.

They froze, clinging to the rungs.

"Clear," a harsh voice echoed through the metal. "Push forward. They didn't come this way."

The boots receded, continuing down the main tunnel. Elias let out a breath he didn't realize he was holding. He continued his descent, hand over hand, until his boots hit a solid metal gantry.

"We're in the sublevels," Elias whispered, activating a low-lumen light on his terminal. The beam illuminated a vast, echoing chamber filled with massive water filtration tanks and tangled pipes. "Sector Six is directly above us."

Kaelen dropped lightly beside him. "Jarek said he was holding the staging ground perimeter. If we can navigate these sublevels, we might bypass the main cordons entirely."

"My signal is still jammed," Elias said, tapping his wrist. "I can't ping him."

"Then we find him the old-fashioned way," Kaelen said. "We move."

They navigated the labyrinth of the maintenance sublevels for what felt like hours. Every shadow seemed to stretch and twist, playing tricks on Elias's exhausted mind. His muscles ached, and a dull throb pulsed behind his eyes—a lingering echo of the massive data transfer he had initiated at the Spire.

Finally, they reached a heavy blast door marked with the faded insignia of the old transit authority. Kaelen pressed her ear against the cold metal, listening intently. She signaled Elias to step back, then keyed the manual release.

The door ground open, revealing a makeshift barricade of scrapped transports and concrete barriers. A massive figure rose from behind the cover, a heavy pulse rifle leveled at the doorway.

"Hold your fire," Kaelen said, stepping into the dim light.

Jarek lowered the weapon, a rare expression of relief crossing his hardened features. "You made it."

"Barely," Elias said, stepping through the blast door. The air here smelled of ozone and cordite. "The sweep teams are already blanketing Sector One."

"They're hitting the Outskirts, too," Jarek rumbled. "Vane is striking out blindly, trying to contain the panic. But the perimeter is holding. For now."

"We need a plan," Kaelen said, checking the charge on her carbine. "We can't just wait for them to find us."

Elias looked at the massive staging area, filled with the battered remnants of the Scavenger resistance. They had struck a blow against the Committee, a devastating strike that had exposed the truth to the entire city. But the war had just begun.

"We don't wait," Elias said, his voice steady despite his exhaustion. "We use this chaos. Vane is vulnerable. We strike again before she can reestablish control."
