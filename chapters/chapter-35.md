# Chapter 35

## Outline

1. Elias and Kaelen move through the chaotic streets of Sector One as the counter-broadcast disrupts the city.
2. They encounter a squad of confused Committee enforcers attempting to lock down a transit hub.
3. Using the chaos to their advantage, Elias and Kaelen breach the perimeter but realize Vane's loyalist sweep teams are already moving to cut off the routes to the Outskirts.

---

## Chapter Text

The central plaza of Sector One was dissolving into pandemonium. Sirens shrieked from automated security nodes, but their rigid electronic wails were drowned out by the rising clamor of the populace. On the monolithic displays above, the raw data stream of restored memories continued to overwrite the Committee's sterilized broadcasts.

Elias Thorne kept his head down, pushing through a knot of staggered citizens. Some were weeping, clutching at their temples; others were shouting, demanding answers from transit officials who looked just as bewildered. Beside him, Kaelen moved with practiced fluidity, her heavy carbine angled low but ready to swing up in a fraction of a second.

"They're going to shut down the transit hubs," Kaelen said, her voice cutting through the noise. "Standard lockdown protocol. Vane won't let this spread without trying to put a lid on the grid."

"The grid is already gone," Elias replied, adjusting the collar of his jacket to hide his face from the overhead optical scanners. "But you're right. We need to hit the secondary transit lines before the loyalists establish a hard perimeter."

They turned down an access alley, the shadows deep and cool compared to the harsh glare of the plaza. At the far end, a heavy security gate blocked the path to the subterranean rail lines. A squad of Committee enforcers stood before it. They weren't the polished elite of the Nexus Spire; these were local peacekeepers, and they looked rattled. Two of them were arguing over a fractured comms link, while the others nervously watched the approaching crowd.

"We don't have time to negotiate," Kaelen muttered, shifting her grip on her weapon.

"Wait," Elias said, grabbing her forearm. He tapped his wrist terminal, interfacing with the local mesh network. The signal repeaters Jarek had set up were still holding strong, amplifying Elias's access. "Their network is flooded with contradiction protocols. The local nodes don't know whether to follow Vane's old security keys or the new overrides the broadcast pushed."

He isolated the gate's control matrix and sent a focused pulse of raw archival data directly into the enforcers' tactical HUDs. It wasn't a sophisticated hack, just a blunt instrument of pure, unredacted history—a burst of the same traumatic memories currently playing across the city sky.

The enforcers staggered. One dropped his shock baton, clutching his helmet. The other stumbled backward, his voice cracking over the external speaker.

"Move," Elias said.

Kaelen surged forward, leading the way. They slipped past the disoriented guards and Kaelen slammed her palm against the manual override for the security gate. The heavy metal groaned and slid open just enough for them to squeeze through.

They descended into the gloom of the transit tunnels, the air thick with the smell of ozone and damp concrete. They were out of the immediate line of fire, but the escape was far from secured.

Elias checked his terminal, his breath coming in short, sharp bursts. "Jarek's signal is faint, but he's holding the rendezvous point at the staging ground."

Kaelen stopped suddenly, raising a hand. Her gaze was fixed on the darkness of the tunnel ahead. "He might be holding it, but we're not going to reach it easily."

The rhythmic, heavy thud of combat boots echoed down the corridor. It wasn't the disorganized shuffle of local peacekeepers. This was a synchronized march.

"Loyalist sweep teams," Kaelen whispered, raising her carbine. "Vane didn't wait. They're already here."
