# Chapter 1: The Fragment

## Outline

1. Elias Thorne discovers an altered memory file (M-774-B) during a routine archive dive.
2. He realizes the memory is hiding a classified facility and downloads the corrupted fragment.
3. Security protocols trigger, forcing Elias to flee the Nexus Archives.

---

## Chapter Text

Elias Thorne rubbed his temples, trying to massage away the persistent ache that always accompanied a deep dive. The archive node hummed beneath his fingertips, a sleek obsidian curve amidst the clutter of his dimly lit workspace. Screens flickered with lines of raw sensory data, the digital lifeblood of millions of citizens in New Providence. As a memory archivist, his job was routine: catalog, compress, and store. But today, the routine was broken.

The file designated M-774-B was flagged as a standard childhood recollection—a trip to the coastal seawall, circa 2042. But when Elias initiated the playback, the sensory feedback spiked into the red. It wasn't the crash of waves or the smell of synthetic brine that flooded his neural link; it was static, sharp and violent, followed by a fleeting, crystal-clear image of a corporate holding facility.

He leaned closer to the monitor, his breath catching in his throat. Memories were supposed to be immutable, protected by layers of cryptographic seals. Altering one without fracturing the subject's psyche was considered theoretically impossible. Yet, here it was. The timeline metadata showed no signs of tampering, but the raw code underneath the visual overlay was bleeding into neighboring sequences. Someone had clumsily spliced a fabricated childhood over a highly classified, traumatic event.

Elias’s fingers danced across the haptic keyboard, isolating the anomaly. "Computer, run a cross-reference on the visual signature of the holding facility," he muttered, his voice dropping to a whisper in the quiet room.

The system chimed. *Access Denied. Clearance Level 5 Required.*

A cold knot formed in his stomach. The Nexus Archives didn't just store the past; they curated it. And if someone was curating history at this level, it meant the very foundation of societal truth was compromised. He glanced over his shoulder, half expecting the shadows of his cramped office to detach and form the shape of a corporate enforcer.

Elias knew the protocol. He was supposed to flag the file as corrupted, delete the local cache, and submit a report to the Oversight Committee. But curiosity, a dangerous trait for an archivist, rooted him to his seat. He downloaded the corrupted fragment onto an untraceable physical drive—a relic from a bygone era he kept hidden for emergencies.

As the transfer completed, the lights in his office flickered, and the archive node emitted a low, warning hum. A notification flashed on his main screen: *Unauthorized Data Extraction Detected. Security Protocol Initiated.*

He had less than two minutes before the Archive sentinels breached his sector. Grabbing his coat and the drive, Elias plunged into the labyrinthine corridors of the Archives, leaving behind the safety of his routine and stepping into the shadows of a rewritten past.
