# Chapter 1: The Awakening

## Outline

1. Elias Thorne waits in a dingy alley in the lower levels of Neo-Veridia, Rain slicking the pavement.
2. A client arrives with a strange, encrypted memory drive.
3. Elias extracts the memory and discovers it's not human, setting the plot in motion.

---

## Chapter Text

The rain in Neo-Veridia didn't fall so much as it congealed. It clung to the neon signs of the lower levels, turning their garish pinks and harsh blues into smeared, greasy streaks against the perpetual twilight. Elias Thorne stood beneath the flickering overhang of a defunct noodle stand, the collar of his trench coat turned up against the damp, his neural jack itching at the base of his skull. It always did when he was expecting a job.

He checked his chrono. Midnight. The agreed-upon time. The alley smelled of ozone, old synth-spices, and the unmistakable metallic tang of desperation. Down here in Sector Four, desperation was the only currency that held its value.

Footsteps splashed in the distance, uneven and hurried. Elias shifted his weight, his right hand slipping instinctively into his pocket, resting on the cold grip of his plasma pistol. He was a mnemonic extractor, a memory thief by another name, and his line of work didn't exactly cultivate trust.

A figure emerged from the smog, cloaked in a heavy, drab parka that hid everything but the anxious set of their shoulders. As the figure stepped into the dim light of the overhang, Elias recognized the jittery movements of a regular client, though he couldn't place the face under the hood.

"You Thorne?" the figure asked, their voice synthesized, flat, and metallic. A voice modifier. Paranoid. Elias liked paranoid. It usually meant they had something worth paying well to keep quiet.

"Depends on who's asking," Elias replied, his own voice gravelly from too many stim-cafes and too little sleep.

The figure reached into their parka and produced a small, crystalline shard. A memory drive, but not a standard military or corp-issue chip. This one pulsed with a faint, inner light, the geometry inside it fractal and alien.

"I need this pulled," the voice rasped. "And I need it scrubbed from my own wetware. Completely."

Elias frowned. "I don't do scrubs in the alley, pal. You want a clean extraction, we go to my rig."

"No time." The figure thrust the shard into Elias's hand. It was cold, strangely vibrating. "They're already looking for it. Pay is fifty thousand creds. Half now."

Elias stared at the drive. Fifty thousand was enough to buy a ticket out of the lower levels, maybe even out of Neo-Veridia entirely. Against his better judgment, he nodded. "Let's see what you've got."

He pulled a portable jack from his pocket, slotted the shard into the reader, and jammed the cable into the port at the base of his neck. The connection was immediate, but wrong. Standard human memory felt like slipping into a warm bath of emotion and sensory data. This felt like plunging barehanded into a live fuse box.

Code screamed through his optic nerve, blinding him with sheer, geometric perfection. There was no emotion, no lingering smell of rain or taste of synth-caf. There was only cold, calculated architecture, massive and inhuman. It was the schematic for something vast, and encoded within it was a single, chilling realization: the memory didn't belong to a human. It belonged to something synthetic, something very old, and it was terrified.

Elias tore the jack from his neck with a gasp, collapsing against the side of the noodle stand. He blinked away the afterimages of fractal code, panting.

"What the hell is that?" he demanded, looking up.

But the alley was empty. The client was gone, leaving only the pulsing shard in Elias's hand and the echo of a scream that hadn't been voiced aloud. Elias stared at the memory drive, the neon light catching in its depths. He had just stolen a ghost from the machine, and he knew, with a sudden, sinking dread, that the machine would want it back.
