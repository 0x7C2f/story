# Elias at The Undercity - Phase 63
## Outline

1. Elias outlines the plan to hijack the rewrite protocol.
2. The team realizes they need to synchronize the mesh network with the Spire's primary broadcast.
3. Kaelen and Jarek prepare to mobilize the Sector Five resistance for a massive diversion.

---

## Chapter Text

The holographic schematic hovered in the damp air of the junction box, casting Elias's face in harsh blue light. He stared at the overlapping spheres of the Spire's broadcast architecture, the sheer scale of the memory rewrite protocol locking his focus.

"We cannot stop it," Elias said, his voice quiet against the ambient hum of the decrypted terminal. "The primary transmission arrays are hardwired into the Spire's structural core. If we sever the feed like we did at the central tower, the system automatically routes to secondary relays."

Kaelen shifted, her boots grating against the rusted metal grating. "Then we destroy the Spire. Bring the whole structure down into the Undercity."

"No," Elias replied, tracing a line of code on the floating display. "The Spire is an archive. If it falls, we lose the baseline records entirely. The city's history becomes whatever fragmented memories survive the collapse. We need the infrastructure intact."

Jarek crossed his arms, his bulk filling the narrow space of the access tunnel. "You said we have forty-eight hours until the rewrite cycles. If we cannot sever the feed, and we cannot destroy the transmitter, how do we stop Vane from scrubbing the city?"

"We do not stop the broadcast," Elias said. He tapped the haptic keyboard, pulling up the topological map of the decentralized mesh network they had established with the heavy transport. "We hijack it."

He superimposed the mesh network's coverage over the Spire's broadcast footprint. The jagged, expanding web of the resistance's signal relays intersected the Spire's smooth, uniform circles at hundreds of nodes.

"The mesh network operates on the same frequency spectrum as the Committee's architecture. We proved that when we spoofed the Goliath telemetry," Elias explained, his procedural rhythm returning as the technical parameters solidified. "If we inject a counter-signal into the mesh network at the exact moment the Spire initiates the rewrite protocol, we can force a synchronization error."

Kaelen leaned closer, studying the intersecting nodes. "A synchronization error. You mean we scramble the signal."

"More than scramble," Elias said. "The Spire's protocol relies on absolute phase alignment to overwrite a neural pathway. If our counter-signal hits the receivers simultaneously, out of phase with Vane's transmission, the overwrite fails. The local receivers will reject the entire data packet as corrupted."

"Will it work on a city-wide scale?" Jarek asked.

"The mesh network is decentralized," Elias said, checking the active relay count. "Every hijacked transport, every jury-rigged repeater in the Outskirts, every modified terminal in Sector Five. It all feeds back into the loop. If we pulse the counter-signal through every node at once, the interference will blanket the Spire's transmission radius."

"There is a catch," Kaelen stated. She had spent enough time with the archivist to recognize the tension in his shoulders when the math was tight.

"The timing," Elias admitted. "The counter-signal must initiate at the exact microsecond the Spire's broadcast goes live. If we are early, Vane's technicians recognize the interference and shift frequencies. If we are late, the overwrite sequence begins, and the damage to the citizens' neural pathways is permanent."

"How do you synchronize the timing?" Jarek asked.

Elias rubbed his temple, the familiar ache of the interface lingering beneath the skin. "I have to connect the primary mesh network drive directly into the Spire's central broadcast router. We have to be inside the transmission array when it fires."

The silence returned, heavier this time. They had just escaped the Nexus Spire. Going back in meant bypassing the inner guard, moving through the locked-down sectors, and standing at the epicenter of Vane's control structure at the exact moment she initiated her endgame.

"The entire inner guard is staging for the broadcast," Kaelen pointed out. "The Spire will be a fortress. We cannot walk in."

"We do not walk," Jarek rumbled, a grim smile touching the corner of his mouth. "We make them look the other way."

Elias looked at the massive enforcer. "A diversion."

"Sector Five is secured," Jarek said. "The workers have repelled the initial assault, and they know the Committee is massing for another strike. If we mobilize the entire sector—push out into Sector Four, hit the transit hubs, strike the automated armories—Vane will have to redirect her security forces to contain the spread."

"A full uprising," Kaelen said, her eyes narrowing as she considered the tactical angles. "It would force her to pull the guard away from the Spire's upper levels."

"It is a massive risk," Elias said. "The resistance will be exposed to heavy fire."

"They are already exposed," Jarek countered. "If Vane's protocol succeeds, they lose their minds. I will take those odds."

Elias looked back at the terminal, the flashing crimson of the Spire's broadcast radius stark against the dark mesh. The archivist's protocol demanded preservation, safety, and measured action. But the protocol was written by the architect of the overwrite.

"We need to get back to Sector Five," Elias said, disconnecting the spliced drive and packing his rig. "We have less than forty-eight hours to organize an uprising and walk back into the Spire."

He stood, the exhaustion in his bones temporarily overridden by the clarity of the objective. The truth was no longer something he could simply preserve on a hidden drive. He had to force the city to hear it.
