# Elias at Sector Four - Phase 70
## Outline

1. Elias, Kaelen, and Jarek cross the unstable fracture zone chasm in Sector Four to reach the abandoned relay substation.
2. The crossing is physically demanding and treacherous, testing Elias's exhaustion and the group's trust.
3. They successfully reach the substation, discovering that it still has latent power and can be used to tap into the mesh network.

---

## Chapter Text

The fracture zone was less a bridge and more a graveyard of architecture. Massive ferrocrete slabs hung suspended by tangles of industrial rebar, swaying in the constant, frigid updraft from the unseen depths below. The pale emergency lighting on the far side painted the broken edges in sickly green hues, emphasizing the yawning abyss that waited for a single misstep.

Standing at the edge, Elias rested his boots on the scarred lip of the solid corridor. His lungs still burned from the sprint while his temples throbbed with the familiar ache of a recent, deep neural dive. Without his deck, his hands felt light—too light, as if a vital appendage had been severed. Swallowing hard, he listened to the dry click in the oppressive silence.

"We move one at a time," Jarek rumbled, stepping past Elias to examine the nearest slab. The scavenger's massive frame seemed wholly unsuited for the delicate traversal, yet he moved with a fluid, surprising grace. "Test your weight before you shift your center. If a piece drops, you do not drop with it."

"Comforting," Kaelen muttered. She slung her submachine gun tighter across her back, securing it against her armor. She looked back at Elias, her sharp features softened only a fraction by the gloom. "Keep your eyes on the next anchor point, Archivist. Don't look down. It is basic survival geometry."

When Jarek went first, he extended a heavy boot onto a jagged protrusion of a downed support strut. The metal groaned with a low, metallic shriek that echoed off the unseen walls, but it held. Shifting his weight, he moved steadily across the first gap while his thick fingers found purchase on a rusted cable.

Watching from the ledge, Elias felt his pulse hammering against his ribs as every scrape of Jarek's boots sent a jolt of anxiety through him. Checking his wrist terminal was an instinctive, useless gesture, considering it was now isolated from any network. *Basic survival geometry,* he repeated to himself. He wasn't a soldier like Kaelen, nor a survivor of the wastes like Jarek. He was a cataloger of the past, suddenly forced to cross the crumbling present.

"Your turn," Kaelen prompted, gesturing toward the improvised path. "I will cover the rear."

Elias nodded tersely. His breath hitched when the metal shifted beneath his boots, forcing him to dig his fingers into the cold, damp concrete of a neighboring slab and freeze in place.

Jarek's voice drifted back from the darkness ahead. "Keep moving. Momentum is your friend."

He pushed his legs into a slow progression, carefully testing his weight with each shift forward while the air grew colder and carried the scent of ancient dust. A chunk of plaster dislodged to his left and plummeted into the dark, but the expected impact never came, leaving absolute silence below.

He reached the midpoint, a large, mostly stable section of what used to be a transit platform. He paused, gasping for air, the muscles in his thighs shaking uncontrollably. The exhaustion was bone-deep, a cumulative toll of the past few days.

"Don't stop," Kaelen commanded, dropping onto the platform beside him without a single sign of being winded. "We are exposed."

Pressing a hand against his forehead, Elias whispered, "I just need a second. I'm offline, Kaelen. If Vane accelerates the rewrite—"

"Then she accelerates it," Kaelen cut him off, her voice clipped, efficient. "Worry about the code when we have a terminal. Right now, worry about the next slab."

Pointing ahead, she highlighted Jarek nearing the far side and pulling himself up onto the solid floor near the substation's entrance. The final stretch was the worst—a narrow, twisted beam that looked as though it could snap under minimal pressure.

By closing his eyes for a fraction of a second, Elias centered himself. Opening them to fix his gaze on Jarek's silhouette, he stepped onto the beam, which bowed at once with a protesting, sharp crack. He dropped low to lower his center of gravity, then half-crawled and half-slid across the metal while rust and jagged edges scraped his raw hands.

Jarek grabbed the collar of Elias's coat with a final, desperate heave, hauling him over the ledge. Collapsing onto the solid ferrocrete, Elias felt his chest heaving as his heart attempted to batter its way out of his ribcage.

When Kaelen landed beside them a moment later, the beam behind her shuddered with the strain before finally giving way. It plummeted into the abyss, taking a section of the improvised bridge with it.

"We are not going back that way," Jarek noted with a flat tone.

Dragging himself up, Elias brushed the dirt from his archivist uniform—a garment now all but unrecognizable under layers of grime—and turned his attention to the structure before them.

The relay substation was a squat, brutalist block of dark metal and reinforced glass, untouched for perhaps decades. But as Elias approached the heavy blast door, he noticed the faint, rhythmic hum beneath his boots. He placed a hand against the cold metal panel beside the entrance.

"It has power," Elias breathed, the exhaustion eclipsed for a moment by a surge of pure, procedural focus. "Latent grid draw, but it's enough. If I can bypass the primary lock, I can patch directly into the foundational layer."

Kneeling beside the access panel, his fingers went to work on the rusted latches. They had crossed the void. Now, it was time to build a bridge back to the city.
