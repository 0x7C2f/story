# Chapter 23

The interior of the captured crawler was a cramped, claustrophobic shell of polished ferrocrete and heavy mesh. The air smelled of ozone, sweat, and the residual tang of the electromagnetic pulse. The transport's auxiliary lights cast a cold, flickering glow over the cargo bay, leaving the corners in shadow.

Elias knelt before the primary communications terminal, his neural rig humming softly as it translated the raw data stream from the Committee's operational network. He ignored the aching throb at his temple. "The local lockout is bypassed," he muttered, his fingers hovering over the haptic keyboard. "But the encryption on the main command tier is active. I have to splice the handshake."

"How long?" Jarek asked. The Scavenger leader stood by the heavy mag-locked door, his slug-thrower resting against his hip. He watched the span of the old transit bridge through the narrow viewing slit, checking the perimeter.

"I need three minutes," Elias replied, his voice clipped, procedural. "The encryption is a rolling cipher. If I force it, the network will flag the anomaly and sever the connection."

Kaelen leaned against a stack of munitions crates, her eyes locked on the terminal screen. "Vane will know we have the transport. The drone telemetry went dark the moment the EMP hit. She will send a recovery team."

"Let her," Jarek said, his tone flat. "We have heavy ordnance now. The bridge is a chokepoint."

Elias exhaled, a sharp, ragged sound. "Computer, run a heuristic decryption pattern against the incoming cipher. Match it to the root key signature."

The screen blurred, lines of code cascading downward in a chaotic torrent. Elias stared into the matrix, searching for the anomaly. He found the seams in the cipher, the minute gaps where the code hesitated. He pushed the root key through those gaps, forcing the encryption to acknowledge the ghost handshake.

The screen flashed green.

"I am in," Elias said, the words barely a whisper.

The terminal reorganized the data into a clean, legible interface. Maps of the Outskirts resolved on the screen, overlaid with shifting tactical markers. Elias tapped a key, isolating the primary command directive.

"They aren't just sending sweep teams," Elias said, the chill settling into his bones. He leaned closer to the projection. "Look at the deployment pattern. The heavy transports aren't scouting. They are anchoring a mobile network."

Kaelen stepped forward, her gaze narrowing. "A mobile network? For what?"

"A localized rewrite protocol," Elias answered. He pulled up the technical specifications attached to the directive. "Vane is circumventing the central broadcast tower. She is deploying signal repeaters on these transports. They are going to carpet-bomb the Outskirts with a concentrated memory wipe."

Jarek stepped away from the door, the heavy bulk of his frame tense. "Can they do that?"

"They are doing it," Elias said. "The radius is small, but the intensity is absolute. If they establish a perimeter around the Scrapyard Settlement and activate the repeaters, the entire population will be reset to a baseline compliance state. The settlement will forget the resistance exists."

The silence in the crawler was heavy, broken only by the hum of Elias's rig.

"When does the deployment reach the perimeter?" Kaelen asked.

Elias checked the timeline. "Twelve hours. The staging ground is Sector Six, an old distribution hub on the edge of the Dead Zone. They are marshaling the transports there before they fan out."

"We can't defend the settlement against a concentrated broadcast," Kaelen said. "We don't have the shielding."

"Then we don't defend," Jarek said, his voice hard. He looked at Elias, then at Kaelen. "We hit the staging ground. We take out the repeaters before they deploy."

Elias rubbed his temple. The interface ache flared, a sharp spike of pain that made him wince. He looked at the map, at the red markers clustering in Sector Six. The archivist in him saw the sheer statistical improbability of an assault on a fortified staging ground.

But the archivist was dead. The man who remained saw only the necessity.

"We need the exact coordinates of the primary signal repeater," Elias said, his hands moving back to the keyboard. "If we destroy the central node at the hub, the secondary transports won't be able to synchronize the broadcast."

He isolated the command node in the Sector Six schematics, a small, heavily fortified structure at the center of the distribution yard.

"Then that is the target," Kaelen said. "We use this crawler. They will think it's a retreating unit until we are inside the perimeter."

Elias looked at the screen, the data streaming continuously across the terminal. They had the transport. They had the map. And they had the target.

"Then we better start planning," Elias said.
