# Elias Thorne

## Appearance

On the page he is read through his kit and his coat rather than his features. He
wears an archivist uniform under a heavy coat — the guard at the Scrapyard
Settlement still notices "the pristine cut" of it under the grime (ch. 14) — and
he works a portable neural rig: tether cable, temple-worn primary node, haptic
keyboard, projector array, and a wrist terminal he checks when he is nervous. The
cramped, dim clutter of his archive workspace and the Undercity's damp both sit
badly on him; he is a man built for filtered air.

Physical state moves across the book and should be tracked, not stated fresh each
time: temple ache after every deep dive (ch. 1, 4, 6), nosebleed and shaking hands
after Vane's vault (ch. 8-9), bone-deep exhaustion through the Outskirts walk
(ch. 13-14), shivering in the lower hab block on his first morning in the
settlement (ch. 16).

**Not yet on the page:** age, height, build, coloring, hair, and any marking. No
chapter has described his face. Establish it when a scene earns it — do not guess.

## Personality & Traits

Procedural to the bone. He isolates an anomaly before he reacts to it, narrates
his own method under his breath, and talks to terminals as though they were
colleagues ("Computer, run a cross-reference on the visual signature of the
holding facility"). Curiosity is the trait he names as dangerous in his own trade,
and it is the one that moves the plot: the protocol said flag, delete, report, and
he downloaded the fragment instead.

He is observant enough to be paranoid — he glances over his shoulder in an empty
office, expects shadows to detach and take the shape of an enforcer — but the
paranoia never freezes him. Shock converts into resolve within a scene, and he
states the next action rather than the feeling: "Then we better start planning."
He treats memory with an archivist's reverence, which is why the vault dive reads
as sacrilege to him and why he is the one who notices the cost afterward.

Habits to keep consistent: rubbing his temples where the interface ache sits;
whispering when a room is quiet; checking the wrist terminal under stress;
reaching for the rig or the drive as a hand reaches for a rail.

## Backstory

Only what the draft has established. He is a memory archivist at the Nexus
Archives in the upper tiers of New Providence: catalog, compress, store. His
clearance sits below Level 5 — the cross-reference on the holding facility comes
back *Access Denied. Clearance Level 5 Required.* — and his office is cramped,
cluttered, and dimly lit.

Two details quietly contradict the job description, and both are load-bearing. He
keeps an untraceable physical drive, "a relic from a bygone era," hidden for
emergencies. Months before chapter 1 he hid a jury-rigged terminal in an
abandoned hydro-maintenance hub in the Undercity as a fail-safe "for his more
sensitive, off-the-books archivist work." He was already working outside the
record before the record broke.

**Not yet on the page:** family, where he came from, why he chose archiving, what
the Archives did with him once he was flagged, and whether anyone is looking for
him from his old life.

## Motivations & Goals

**External want:** prove that memory M-774-B was altered, then stop the overwrite
— first by exposing the batch protocol, then by carrying the root key to the
central broadcast tower and pushing the unaltered files to the city himself.

**Internal need:** to stop being a custodian who files what he is handed. The
protocol he is supposed to follow is the same protocol that would have buried
M-774-B, and every turn of the plot is a refusal to file it.

**The conflict between them:** the need costs him the life the want is meant to
protect. By chapter 11 he has traded employment, citizenship, and a city he can
walk for the truth, and by chapter 13 he accepts that the trade is permanent. He
never argues with the cost; he argues with the odds.

## Location References

His working geography runs top to bottom through the city: the Nexus Archives
(his post), the Undercity (refuge and rig), the Sector Four black site, the Nexus
Spire (Vane's vault), the central broadcast tower, and after chapter 13 the
Outskirts and the Scrapyard Settlement. The descent is the shape of his arc —
every chapter takes him further from filtered air.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Technical, clipped, and quieter than the room. He reaches for procedural
vocabulary under pressure — protocol, parameters, handshake, air-gap, splice —
and drops into single-word assessments when he is diagnosing machinery or people.
The muttered commands he gives his own terminals ("Computer, run a cross-reference
on the visual signature of the holding facility") are the same register turned
outward. System messages carry his interior weather in italics; he whispers; he
answers himself in the second person when he is alone with a screen.

> "Computer, run a cross-reference on the visual signature of the holding facility."

> "It's an assembly line. They aren't targeting dissidents or threats. They're rewriting collective memory. Changing the baseline reality."

> "The matrix isn't fried. It's out of sync. The power core is generating energy faster than the aging distribution grid can handle."

> "We have time. We have the rest of our lives. And this time, we get to remember it all."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** A routine-bound archivist who knows the protocol, trusts the
  immutability of the record, and treats his own off-the-books work as a private
  exception rather than a habit.
- **Key turning points:**
  - ch. 1 — downloads the corrupted fragment instead of flagging it; the first
    deliberate breach.
  - ch. 3-4 — hears the scope from Kaelen and converts shock into a plan.
  - ch. 5-6 — takes the field, loses thirty percent of the manifest, and chooses
    to go back in rather than leak an unprovable half-truth.
  - ch. 7-8 — accepts a dive that can fry his cortex, and sees Project:
    Synchronization while he is inside.
  - ch. 10-11 — commits to the tower and the counter-broadcast: the moment he
    stops defending the record and starts authoring one.
  - ch. 13-16 — accepts exile, then converts skill into standing at the Scrapyard
    Settlement. "The fear that had driven him since he first discovered the
    corrupted file had burned away."
- **Ending state (as drafted):** Resident technician of the Scrapyard Settlement,
  allied with the Scavengers, wanted by a Committee that no longer has a working
  command structure. He is no longer preserving memory; he is the reason it
  survived.
