# Kaelen

> **Pronoun decision (canonical):** she/her. Chapters 10-16 use she/her
> consistently, and chapters 3-9 were revised to match in this run. Chapters 3-4
> had used they/their and chapters 5-9 he/him. A later run must not reintroduce
> the older forms.

## Appearance

Built for the Undercity. She wears layers of dull, protective fabric that read as
shadow in Sector Four, with the slight metallic ridges of neural implants visible
along her neck and jawline. One arm is cybernetic — it strains with hydraulic
whines when she forces a lift door and whirs through a diagnostic when she sits
down. Her boots make almost no sound on grating; she drops from rafters and moves
ahead of Elias without being asked twice.

Her kit is her signature more than her body: a frayed splicing rig in a satchel, a
pulse pistol, a wrist monitor, a comms channel she talks through in a low scrape,
and a reinforced console of her own in the sanctuary she keeps hidden behind a
bulkhead in a defunct reclamation plant. In the Outskirts she wears her sidearm
openly at her hip, because out there a hidden weapon reads as a liar's weapon.

**Not yet on the page:** height, build, age, coloring, hair, and what the
cybernetic arm replaced. Establish when a scene earns it.

## Personality & Traits

Suspicious first, useful second, loyal last and only once it has been paid for.
She opens chapter 3 by telling Elias he is broadcasting his anxiety and closes it
by treating the drive as though it might detonate; nothing in between is
performance. She taps her fingers against her thigh in rapid succession when she
is thinking, checks her surroundings on a loop, and reads a room by its exits.

Her cynicism is a working tool rather than a mood — "The Oversight Committee has
turned history into a software patch" is how she keeps a terrifying fact
handleable — but it drops away exactly twice on the page: when she sees the batch
protocol's architecture ("This isn't an isolated incident... We're looking at a
factory") and when she agrees to the tower run ("They won't... We didn't come this
far to let them rewrite history"). Save those cracks; they are the character.

Under pressure she is the one who gives the order: *Move, Thorne. Pull it. Keep
climbing.* She is never the one who hesitates. She is deeply paranoid, and she
carries a strong, cynical sense of justice underneath it.

## Backstory

Formerly a junior analyst for a minor data conglomerate, ousted after uncovering
widespread algorithmic manipulation in public records. Disillusioned with the
system, she retreated to the Undercity and built a living out of raw data, forged
credentials, forgotten histories, and contacts — scavengers, rogue data-brokers,
and a smuggler crew in the lower sectors who can source localized EMP charges.

She keeps more than one refuge: the hidden sanctuary behind the reclamation
plant's bulkhead, a shielded maintenance junction safehouse, and a reinforced
shipping container wedged beneath a collapsed monorail line. Each one sits in a
dead zone the corporate monitoring grid cannot penetrate, which tells you how she
thinks about trust.

**Not yet on the page:** what she did between the conglomerate and the Undercity,
who taught her to crack Committee architecture, and whether anyone from her old
employer is still looking for her.

## Motivations & Goals

**External want:** tear down the corporate monopoly on truth — first by proving
the batch protocol exists, then by getting the root key onto the broadcast
network and pushing the unaltered files to the whole city.

**Internal need:** to let herself depend on someone again. She has organized a
network precisely so that no single failure can take her down, and every step of
the plot asks her to put weight on another person instead.

**Fear:** being discovered by the enforcers and having her own memories erased or
rewritten. She names the risk to Elias before either of them goes near a node.

**The conflict between them:** the alliance is the vulnerability. By chapter 16
she is planning a settlement's defenses — work that only matters if the
settlement survives, which means it only matters if she stays.

## Location References

Undercity native by choice: her sanctuary and safehouses sit in the dead zones
where the monitoring grid cannot reach. Her route through the book runs from the
reclamation plant and the maintenance junction, down to the Sector Four black
site, up through the Nexus Spire and the central broadcast tower, and out to the
Outskirts and the Scrapyard Settlement.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Fast, clipped, cynical; efficiency over politeness. She addresses Elias by
surname, narrates her own cracking in real time, and reaches for the Undercity's
vocabulary — heat, ghosts, rig, old school, soft-skins. She states odds as facts
and facts as orders.

> "You're broadcasting your anxiety, Thorne."

> "Old school. That's a severe breach of protocol for an archivist."

> "It's efficient. If you want to control the narrative, you don't argue with the past. You just delete it and install an update."

> "It's a suicide run, Elias. But it's the only play we have left."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** A contact doing a transaction — suspicious of Elias, weary
  of the whole city, and certain that nothing she touches survives contact with
  the Committee.
- **Key turning points:**
  - ch. 3 — takes the drive seriously enough to crack it herself; the first
    unforced investment.
  - ch. 4-5 — commits to the black-site run and covers the extraction when the
    sweep starts.
  - ch. 8 — holds the perimeter in Vane's vault with a pistol and pulls Elias off
    the floor when the dive nearly kills him.
  - ch. 9 — finishes Elias's sentence about broadcasting the truth, which is the
    moment the job becomes hers too.
  - ch. 10-11 — rigs the Sector Three diversion and takes the blast doors, i.e.
    volunteers for the exposed half of the plan.
  - ch. 13-16 — walks into exile with him, negotiates sanctuary, and then does
    the defensive work that keeps the settlement standing.
- **Ending state (as drafted):** Resident and defense planner of the Scrapyard
  Settlement, allied with the Scavengers, still watching the perimeter — but now
  watching it for people who are watching hers.
