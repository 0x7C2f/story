# Jarek

## Appearance

Rugged and weather-beaten, carrying the scars of a decade in the Outskirts. Heavy,
mismatched armor — a patchwork of discarded city enforcer gear and scavenged metal
plating — over a frame built for work rather than war. Calculating, unforgiving
eyes. Calloused hands stained with rust and machine grease; the smell of both
clings to the armor. He stands where he can look down on people: on a raised
catwalk above the main generator, on a rusted railing above the command deck on a
gutted transit hauler.

## Personality & Traits

Pragmatic to the point of bluntness, and suspicious of outsiders in proportion to
what they might cost him — city dwellers most of all, because he was one once. He
prices everything in what it does: words are "a luxury for those who don't have to
worry about where their next meal is coming from," and memory wars "mean nothing to
the dust." He is fiercely protective of the settlement and measures people by
whether they pull their weight.

He is not cruel and he is not sentimental. He grants a damp corner and a morning
test rather than a welcome, he watches in unreadable silence while the test runs,
and he gives credit exactly once, in a curt nod, when it is earned. His suspicion
is a tool he sets down deliberately — the "glint of genuine interest" when Elias
diagnoses the generator is the most expressive thing he does on the page.

## Backstory

Once a low-level maintenance worker in New Providence, exiled to the Outskirts
years ago after discovering too much about the city's resource allocation
inequalities. He survived by banding together with other exiles and became a
respected leader of the Scrapyard Settlement — a position he holds by being the
person who decides what the settlement can afford.

**Not yet on the page:** how he was exiled, who he left behind in the city, how
long he has led the Scavengers, and what the settlement's other leaders think of
him.

## Motivations & Goals

**External want:** the survival and prosperity of the Scrapyard Settlement —
resources, working generators, water recyclers, and perimeter defenses that hold
when the city comes looking.

**Internal need:** to be able to trust an offer again. He was thrown away by the
last system that asked him to believe in it.

**The conflict between them:** sheltering two wanted fugitives is the most
expensive thing he can do for the settlement, and the only thing on offer is a
promise. He resolves it the only way he knows how — by making the promise prove
itself on the generator deck.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

A low, gravel rumble that the wind takes most of. Short declaratives, transactional
framing, and the vocabulary of survival: scrap, water, dust, weight, cold. He asks
what someone can do before he asks who they are, and he never uses a warm form of
address — the closest he comes is "city soft-skins."

His one verbal habit worth protecting: he repeats a supplicant's own word back at
them as a test. Kaelen asks for sanctuary and he answers "Sanctuary?" — the echo is
the price of the question, not agreement with it.

> "Words alone don't keep the cold out. But concrete actions might just buy you some time."

> "Memory. That's a luxury for those who don't have to worry about where their next meal is coming from. Out here, we deal in scrap, water, and survival."

> "This isn't a charity. Everyone here pulls their weight, or they get thrown out. What can you offer the Scrapyard?"

> "You claimed you have technical skills. Prove it. Fix it, or you walk."

> "The city throws away its problems. We make them useful."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Gatekeeper. Two soft-skins at the airlock, a claim he has no
  reason to believe, and a settlement that cannot afford to be wrong about them.
- **Key turning points:**
  - ch. 14 — the gate guard reports the arrivals; he lets them in far enough to be
    assessed rather than turning them back into the dust.
  - ch. 15 — hears their claim, refuses the ideology, and converts it into a test:
    one night, one morning, proof of usefulness.
  - ch. 16 — watches the generator stabilize and the defense overhaul land, and
    gives the settlement's alliance in a single curt nod.
- **Ending state (as drafted):** Allied. The Scrapyard Settlement is their base and
  the Scavengers are their people — the first institution in the book that takes
  them in on the basis of what they can do rather than what they are allowed to
  know.
